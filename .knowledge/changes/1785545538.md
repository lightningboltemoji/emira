# Park slots clear the chrome the window insists on keeping

> Change `1785545538` — 2026-07-31

## Goal

A parked window that macOS would not put where the slot said was re-parked on **every** placement pass,
for the life of the window. One 20-minute session's trace shows `park(2, 1799,1129 1452x1102)` issued 27
times to one Safari window, three of them inside 40 ms — every `dragEnded`, every focus change, every
arrival, each costing a `setSize`/`setPosition` pair, a read-back and the corrective pass that `place(at:)`
runs when the read-back disagrees, all on that app's lane.

The clamp is per app and easy to measure. On an 1800×1169 working area, a row-0 slot asks for `y = 1129`
(40 pt of chrome). That Safari window lands at `1117` — it will not keep less than its 52 pt toolbar on
screen — while Ghostty lands on `1129` exactly and stays quiet. Asked for `1121` it lands at `1117` again;
asked for `1113` it lands at `1113`, so the ask being *achievable* is the whole of the problem.

Two things followed from the drift. `Engine.isAlreadyPlaced` is exact-rect equality, so a clamped window
was never where the layout said and the placement diff re-emitted its park forever. And the clamp is to a
fixed value, so rows 0 and 1 — 40 and 48 pt of chrome — **both** land on 52: two same-size windows of one
app parked at consecutive ordinals get byte-identical frames, which `WindowIdentity.bind` calls ambiguous
and refuses both of, costing them their management at the next rebind. The stagger exists to prevent
exactly that, and the clamp was quietly defeating it.

## Implementation

The chrome a window keeps is learned, and slots are allocated to clear it. `AXExecutor` reports a drifted
park as `parkCorrected` — a sibling of `placementCorrected` carrying its request, rather than the
`windowFrameChanged` it used to be sent as — and the reducer reads one scalar out of it into
`World.parkFloors`, which rides into `LayoutMetrics` beside `corrections`.

**A floor, not an answer to a slot.** Keyed on the question, as `SizeCorrection` is, a park answer would be
re-learned every time the ordinal run renumbers — which is every column that opens or closes, so the
learning would never converge. Chrome is a fact about the window (its title bar, its toolbar) and holds
wherever it is parked, so it is asked once. The size half of the same answer is still discarded, and for
the reason it always was: a window refuses a resize at a sliver that it accepts back in view, and recording
that would freeze the column at its parked width. "A park teaches nothing" was an argument about size that
had quietly annexed position, where it does not hold — the refusal is not an artifact of being parked, it
*is* the park.

**Up the lattice, and through the cursor.** `ParkingLot.ordinal(atLeast:clearing:)` rounds a floor up to the
next staggered row rather than honouring it exactly — 52 becomes row 2's 56 — so every slot stays a lattice
point and distinctness remains a property of the lattice rather than something each app's answer has to
re-argue. The floor moves the run's **cursor**, not just its own window, which is what fixes the collision:
two windows refusing 40 pt take rows 2 and 3, not one frame twice. Ordinals only move forward, and a floor
past the last row of a lane takes the tallest nub there is, so an app that refuses to park at all cannot
walk the whole run off the working area.

That makes the cursor an output as well as an input: a strip can consume more ordinals than it has windows,
so `Workspaces.targetFrames` threads one `inout` cursor through the strips instead of counting each strip's
windows from the outside. `Layout.parkedWindowIds` existed only to supply that count and is gone.

Convergence costs one write. The clamped park is corrected, the floor is recorded, and the same pass
re-parks at a slot the app accepts — after which the placement diff is silent, which is the property the
new engine test asserts. A repeat of a floor already recorded re-places nothing, so an app that lands
where it likes cannot trade writes with us.

Rejected: **weakening the park predicate** (accept any window parked at-or-above its target) — cheapest,
no new state, but it discards "each parked window is at its assigned slot", so a window whose ordinal moves
down to a shorter nub reads as already-placed and never re-spreads. **Raising `visibleChrome` globally** —
a guess that costs every app a taller nub for a few apps' opinions, and 52 today is not 80 tomorrow.
**De-duplicating writes in `AXExecutor`** — the wrong plane: the core would go on believing something
false, and the shell would need a shadow copy of the core's intent to do it.

## Observations

The floor only ratchets **up**. Nothing re-asks whether a window would accept a shorter nub now (a Safari
window whose toolbar was hidden after the answer keeps its 56 pt nub), and nothing invalidates it on a
display change, since chrome is in points and display-independent. The cost of being wrong is 16 pt of
visible nub, against a size correction's frozen column, so it buys no mechanism of its own.

An app that ignores the slot's `y` entirely — landing at its own position however tall a nub it is offered
— is not modelled: the floor is learned from it, the next slot is refused the same way, and the guard on a
repeated identical answer is what stops the loop rather than a fix. It would re-park once per distinct
answer. Nothing observed does this; Safari's clamp is monotone.

A **horizontal** clamp is not modelled either. A window that also moved its `x` is read as refusing to park
rather than stating a chrome, so nothing is learned and the park is re-issued as before. The sliver is 1 pt
and no app has been seen to refuse it.
