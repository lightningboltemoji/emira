# The vertical transition

> Change `1785112033` — 2026-07-26

## Goal

A workspace switch jumped. Animate it: the outgoing strip slides out and the incoming one slides in, up
or down according to the lexicographic move, under the existing cover.

## Implementation

A workspace switch is a structural edit in the sense `Engine.finishStructuralEdit` already means —
before and after are two different geometries, no number the new frames derive from exists, and what
animates is each window's displacement from where it now belongs decaying to zero. All three verbs
route through it. No new `Effect`, no new `Event`, no fourth animated quantity; `Motion.swift` and
`EmiraShell` are byte-for-byte unchanged.

**The new geometric term is a sign, not a distance** (`Workspaces.verticalOffset(of:metrics:)`). Every
unfocused workspace sits one screen away, so `1 → z` travels the same distance as `1 → 2`. As a distance
the address space would be a 36-screen ribbon, a jump across it would sweep 34 workspaces, and the
capture scope would be the whole set. As a sign, a third workspace's offset is unchanged by a switch
that does not involve it, so a spammed switch stays a two-strip motion. `H` is the physical working
height; against the content area a neighbour would rest inside the outer-gap margin, which the cover
paints.

**The arithmetic is a two-row table.** With the new address sorting after the old, a window on the
outgoing strip goes `y → y − H` and one on the incoming strip `y + H → y`, so both seed `(0, +H)` and
the strips travel rigidly one screen apart. `move-to-workspace` follows without additions: the moved
window's "after" is a screen away, so it travels toward its new workspace while the columns it left
close ranks. The follow verb differs geometrically — the moved window is on the focused workspace both
before and after, so its seed is purely horizontal.

It went into `Workspaces`; `Layout.swift` is untouched, since a single strip has no opinion about
workspaces. `Workspaces.naturalFrames(…)` asks each strip its own question and offsets the answer. Both
new members are presentation-plane only, because on the truth plane an off-workspace window is parked.

Three consequences:

- Each unfocused strip resolves at **its own stored scroll offset**. Applied the live animator, an
  outgoing strip would slide sideways as it left, in step with a scroll on the arriving workspace.
- `StructuralSnapshot.start` is gone; the after-read takes the viewport's live value. For a
  single-strip edit the two are the same number; for a switch they must differ, since the offset is
  per-workspace and switching restores the incoming strip's remembered scroll between the two reads.
  That is what cancels the horizontal axis.
- `scopeUnion` orders by `Workspaces.allWindowIds` rather than the focused strip's, or the departing
  half is dropped from the scope. Across two strips z-order is arbitrary, since they never overlap.

Two decisions from the snapped slice reversed: a switch mid-transition rides the open cover rather than
abandoning it, and the outgoing workspace remembers where its strip **was** rather than where an
abandoned scroll was heading, which would jump it sideways as it left.

## Observations

- Fixed here, one line: the shell places every layer at its capture-time frame, and `captureReady`'s
  raise emitted teleports but no `setLayerFrame`s. For a scroll that is a no-op blit; for an
  off-viewport column scrolling in it is ~1 × 40 pt for one frame; for a switch it is a whole screen,
  since the incoming strip is captured at its park slivers. The raise now blits before it teleports.
- Residual: a second switch mid-flight exposes a band of desktop at the screen edge the arriving strip
  enters from. A switch has no runway — the next workspace is flush with the screen edge, so its layers
  expose the band on the first frame. Measured on an 800 pt screen: 0 pt when the second press lands
  before the raise, 65 pt at a 2-frame capture batch, 195 pt at 4 (about the real 36 ms one), 450 pt at
  8. Bounded by a test. The fix is shoulder *workspaces*, an extra screen of stills on every switch.
  **Needs a visual call from Tanner**, as does the vertical clip: layers travel through the top and
  bottom of the strut-inset overlay, so a strip vanishes at the menu-bar edge rather than sliding under
  it.
- Not run on a real desktop. 759 tests pass. Frames, duration, fps, capture-head latency, RSS and a
  mid-motion screenshot of both strips in flight are outstanding.
- `Column`'s water-fill lets a floored window overflow its box, and such a window can show that
  overflow at the top of the screen mid-switch. `max(H, tallest)` would fix it and would break the
  property the model rests on: both strips must move by the same number, and a content-dependent one
  differs per strip.
