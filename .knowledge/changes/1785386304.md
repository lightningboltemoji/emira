# an app icon the system can relight

> Change `1785386304` — 2026-07-29

## Goal

emira shipped with no icon, so every surface that names it drew the generic application placeholder.
`LSUIElement` means that is never a Dock icon, which makes it easy to assume it does not matter — but
the surfaces it does reach are the Accessibility and Screen Recording rows in Privacy & Security, and
the prompts that precede them. That is the first thing emira shows a user, and it was a grey sheet of
paper.

A flat 1024 image would not have fixed it. macOS 26 does not draw an app icon, it *relights* one:
specular highlights, per-group shadow, blur and translucency are applied to the artwork's layers at
draw time, and the tinted and clear appearances remap those layers onto a luminance ramp. Hand it a
single flattened image and there is nothing to light — it takes the legacy path, which masks the image
into the squircle and stops, and looks visibly wrong in every appearance except Default.

## Implementation

`Resources/emira.icon` is the source: three layer SVGs and the `icon.json` that composes them.
`actool` compiles it to `Assets.car` and `CFBundleIconName` names the icon inside.

**Three offset panes, on a unit grid.** A pane is 10 × 7 units — the shape of a window — each one
placed a unit right and a unit down from the last, so the bounding box is 12 × 9. One unit is
64.85 px: the box's long side is 76% of the 1024 canvas. 82% pushes the front pane's corner into the
squircle's own curve, where the mask eats it; 70% reads small, because the two-unit offset means this
bounding box is mostly stack and not mostly pane. Corner radius is 0.35 units — 22.7 px. The grid is
what makes the whole composition two numbers, and a transpose a swap of them.

**The panes are told apart by tone, and nothing else.** No ring, no stroke, no gap. That is the rule
the site's demo animations already work under, and here it is not a preference: the tinted and clear
appearances collapse the artwork onto a monochrome ramp keyed on luminance, so two panes separated by
hue alone merge into one shape the moment the user leaves Default.

**Which greens, therefore, is the load-bearing decision.** Back to front the panes are `--color-logo`,
`--color-logo-mid`, `--color-logo-lit` on a plate of `--color-logo-deep`. The brand's usual
arrangement is the inverse — green shapes on `#fafafa` — and it was tried first and rejected: its three
tones span 0.109 to 0.292 in relative luminance, and in tinted mode the back two panes dissolve into
the plate. The deep plate frees the top of the palette, and the span becomes 0.191 to 0.575, roughly
three times the headroom, with every pane still contrasting against the plate it sits on. That the
front pane is `lit` is not incidental: in the site's palette `lit` means the window that just opened,
or the one the screen has come to rest on. The front pane is the focused one.

**One group per pane, not one group holding three layers.** A group is the unit that `shadow` and
`specular` apply to, so three groups is what makes each pane cast onto the one behind it and pick up
its own highlight — which is the whole reason to layer rather than flatten. `groups` is ordered front
to back; the checked-in `.icon` files of apps that have a full-bleed backdrop list it last.

**Nothing the system supplies is baked in.** The SVGs carry no mask, no rounded-rect container, no
shadow and no lighting gradient, and each is the full 1024 canvas with the pane positioned inside it,
so `translation-in-points` stays zero. Baked lighting would be lit a second time.

**The layers carry no `fill` in `icon.json`**, so their own SVG fills are used and each file is a
viewable drawing on its own rather than a shape needing the manifest to have a colour. Key order
matches what Icon Composer serialises, so opening this and saving it should be a minimal diff.

**Dark swaps the plate for `system-dark` and leaves the greens alone.** They were chosen to read
against a near-black as well as against the deep plate, so a second set of tones would be a second
thing to keep in step for no gain.

**No `CFBundleIconFile`, and the legacy `.icns` is deleted.** `actool` writes one unconditionally,
and it is not a fallback for a missing `Assets.car` since the same command produces both — its only
reader would be a macOS older than 26, which `LSMinimumSystemVersion` rules out. Measured before
removing it: with the `.icns` gone and the bundle re-signed, `NSWorkspace.icon(forFile:)` still returns
emira's icon.

**`make app` compiles the icon when it can and warns when it cannot.** `actool` lives in Xcode, not
the Command Line Tools, and `xcrun --find` is the probe — `/usr/bin/actool` exists either way and only
errors when asked to work. A bundle without an icon runs exactly as well as one with it, so a missing
Xcode is worth a line of warning rather than a failed build.

## Observations

- `icon.json` was written by hand against the schema that shipping `.icon` bundles use, not produced by
  Icon Composer. `actool` accepts it with no notices or warnings and `assetutil` shows the compiled
  colours as the exact sRGB triples the palette asks for, so the format is right. Opening it in Icon
  Composer and saving it is the round trip that has not been done.
- The four appearances have not been seen in Finder at each of the four Icon & widget styles. Default
  and Dark were read out of the `.icns` `actool` emits, which is Apple's own render of these layers;
  tinted was read back through `NSWorkspace`, which on this machine is set to `ClearLight` with a
  Graphite tint and where the three panes stay separable. That is the mode the tone ramp was chosen
  for, so it is the one worth having seen — but it is one style, not four.
- `translucency` is off on all three groups. It lets the plate through the pane, which is exactly the
  erosion of tone separation this composition cannot afford. If Clear reads flat, it is the first knob
  to turn, and the honest reason it is off is that it was reasoned about rather than compared.
- The exploration that picked the fill fraction and the tones was a throwaway script and was not kept;
  the geometry above is the whole of what it produced.
- `Assets.car` is 1.6 MB for one icon, which is every appearance and scale the system may ask for,
  precompiled. It is most of the bundle's resources and none of its code.
