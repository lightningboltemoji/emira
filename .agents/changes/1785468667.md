# `grow` and `shrink` catch where the strip goes flush

> Change `1785468667` — 2026-07-30

## Goal

The continuous width knob had no opinion about what was on screen. Two columns at 50% and 45% of a
1000-wide viewport have 50 pt of slack between them and the edge; `grow 10%` spends 100 and takes a
window that was whole down to 50 pt cut off, when what was almost certainly wanted was the flush
arrangement 50 pt away. The packed strip is a configuration worth being able to land on, and by
arithmetic alone it is reachable only by computing the remainder by hand.

`[layout] resize-detent` makes the two resize verbs catch there. A delta that would carry a viewport
edge across a column edge is cut short at that edge; a delta starting *from* it spends in full. So the
first press packs the strip and the second pushes the neighbour off, which is the thing the second
press can only have meant.

Off by default, beside `center-focused-column` — the other `[layout]` bool that changes what every
reveal does.

## Implementation

**The notch is stateless, and that is what makes it worth building.** The obvious spelling is a
remembered one: mark the press whose delta was clamped, and let the next press within some window
through. That flag has to be invalidated by everything else that moves the strip — a focus change, a
new window, a workspace switch, a monitor change, an app answering a `SizeCorrection` — and each of
those is a place to forget. Being *in* the notch is the same information and cannot go stale: the
strip is flush, or it isn't. `Strip.resizeDetent` returns `nil` from a viewport edge already sitting on
a column edge, and the pass-through falls out of that one `nil` rather than out of a rule.

**Without the pass-through the key goes dead.** Flush counts as fully visible (`isFullyVisible` is
inclusive at the edges), so a predicate phrased as "no column may stop being whole" computes a clamp of
zero once the strip is packed, and every press after the first does nothing. The notch has to be a
place the sweep *starts on* as well as one it stops at.

**Edge crossings, not a search over widths.** The first spelling tried was a simulation: write the
candidate width into a scratch layout, re-derive the reveal offset, ask whether the set of whole
columns shrank, and bisect for the boundary. It fails twice. The predicate isn't monotone in the width
— `offsetToReveal` scrolls *left* for a focused column cut on its left, which can bring a column back
as the width grows — so a bisection has no boundary it is guaranteed to find. And it lands on the notch
only to a tolerance, where the whole mechanism needs the arithmetic to land on the edge exactly, since
landing on it is what the next press reads. The geometry is piecewise-linear with finitely many
breakpoints, so the breakpoints are what to compute: the column edges, and which one the sweep reaches
first.

**Only the columns from `i` on can be crossed.** A column's width moves itself and everything to its
right; the edges to its left are fixed under it. So the candidates for the right viewport edge are the
right edges of columns `i...`, and a focused column already cut has none below the edge — nothing to
protect, and `grow` passes through. Right edges only, not every column boundary: the viewport's right
edge landing on a column's *left* edge is that column gone entirely, which is a legitimate arrangement
but not one to stop a press at. It reappears anyway where it is the honest answer — for a neighbour
already cut, the nearest edge below is the focused column's own right edge, which is that column
filling the viewport.

**Uncentred, the left edge is never the first crossing, and the code doesn't compute it.** A reveal
does the minimal scroll, so the viewport does not move until the focused column is flush against its
right edge — at least as far as the right-edge notch, which is at most that same distance. Anything
evicted off the left is therefore strictly further away. Centred, both edges move at half the width's
speed from the first point of the delta, so both are candidates and the notch is the nearer of the two,
doubled.

**The offset is read at `target`, not `current`.** The detent is computed against the widths `Layout`
holds, which are resting values; pairing them with an in-flight offset describes a strip that exists in
neither plane. Hammering the key is the case that makes it matter, and `.target` is what the second
press is aimed at.

**Scoped to the continuous verbs.** `resizeFocusedColumn` is shared with `cycleWidth` and with both
halves of `fullscreen`, and the clamp went into `handleResizeColumn` above it rather than into the
shared body. A preset is an exact intent — ½ has to stay ½ on any strip — and a fullscreen column is
100% by definition. Only the knob with no intended value of its own can be cut short without lying.

**Not `snap`.** The first name for it, and it collides with `TransitionMode.snap` badly enough to be
disqualifying: `resizeFocusedColumn` reads both values inside one function, where a comment already has
to say "under `snap` the width is left out of `Motion`". `detent` is the word for a continuous control
that catches at a notch you can push past, and it is only opaque until the help string is read.

## Observations

- **Flush is approximate wherever an app quantizes.** The notch is computed from resolved widths, but a
  resize re-asks every window in the column, and one that snaps to character cells answers with a width
  some points off what was asked. So the press lands *near* the notch, and the tolerance that decides
  "already flush" — `Strip.visibilityTolerance`, half a point — is tighter than such an app's error.
  For those columns the second press will sometimes catch a second time instead of passing through.
  Unmeasured against a real terminal; the fix, if it is one, is a tolerance the detent owns rather than
  one borrowed from placement diffing.
- **A shrink at the far end of the strip is untreated.** Shrinking reduces `contentWidth` and so
  `maxOffset`, which can pull a viewport resting against the strip's right end leftward — motion the
  detent does not model. It is inert in practice: a viewport at `maxOffset` has no cut column to its
  right, so there is no shrink notch to be wrong about.
- **Not yet looked at on a screen.** Everything here is asserted against resolved widths and visibility,
  which says nothing about whether catching at 50 pt *feels* like a catch or like a dropped keypress
  under `smooth`. The width spring is unchanged, so a caught press is a shorter animation of the same
  shape — the open question is whether that reads as intent or as lag.
- The centred branch is asserted at both altitudes (`StripTests.centredResizeCatchesOnTheNearerEdgeAtHalfSpeed`
  and `EngineTests.aCentredResizeCatchesOnTheEdgeTheUncentredOneNeverReaches`), the second because the
  arithmetic being right does not mean `centerFocusedColumn` reaches it.
