# Trackpad scrolling

> Change `1785913219` — 2026-08-05

## Goal

A three-finger horizontal swipe scrolls the strip, continuously, under the cover — the strip tracks the
fingers, and where it comes to rest is a setting. A scrollable tiler's signature interaction is the strip
following your hand; a swipe that fires a `focus right` is a keybinding with a worse trigger.

Suppression stays out of scope. A user who wants emira's three-finger gestures turns off the system's own in
System Settings → Trackpad. That decision is what keeps the tap `listenOnly`, which is load-bearing
throughout: a non-consuming tap cannot stall the event stream if emira hangs, the risk `CarbonHotkeys.swift`
documents choosing Carbon to avoid.

## Implementation

What a daemon can see of a trackpad decided the shape of everything else, so it was measured first — four
throwaway probes, on this machine, with a real hand on the pad:

| Layer | Sees three fingers? |
| --- | --- |
| `NSEvent.addGlobalMonitorForEvents` | **No.** Zero gesture or touch events, ever. |
| `CGEventTap` on `NSEventTypeGesture` (29) → `NSEvent(cgEvent:)` → `allTouches()` | **Yes.** Per finger: identity, phase, normalized position. |
| `MultitouchSupport.framework` (private) | Yes — raw contact frames. Not needed. |

The global monitor is not a tuning problem. Over a window in which two-finger scrolling produced 300
`scrollWheel` events with a full phase vocabulary, gesture and touch events numbered zero — `NSTouch` data is
delivered only into the frontmost app's own view hierarchy. **So the tap is the requirement, not a
preference**, and that one finding constrains everything else. Through it a swipe arrives as ~8 ms samples
carrying all three contacts, with a clean `began → moved → ended` arc and fingers lifting one at a time.

Four things that bit, each now a comment beside the line that answers it:

1. **Fold only type 29.** A vertical three-finger swipe interleaves `magnify` (30), which `NSEvent(cgEvent:)`
   cannot bridge — it yields an event carrying *no touches*, which reads as "the fingers left". The first
   recognizer tore its episode down 323 times and could never call a vertical swipe.
2. **Touch space is bottom-left origin**, unlike the `CGEvent.location` seam in `observePointerMotion`. No
   flip appears in the code because the only axis kept is `x`, which runs the same way in both spaces, and the
   vertical term is read as a magnitude by the dominance test alone. A feature taking a *sign* off `y`
   would owe the flip.
3. **`allTouches()` raises on a non-touch event and AppKit swallows the exception mid-dispatch** — the handler
   stops half-run, with no crash and no log. The guard is the event type, never a `nil` or an empty set.
4. **The tap fires for any finger at all**, including one-finger cursor movement (2370 samples in a single
   observation window). Same standing idle cost `observePointerMotion` exists to dodge, and the same answer:
   not installed until something wants it.

A window cannot be moved at 120 Hz, so a finger-driven scroll is the transition machinery with the spring
replaced by a hand: cover up, layers sliding every frame, reals teleported once. Recognition stays in the
shell, where hysteresis belongs, but the **live delta reaches the reducer**, because the quantity it moves is
the reducer's and nothing else may hold a second opinion about it.

`IMPLEMENTATION.md §2` forbids anything entering the pump at the refresh rate that isn't a tick, and its cost
is the `onStateChanged` drain where the guide re-derives its projection. So the stream is not exempted: **the
two edges are dispatched the instant they happen, and the stream is not dispatched at all.** The recognizer
accumulates travel and `DisplayLinkDriver.onFrame` drains it on the frame boundary, ahead of that frame's
tick — one write per painted frame, rather than two between paints on a 120 Hz pad over a 60 Hz screen with
the first thrown away.

What this does *not* buy, and should not be overclaimed: the drain and the tick are two `dispatch` calls, so
during a gesture the guide re-derives its projection twice per frame instead of once. That is a factor of two
on a cost it already pays at 120 Hz through any transition, bounded by a hand. If it ever measures, the fix is
a batch `dispatch([Event])` appending both before pumping once, which invariant 4 is indifferent to. Not built
speculatively.

At the lift the hand has a velocity `v`. For a critically damped spring a target placed at exactly `v/ω` ahead
makes `b = v + ωd₀` vanish, taking the `(1 + …t)` term of the critical solution with it, and the solution
collapses to `error(t) = D·e^(−ωt)`, `velocity(t) = v·e^(−ωt)`. **A glide is not a new solver — it is a spring
aimed at the one place it would have coasted to anyway**, and the throw distance is not a second tunable: it
*is* `1/ω` of the spring the user configured. A friction solver was rejected on exactly that ground.

- **`free`** → rest is the clamped projection, travelled under `glideSpring`, seeded with `v`.
- **`magnet`** → rest is the magnet offset nearest the projection, travelled under **`scrollSpring`**: the
  destination is a column exactly as `focus left`'s is, so it should arrive with the feel every other column
  arrival has. The projection is used only to *choose*. It cannot overshoot in practice either — the scroll
  spring's own throw is `v/28` against the `v/10` that picked the edge, and overshoot needs the opposite.

**Which way a swipe carries the strip is a second key**, `mouse.trackpad-scroll-direction`, orthogonal to
the mode — two keys rather than six words. It is the core's, not the shell's, for §3's rule: the event stays
what the pad said (`+x` toward the right of the pad, a fact about the hardware) and what a fraction of the pad
*means* is the reducer's, exactly as the gain and the content-area scaling are. It is applied inside
`trackpadPoints`, the gesture's one conversion, so the lift's velocity and its projection flip with the travel
— a distance and a velocity that disagreed about which way is forward would throw the projection off the wrong
end of the strip, and having one conversion is what makes that unrepresentable rather than merely tested.

emira cannot derive this from the system: it reads raw contacts rather than scroll events, so macOS's own
*Natural scrolling* inversion is never applied on the way in. `standard` is the default because it is what a
scrollbar does and because a user who has not thought about it has not asked emira to invert anything — while
macOS's own default, and its three-finger Space swipe, are `natural`. Following the system preference instead
of offering a key was considered and left alone: it would trade an honest setting for a defaults read plus a
change notification, and the answer is one the user can state in one line.

The glide spring's default is a calculation, not a taste. Settle is `ln(throw/ε)/ω` and must fit inside
`hold-timeout` with margin, or the deadline pulls the cover out from under a glide still travelling — and
because the reals were teleported to the rest at lift, that reveals a truth plane the layers have not reached,
which is a visible jump. Settle being *logarithmic* in the throw is what makes the constraint satisfiable: at
`ω = 10` a 6000 pt/s flick throws 600 pt and settles in ~710 ms, and quadrupling it throws 2400 pt and still
settles in ~850 ms. Hence `stiffness = 100, damping-ratio = 1`, spelled as stiffness for `.smooth`'s reason —
a round trip through a square root prints `100.00000000000001` into the golden document.

**The echo hazard.** `Effect.focus` comes home as `focusChanged(.ours)` → `revealAcrossWorkspaces` →
`scrollReveal`, which asked `offsetToReveal(i, from: offset.current)` — a minimal reveal derived from wherever
the strip happens to be mid-glide. That answer is not the magnet offset, so left alone every magnetized settle
would be undone milliseconds later by the echo of the focus its own settle emitted.

Nothing had exercised it, because **every aim in the system is itself a minimal reveal, so re-deriving one is
idempotent. A magnetized rest is the first that is not.** `scrollReveal` asks its question at `offset.target`
rather than at the live offset, and a column already framed there re-aims nothing. Small, and correct beyond
this feature — the same reason `clampRestingViewports` leaves a display mid-transition alone.

Rejected, each with the failure mode that decided it:

- **A discrete recognizer — one swipe, one `focus left`.** Strictly less machinery, and it works with
  `transition = off`. It cannot express `free`, and a scroll that rests anywhere is the only thing that proves
  the fingers were driving position. Worth keeping in view as the degradation `off` could have instead of
  clamping, if a user with no Screen Recording grant ever asks.
- **Raw samples straight into the pump.** Honest — the delta genuinely is an input to the reducer — but it
  doubles `onStateChanged` at 120 Hz, which is the exact cost `Event.pointerEntered`'s note refuses.
- **Folding the delta into `tick(dt)`.** Would remove the third event, at the price of contaminating the one
  event the clock story rests on — and the trackpad is not phase-locked to the display, so the tick would
  carry a number measured at some other time.
- **Rubber-banding at the strip's ends.** Needs an out-of-range regime and a return spring, and contradicts
  the clamp-don't-wrap rule `WorkspaceRef` and `MonitorRef` both state.
- **A stored `glideSpring` inside `Motion`.** `glideViewport(to:velocity:under:on:)` takes the
  spring instead, because `magnet` and `free` want different ones and the reducer has both to hand.
  That leaves *which spring a viewport is under* derived from the last aim — `retargetViewport` and
  `snapViewport` reset it — with no second copy to keep in step.

## Observations

**Numbers, all of them first guesses.** `trackpadGain = 3` screens per pad sweep, matched to macOS's own
three-finger swipe by eye and deliberately a constant rather than a key. Commit threshold ~0.01 normalized,
reasoned from the cost asymmetry rather than measured — a false positive is a cover that goes up and comes
straight back down, which is invisible, while a false negative is 200 ms of dead strip at the head of every
gesture. Dominance 1.5×, straight from the probe, where it called four of four directions correctly. Watchdog
silence 200 ms, an order of magnitude over the observed ~8 ms inter-sample gap.

**The lift's velocity is decayed by the staleness of the last sample**, to full decay at the watchdog window.
Displacement over the trailing window alone reads a hand that stopped before letting go as the flick it
stopped being; measuring to the lift's own timestamp instead under-reads every ordinary lift by the frame gap.
The decay is the estimator that gets both, and it is not something the probe measured.

**Untested by anything running.** The tap's install and removal are verified against the shipped
`CGGestureTap` — it comes up on this machine, so the capability clamp does not fire — but the contact bridging
is inherited from the probe rather than re-verified through this code with a hand on the pad. Tap re-enable
after `kCGEventTapDisabledByTimeout` is a known obligation the probe never provoked — the watchdog is the
backstop that makes getting it wrong survivable. And what no suite can answer: whether the gain feels right,
whether the commit threshold fires early enough to feel direct, whether the magnet picks the edge the eye
expected, and whether a glide reads as momentum or as a spring.

**`free` under `layout.center-focused-column` re-centres when focus moves.** The magnet candidates become the
column centres under that setting, and the echo guard's test with it — so a free rest that displaces focus is
pulled onto the new column's centre by the echo. That is coherent with what centering *means* (a statement
about where the strip rests), and it is inconsistent only in that a free rest which moves no focus stays put.
Left as it is rather than given a rule of its own.

**A window arriving mid-gesture re-aims the viewport** through `finishStructuralEdit`, and the next drained
sample takes it back. Self-correcting and harmless — the hand wins, which is the right answer with fingers on
the pad — but it is not the explicit "drops the latch" rule a command gets.
