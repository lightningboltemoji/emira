# A settings window that isn't a window

> Change `1786163175` — 2026-08-07

## Goal

emira had one configuration surface: the TOML file, plus `emira config` over it. That is a good surface for
someone who already knows what `layout.column-gap` does and a poor one for everyone else — the number is
legible, its effect is not, and the only way to find out is to save and watch a whole desktop rearrange.

`ConfigSchema` had been built for this since it became a table: one entry per setting carrying its label, its
sentence, its legal values and its section, with the reader, the writer and `emira config explain` as three
consumers off the one list. The fourth was named in the comment and missing. This adds it.

## Implementation

**A target, not a folder.** `EmiraSettings` sits between `EmiraConfig` and `EmiraShell`. It may read `Config`
and call `Layout`, `Strip` and `LayoutMetrics`; it may not name `Engine`, `State`, `Event`, `Effect` or
`Command`. The module graph cannot enforce that — `EmiraConfig` pulls in `EmiraCore`, so the reducer is one
`import` away — so `ImportFenceTests` scans the sources and fails on any of the five. It strips comments and
string literals first, because the module's own header states the rule by naming all five, and a fence that
tripped on its own explanation would be unwritable. It was checked in both directions: a file containing
`let reducer: Engine?` makes it fail.

The line falls there because what a preview honestly needs is the **geometry**, and geometry is the authority
worth sharing. What the reducer does is reconcile a truth plane against a structure, and a preview has no truth
plane: nothing to place, nothing to cover, nothing that can refuse.

**Three seams moved to keep it one authority rather than two.** `LayoutMetrics(config:workingArea:…)` — and
this is not the clean hoist it looked like, since only five of the nine arguments come from `Config`; the other
four are the world's and the workspaces', and they default to empty, which is honest because a preview has no
world to correct and nothing parked. `Layout.scrollOffsetToFrame(window:from:metrics:center:)`, the
centre-versus-reveal fork that `layout.center-focused-column` chooses between, which was private on `Engine`
behind a `State`. And `GuideModel` into `EmiraCore`, so the guide take draws through the projection that places
the real guide; `GuidePanel` and `RoundedLayer` stayed in the shell. `GuideModel.layout(for:on:)` takes a
`State` and is therefore unreachable from the preview, correctly — what is shared is
`projection(settings:working:strip:)`, which is pure.

**The composition was built from a measured probe rather than described.** `NSVisualEffectView` with
`.hudWindow` and `blendingMode = .behindWindow` gives a live blur of the real desktop from an accessory app;
`.underPageBackground` and `.windowBackground` are opaque and render as flat black **with the blur working
perfectly behind them**, which is a silent failure worth a full afternoon. A borderless window at
`CGShieldingWindowLevel() − 1` covers the menu bar and the Dock. No Screen Recording grant is involved, and the
blur being live is what makes Save land as a visible event: the daemon's ordinary hot reload rearranges the
real desktop behind the scrim while the mock holds still.

Rejected: a frozen capture of the desktop, blurred by us. It would need the grant, it would need degrading when
the grant is absent, and it would throw the Save moment away.

**It is set down and lifted off, and those are one animation run in either direction.** The whole composition
dissolves while the stack it carries travels the last 4% of a zoom, so it arrives as an object placed on the
desktop rather than as a window that appeared over one. A scale *above* one is what makes it a placement —
below one is the same motion telling the opposite story, something growing out of the screen — and the curve
is symmetric about its midpoint, so the exit is the entrance backwards and not a second animation with the
same duration. **A scrim cannot zoom**: its edges are the screen's, so a scaled one shows desktop down one
side and crops the other, which is why the dissolve belongs to everything and the travel to the stack. That
is what `Stage` is — the monitor and the slab as one view, since two transforms about two centres are two
zooms that happen to agree. Reduce Motion keeps the dissolve and drops the travel, which is what the
preference asks a zoom to become rather than what it asks to be removed.

Two traps there, both silent. A `CALayer`'s transform is applied about its **anchor point** and an AppKit
backing layer anchors at its origin, so a bare `CATransform3DMakeScale` drags the stack toward the bottom
left as it shrinks — at 4% over 220 ms that reads as a wobble rather than as a mistake, so `Stage.zoom` is
tested against three anchor points. And `NSView` answers `self` for any point inside its bounds: a container
over the composition swallows every click in the 44 pt gap between the monitor and the slab, and that gap is
blur, and a double click on the blur is the way out.

**The one number.** `Projection` owns `k` and is display-*local* — the mock is a little world whose origin is
zero — so no `flipHeight` appears and nothing depends on `ScreenGeometry`, which is on the wrong side of the
fence. The working area falls out as `visibleFrame` expressed inside `frame`, which is the display's struts by
definition, so no strut arithmetic is duplicated. The layout runs at real scale and only the result is
projected: run it against the small rect and an 8 pt gap becomes 8 pt of a 900-point screen. A window's corner
radius, title bar and stoplights are real dimensions projected the same way — they were fixed mock points
first, which is a different real dimension at every `k` and reads next to an actual desktop as the wrong window.

**A `Scene` is the set; a `Take` is the script over it, keyed per setting.** The split earns itself because the
script is empty for most settings: geometry re-derives with nothing playing, and a script is only for behaviour.
Several takes share one scene, so crossing between two settings in a section produces no motion at all. The
`Catalog` test requires every schema entry to have a take or be named on `notDemonstrable`, which holds three:
`layout.interactive-resize` is a hand on a real frame, `animation.hold-timeout` is a timeout, `animation.cover`
is a policy about capture.

The scroll offset is a **fold over the beats** rather than a function of the final set, because `offsetToReveal`
is relative to where the strip already is. It is observable and tested: reaching the third column by walking to
the end and stepping back gives offset 1816; arriving from rest gives 916.

**`PreviewMotion` is `Motion` with the sessions removed.** Reusing `Motion` would have meant the preview holding
a `TransitionSession` that can never open. Which spring carries a displacement follows the schema's own
sentences — a size that changed is a resize, a pure translation is the viewport's if the viewport moved and the
strip's own rearrangement if it did not. That last is a stated approximation: a beat that scrolls *and*
rearranges attributes both to the scroll, and the alternative is decomposing each window's travel to run two
springs on one layer, which would animate no real quantity.

**Save writes the file; nothing pushes a draft to the daemon.** One path in, so the preview and the desktop
cannot hold different opinions. The write is checked against what is on disk at the last moment, because the
watcher-driven banner can be raced and because a comment-only edit changes the file without changing the
`Config` the loader reports at all.

**Dismissal is deliberate.** Escape, or a double click on the blur. `DesktopView.hitTest` answers `self` so a
click on the mock is swallowed — `nil` would let it fall through to the scrim, which is dismissal, and the mock
is the one thing on screen the user is certainly looking at. Nothing inside it is addressable either way.

**Hotkeys are suspended while the scrim is up.** `RegisterEventHotKey` claims a chord at the window server, so
a binding fires whatever is focused; with every display scrimmed that rearranges a desktop the user cannot see,
and a bound `⌘A` never reaches a numeric field. `HotkeyManager` holds what to restore rather than the caller,
because the daemon's copy of the config goes stale across a reload while `live` is by definition what is
registered. A reload arriving while suspended replaces what `resume` will take.

Four bugs are worth recording because none was findable by reading. A row pinned to a stack it had not yet been
added to **hung the whole app** on open — a constraint between two views with no common ancestor. `masksToBounds`
clips a layer's shadow along with its sublayers, so a pane needs two layers or its square title band paints over
its own rounded corners. `NSWorkspace.icon(forFile:)` answers an image whose `size` is 32 points while carrying
representations up to 2048, and `CALayer.contents` resolves through that size — so a layer got 64 px and scaled
it up sixfold. And `desktopImageOptions` reports *Fill Screen* as proportional scaling **with** `allowClipping`;
reading the scaling alone letterboxes a filled desktop and paints the bars in the fill colour.

## Observations

`animation.transition` and `animation.window` play a take rather than changing how the mock moves —
glide-versus-cut and stretched-versus-cropped are not modelled in `PreviewMotion`, so those two settings
demonstrate the motion they govern rather than the difference between their values. That is the largest gap in
the demo story.

The guide preview draws no separators. `GuideModel` computes them inside `layout(for:on:)`, behind a `State`.
With a non-zero `column-gap` the tiles separate on their own, so it only reads as one block at gap 0 — which is
what the desktop looks like there.

`mouse.follows-focus` demonstrates `off` against everything else. Its three live rungs differ over *when* they
decline — a hover, a pointer already inside — and those are facts about a real pointer that a scripted take
cannot stage without lying.

Escape pressed inside the 220 ms of the entrance restarts the travel from the seated scale rather than from
where the stack has got to: the animation is spelled from one placement to the other rather than from the
presentation layer. It is a couple of points of pop inside a fifth of a second, and carrying the in-flight
value is the only thing that would remove it.

The multi-display scrim is written and unexercised: the machine this was built on reports one screen. So is the
white-text branch of the menu bar, whose wallpaper sampling is unit-tested on both sides but was only ever seen
resolving to black.

Nothing measures the cost of a full-screen behind-window blur under sustained motion. It is smooth to the eye
on this machine; there is no number.

`[keys]` and `[[window-rules]]` are still file-only, as `ConfigSchema` says by carrying no entries for them.
A chord editor has to capture keystrokes without the daemon's own hotkeys eating them, and a rules editor has
to reorder blocks, which is an API `ConfigDocument` does not have.
