# A film with a hole in it loses to the whole one held

> Change `1790052991` — 2026-09-21

## Goal

Two columns each wider than half the viewport, and one of them is hanging off an edge at all times. An
app that rasterizes only what is on screen hands back a still that is transparent where the window hung
off — so the stand-in built from it slides that band of nothing across the glass for the length of the
glide, showing `WindowAnimation.crop`'s wash where the window's content should be, and only comes right
at the cross-fade onto a real window that has since painted itself. Reported on Zoom and Chrome, at 70%
columns, bouncing focus left and right.

The pixels are the app's to give and the capture plane loses none of them — measured with
`SCContentFilter(desktopIndependentWindow:)` at up to 70% off-display against a plain `CALayer`-backed
window, a `CAMetalLayer` one, a WebKit one and Zoom's own, all of which come back at the full requested
size with alpha 1.00 across every column band. What the store can do is decline to prefer the film with
the hole, because the film before it did not have one: a window is photographed at the **head** of each
transition it is scoped into, which is the resting state the previous transition ended in, so bouncing
focus films each column whole on the transition that leaves it and holed on the one that returns. The
complete photograph is exactly one transition old at the moment the incomplete one lands.

## Implementation

**`SurfaceCache` gains a second axis, and it is the one place the mint does not decide.** A film
measured holed loses to a held whole film *at the same size*, and to nothing else — not to a whole film
of another size, not to a held holed one. Size is the condition because rule 2 governs which photograph
may stand in at all, so a whole film of the wrong size could stand in for nothing, and holding it over a
right-sized holed one would leave the cover with neither. Everywhere else `mint` still orders the
writes.

The cost is softness: the held photograph has been through `reduced`, so the cover is built from a
quarter-scale still upscaled. Rule 1 already argues that trade in the other direction — the reduction is
the disclosure that keeps an old photograph from passing for the window — and a soft stand-in is the
better of the two wrong answers against one with a band of nothing in it.

**`record` answers whether it took the pixels**, and `CaptureService` gates the refresh on that. A film
the store declined overtakes no stand-in, so it stays out of `Pending.refreshed` — which is already
defined as the films that reached the store — and asks for no `captureRefreshed`: the layer is painting
what the store holds, and a cross-fade to it would be a frame of nothing changing. The ack that rule 1
owes is emitted ahead of that gate and is unaffected. The same return value silently fixes the
overtaken-film case, which asked for a repaint it had no new pixels for.

**The measurement is a 20×4 resample, and a band has to average under one alpha level in 255.**
Resampled rather than scanned: 0.004 ms against 7.3 ms for every pixel of a 2556×2090 still. Columns
only, because the strip scrolls on one axis and a window's height is the working area's. The threshold
has three orders of magnitude of room — a window's own rounded corners cost their band about a
hundredth, and the corner radii in the same file are measured at 1 pt of transparency, so the two
instruments are deliberately different: one is looking for a sliver and this one for a band.

Rejected: telling the measurement where the display edge is, so a transparent band could be confirmed
against the part of the window that actually hung off. It is strictly more specific, and it buys
nothing — a window whose own shape leaves a transparent margin reads as holed either way, and since
*every* film of such a window reads alike, none of them is ever the whole one another loses to. It would
also put display geometry into a pure function of an image, which is the thing that makes the
measurement testable at all.

## Observations

**Measured live at the geometry that produces the bug.** Ghostty at `x = -784` on a 1710-wide display —
61% of it off the left edge — measures whole, worst band alpha 1.000 against the 0.004 threshold, as
does a 1176 pt Safari window. On this machine the new branch provably never fires, which is the same
reason the artifact has never been reproducible here. Eight focus bounces on the worktree daemon ran 22
frames in 339–348 ms at 63–65 fps, indistinguishable from before.

Of every window on the desktop, the ones that measure holed are the system windows that have never drawn
(`loginwindow`, Control Center's off-screen panel, the daemon's own overlays — all bands at 0.00) and
Notification Center's 180×180 panels, whose content genuinely stops short of their left and right edges.
None is a window emira manages, so none is ever filmed for a cover.

**A window that is never seen whole again keeps a stale photograph.** The decline holds whatever was
last whole at that size, and nothing re-films it until the window is fully revealed once — which a
reveal does every time it is focused, and never for a column left permanently hanging off the edge. The
photograph is reduced, which is the disclosure rule 1 relies on, but it is staleness the old behaviour
did not have. A column too wide for the viewport is the extreme of it: no reveal brings one whole into
view, so every film of it is holed, every film wins, and it behaves exactly as it did.

**The Zoom case itself is still unreproduced here**, so what is verified is the policy and the
measurement rather than the artifact going away. The synthetic tests pin the policy on a `CapturedSurface`
built holed outright, which keeps them independent of whether the detector fires on any particular app.

**Untested: two covers over one window.** A window owed by two displays is filmed once per cover, and if
one of those films is holed the other display's cover may be the one that raised over the whole film. The
entitlement is settled separately from the pixels and both covers read the same photograph, so the
declining path leaves them agreeing rather than diverging — but nothing exercises it.
