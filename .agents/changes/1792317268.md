# Multi-display: peeks, the drawing display, and windows carried across

> Change `1792317268` — 2026-10-18

## Goal

Multi-monitor support had several defects that compounded each other. All were reproduced live on a Studio
Display (main, 2560×1440) with the built-in (1800×1169) to its left:

- **Windows spanning displays.** A column hanging off a display's edge toward another screen was drawn whole
  on the neighbour, over that screen's work. Its own display showed a frosted ghost where the column should
  have been.
- **Veils in the wrong place.** The scrim walked every pane whose frame *intersected* its display. That
  produced the ghost, and also a hole in the neighbour's veils wherever a window's undrawn overhang crossed
  them.
- **Wrong heights, kept forever.** A placement that crossed displays landed clamped to the display the window
  left, and the clamp was recorded as the app's answer (`placementCorrected`). From then on the window was
  short on every display: Ghostty 1130 pt tall on a 1374 pt working area.
- **A display left scrolled past its strip.** After `move-to-monitor-and-focus`, the display the window left
  kept its offset. Its last column sat mostly off-screen, which made the first bug happen as well.
- **A dropped window's size adopted as drawn.** Dragging a tiled window onto the smaller display made macOS
  shrink it to fit. The drag adoption read that as a resize and kept it after reverting the move.

## Implementation

**The rule, measured first.** A probe of borderless windows at seven straddles (`home.swift` in the session
scratchpad) found that the window server draws a window on exactly one display: the one holding the largest
share of its *area*. Nothing of it is drawn on any other display. It matched `NSWindow.screen` in every case,
including tall windows whose larger width and larger area were on different screens. The rule is
`Neighbourhood` (carried in `LayoutMetrics` beside the lot) and `World.monitor(drawing:)`. A tie counts as not
drawn here, since macOS breaks it.

**Peeks.** A visible column the neighbourhood would not draw is parked (`Layout.peekingFrames`,
`placedWindowIds`). `writeTruthPlane` records it in `World.peeks`, beside `placedOnScreen` and `parkedSlots`
and held with them under the cover gate. `settlePeeks` emits `setPeeks`. A `PeekPanel` stands over the
display's share of the column, showing the window's photograph. The user suggested a hoist-like stand-in, and
that is what this is, with three departures from `HoistPanel`:

- **It sits at the back of the normal level, not above the cover.** A float or a dialog stacks over a peek as
  it would over the column. No tiled window can, because the strip never overlaps.
- **It is cut to its display.** A window of ours at the column's whole frame is subject to the same drawing
  rule.
- **The scrim reads it as the column** (`PeekPanels.standIn`), at the column's whole frame. The veil is
  therefore the column's own, the display edge cuts it square, and a cover's stand-in reads the same answer
  from `Scrims.veil(of:)`.

Considered and rejected:

- **The panel carrying its own veil, as a cover stand-in does.** The scrim would not know the window was
  drawn, `veil(of:)` would answer 0, and every transition ending on a peek would pop from veiled to opaque.
- **Parking the column with no stand-in.** The strip would look as if it ended at the edge, and the cover's
  layer would pop out at every cross-fade.
- **Clamping the scroll so no column straddles.** That changes what the strip *is*, to dodge a drawing rule.

A peek's picture is a kept still at once (`SurfaceCache`, which the cover that scrolled it there has just
filled), then its own film, then a later cover's still on any move. It is filmed again only on a size
change. A click is `peekClicked`, which runs `revealAcrossWorkspaces` plus a focus, across displays when
needed. Dropping a peek waits for the write putting its window on the glass to land, unless the column left
the viewport entirely.

**A boot with something parked left every veil off the glass.** The tarp is sent ahead of the first set,
and its film of the lot's strip asked for no veiled photograph but still stamped the throttle. The set
arriving in the same batch was then paced past the cover's dismissal, so no display showed a veil until a
transition at least two seconds after boot. It was found while landing this change, as the opening scenario
came back up: a peek was standing unveiled, and the window list showed the Studio Display's scrim at alpha 0.
A photograph never asked for is now missing, not stale (`veiledAsked`). A film that was asked for and failed
still paces the next, as before. A peek appearing now starts a settle watch rather than a single re-cut,
because our own panel reaches the window list in its own time.

**The scrim walks what its display draws** (`Scrims.drawn`), and the tarp walks the same list. The sheet rule
still reads the whole desktop's front, because focus can be on the other screen. Floats, hoists, restack
recuts, `lastFocusedOnScreen` and `watch` all locate a window by its drawing display instead of its centre.

**Crossing writes wait for the app.** A probe through AX measured the cross-display clamp:

| Wait between passes | Landed height |
|---|---|
| 0 ms | 1123 (all three trials) |
| 5 ms | 1123 (all three trials) |
| 20 ms | 1374 (all three trials) |
| 50 ms | 1374 (all three trials) |

The app does not repair the clamp by itself later. An AppKit app learns its window's new display a run-loop
turn or more after the move. So `AXWindow.place(at:crossing:)` gives a tiled write that crosses displays up to
three corrective passes, waiting 25, 50 and 75 ms. `AXExecutor` judges "crossing" from the registry's last
frame. Parks are exempt: their answer never becomes a size correction, and their landings gate cross-fades.
Two boots and an arrangement round trip after the fix logged zero `placementCorrected`.

**The display a window leaves frames nothing, and clamps** (`restingOffset`). This applies only to a display
the user is not on. The acting display keeps its existing snap via `clampRestingViewports`, which the hoist
suite pins. Making that a scroll changed six tests' timing for no gain.

**Drags across displays.** `Drag.subject` now records where the window stood when the hand took it. `carried`
tests the drag against that frame: a resize holds an edge, or a corner, and nothing else moves. A drag that
crossed displays (`dropAcrossDisplays`) is `move-to-monitor-and-focus` to the display the window is drawn on
at release. The source snapshot takes the window at its dropped frame, as an arrival's does, so it travels
from the drop point into its slot.

**Validated live** on both edges. Peeks appear on the Studio Display's left edge and on the built-in's right
edge. Focus, workspace switches and peek clicks all work. A drag works in both directions. A temporary
`.forAppOnly` arrangement with the built-in on the right moved the lot and the tarp and reverted cleanly. An
`SCStream` frame strip of placed→peek shows the peek region holding exactly steady through the cover's
dismissal. Tests: `PeekTests` (core, 16), `MultiDisplayTests` (shell, 6), and one tarp plane test for the boot photograph.

## Observations

- **A peek's content is frozen** while it stands. That is the trade a picture makes; a live `SCStream` would
  keep the recording indicator lit.
- **The cover's reconstruction of the outer gap at a display edge** differs from the real gap by up to ~30
  levels near the edge. It eases over the cross-fade (~300 ms), and peeks are not involved: the identical
  curve appears on a non-peek arrival at the same edge.
- **A pin dragged to another display is still reverted.** Re-pinning on the drop display's same side is the
  natural answer and is not built.
- **Focus-follows-mouse over a peek** is admitted through `World.window(at:)`. It was not exercised live; the
  user's config has it off.
- **Parks crossing displays are not waited on.** A parked window can therefore sit at a stale size until it is
  next placed. By then the app knows its display, so the first pass lands. That was not measured on a slow app.
- **Not tested physically:** displays stacked vertically, and a mirror among three or more displays.
  `Neighbourhood` ignores a display reporting its own frame.
