# A window resized by its own handle keeps that size

> Change `1785892004` — 2026-08-04

## Goal

A tiled window resized by its own handle was reverted on release: `windowFrameChanged` folded the new
frame into `World` and `dragEnded` re-asserted the layout over it. The handle is the most direct thing a
user can say about a size, and it says what `grow` already says by another route, so reverting it reads
as a bug rather than as a policy.

The hard part is not writing the intent, it is being allowed to. **AX reports a resize identically
whoever asked for it**, and our own placements provoke one every time — so `windowFrameChanged` cannot
tell a hand from an echo. Worse than an echo: when an app clamps a write, `placementCorrected` and the
observer's `windowFrameChanged` both carry the clamped frame and **their order is not guaranteed**. A
reducer deriving intent from every frame change would read the app's own concession as the user asking
for that size, and since `dragEnded` fires on every mouse-up anywhere on the system, a shared column
holding a refusing stackmate — which leaves a standing size mismatch by construction — would ratchet on
the next click anywhere.

## Implementation

**`Drag` is the gate, and it is a three-state latch rather than a flag.** `Event.dragBegan` (a new global
mouse-down, on the monitor that already watched the ups) arms it; the first *tiled, on-screen* window to
report a frame change that differs from what `World` already holds becomes the **subject**; `dragEnded`
reads that one window and disarms. Each of the three conditions excludes a different impostor, and the
subject in particular is not belt-and-braces: a placement pass triggered mid-drag by anything else writes
the stackmates, and an app clamping one of those reports a frame change with the button still down.

It sits beside `Pointer` rather than in `World` on `Pointer`'s own argument — every field of `World` is
refreshed by observation and this cannot be — and beside rather than *inside* it because `Pointer`'s
paragraph turns on a hide being an assertion re-made rather than a latch, and this is the latch.

**Adoption is on release.** The truth plane is the target app's main thread, so folding every intermediate
frame would trade AX writes with the drag at the rate of the slowest app in the column, and none of it is
maskable: a cover over the window being dragged hides the one thing the user is looking at. This is
therefore the first motion deliberately outside the transition machinery — uncovered, snapped, through
`reassertTruthPlane` like any other idle placement. Rejected: a live fold with the horizontal reflow
deferred to release, which buys direct manipulation at the cost of a column visibly overlapping its
neighbours for the length of the drag.

**The observed size becomes the intent directly, not a delta on the target.** The user drew a rectangle
and asking the layout for that rectangle is what they asked; it also stays total over a window that was
already refusing its target size, where a delta would fold the old refusal into the new request.

**Width needed no new machinery.** It lands as a `widthOverride`, the same rung `grow`/`shrink` write and
the same rung `cycle-width` clears, under the same clamp. A stackmate that will not be that narrow is
already `Layout.resolvedWidth`'s `max`, so "get as close as possible" on this axis was free — the drag
calls `forgetCorrections` exactly as the explicit verbs do and the answers come back through the
unchanged `SizeCorrection` round trip.

**Height needed the rung that was missing.** `heightSelections` stored a *cycle index*, so an arbitrary
dragged height was not representable at all. `Workspaces.heightOverrides` is its `widthOverride`: a
`PresetSize` shadowing the ladder, cleared by `cycleHeight`, reconciled against the same window set, and
resolved in one place (`LayoutMetrics.heightIntent(of:)`) so no caller can read one rung without the
other. `Column` needed nothing — `.preset(.fixed(h))` already resolved correctly and the water-fill
already honoured bounds in both directions.

**The neighbour on the dragged edge goes back to auto**, and that is what makes this a divider drag rather
than a window growing into its stackmates. An auto window shares the column's leftover height, so pinning
one window is already an instruction to the rest; without it a column whose windows are every one of them
pinned has nobody to take the difference, and three drags walk the last window off the bottom of the
screen (`Column.resolvedHeights` lets pinned heights overflow the box by design, and `windowFrames`
stacks downward, so over-subscription pushes a window off-screen rather than overlapping one). Losing the
neighbour's stored height is the point rather than the cost: what the user just drew is where that
divider goes. `Engine.minimumWindowHeight` is the backstop beside `minimumColumnWidth` — crude on purpose,
since the real floors are unknown until an app is asked and arrive as corrections.

**A left-edge drag moves the viewport by the width delta.** The strip accumulates left to right, so a
column grows rightward whichever edge is pulled; `offset += (after − before)` nails the column's right
edge and puts the moving edge back under the pointer. It costs nothing, the offset being the one
authority on where the strip is looked at, and it falls out of the existing clamp at the strip's end.

**A solo full-height column may be made short.** The first instinct was to refuse it, but the model already
permits it: `cycle-height` on a window alone in its column pins it to a rung and leaves the rest of the
column empty desktop. Refusing the drag would have been refusing something a keybinding already does, and
a silently reverted drag reads as a bug.

**A move drag is still reverted.** Reading one as *insert here* needs a drop target and an ordering, which
is a feature rather than a branch. The classifier and the subject latch are the plumbing it would want.

`[layout] interactive-resize` gates it, at the latch, so nothing downstream carries a second opinion.
Default on: the revert was never a considered behaviour.

## Observations

Two things the suite cannot reach, both needing the daemon running and windows moved by hand. Whether the
observer's `windowFrameChanged` genuinely races `placementCorrected` on a clamp, and in which direction —
the design is safe either way, but it decides whether the button-down gate is the only thing holding this
up or merely the tidiest. And whether the mouse-down arming misfires on an app that resizes its own window
while a button happens to be down: clicking a zoom button is the realistic case, and it would be adopted
rather than reverted. That is arguably the right answer, but it has not been watched.

The `EngineHandResizeTests` adoption cases were each checked to fail with `adoptDraggedSize` stubbed out;
the five gate cases correctly keep passing there, which is what makes them gates.

Unmeasured, and only reachable by hand: what a drag costs when the subject's column has several columns to
its right. On release that is one placement pass, so the expectation is that it is indistinguishable from
a `grow` — but `grow` is animated under a cover and this is not, so the reflow lands at AX latency with
nothing hiding it.

One known limit in the left-edge compensation: the width it is measured across is the *resolved* one before
the drag and the *intent* after, since the drag forgets the column's corrections on the way past. A column
being held open by a stackmate that refuses to be narrow therefore lands its right edge a few points off,
by however much that refusal was worth, and nothing re-aims it — `handlePlacementCorrected` only re-aims a
viewport under a live cover, and this path is deliberately uncovered. Self-correcting on the next drag and
invisible on the ordinary column, so it is recorded rather than fixed.

Not addressed: a drag begun while a transition is already open on that display. `writeTruthPlane`'s phase
gate holds the writes mid-capture and mid-raise, so the adoption lands but its placement waits for the
cover. Harmless, and rare enough that forcing it would be inventing a case to fix.
