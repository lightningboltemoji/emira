# A workspace is a surface, and a surface has a layout

> Change `1788851710` — 2026-09-08

## Goal

A second arrangement — `stack`, a diagonal cascade — beside the scrolling strip, and the seam that makes
a second one possible at all. The strip stays the default and the identity of the project; what changes
is that it stops being the *only* thing a workspace can be.

The headline is what the change did **not** need. No new `Effect`: the output vocabulary is
`setFrame` / `park` / `setLayerFrame` and the cover lifecycle, and a cascade is a different set of rects
rather than a different set of verbs. No new `Event`: nothing new is observed. No new animated quantity:
before and after a layout switch are two arrangements with no number to interpolate, which is precisely
what the per-window displacement already animates. And no new transition machinery, because a layout
change *is* a structural edit in `finishStructuralEdit`'s existing sense and rides the cover it already
opens.

## Implementation

**`Layout.Kind` is a stored field on `Layout`.** Three alternatives were weighed and each fails
concretely. A `protocol LayoutEngine` behind an existential breaks `State`'s `Equatable + Codable +
Sendable`, which replay depends on, and buys nothing: there are two implementations, both in
`EmiraCore`, neither extensible from outside. An `enum` carrying per-kind *structure* duplicates the
four editing primitives in both arms — and those were chosen for atomicity over the invariants, not for
the strip's geometry, so splitting them re-opens the "remove then insert" hole they close and makes
switching lossy. Putting the kind in `LayoutMetrics` is wrong quietly: metrics are a *display's*, and a
display owns many workspaces while showing one, so every parked workspace would be laid out in the shown
one's arrangement. Storing it on `Layout` reaches every call site for free and changed no existing
signature.

**The structure is layout-independent; only the geometry switches.** `naturalFrames` is the one place
the two diverge, and `targetFrames`, `parkedFrames` and the park run now compose from it and
`visibleWindowIds` rather than resolving a `Strip` of their own — which is what makes them total over
both kinds instead of gaining an arm each. The columns, the ids, the width and height intents,
`reconcile`, `adopt`, `remove` and all four primitives are untouched.

**Most of what a cascade does not do is degeneration, not branching.** Its scroll answers are the
constant `0`, so `driveTrackpadScroll` clamps through the layout and comes back where it started, and
`reveal` / `scrollReveal` compute `end == start` and take their existing "already in view → snap, no
cover" exits with no branch in the gesture path. The branches that survive are the places the two
genuinely disagree, and each is one guard: focus and `move-window` walk a diagonal rather than two axes,
eight verbs and three settings are inert, the two pointer dials decline, and `beginTrackpadScroll`
declines. That last one is the plan's one deliberate departure — leaving it alone was "inert by
arithmetic", but the arithmetic still opened a cover over a gesture that could move nothing, and a
cover raised and dropped for nothing is exactly the *something surprising* an inert verb owes not to do.

**`fullscreen` is kept on a cascade** — the open question the plan left. It is not a rung of a ladder
`stack` does not have; it is *zoom in on this one*, the gesture a cascade wants most, and it costs a
shadow over a derived size with `Fullscreen.plain` as the record. No expel (a slot holds one window) and
no anchor (nothing scrolls), so it is six lines in `handleFullscreen` and one clause in the frame walk.
The strip's *solo* rule goes the other way and declines: a lone tile already is the region, so the record
would be one nothing reads — and, left standing across a switch, one tile covering a cascade with
nothing to lift it, which is why `setKind` lifts a `solo` shadow and `handleSetLayout` re-seeds the rule
on the way back.

**`move-window` reuses the strip's horizontal arm for all four directions.** On the singleton columns a
cascade is built from that *is* "swap with the neighbouring slot" and needs no new primitive. On a mixed
partition inherited from a strip arrangement it takes the pop-out arm, which converges the partition
toward singletons rather than away — acceptable, and the alternative (a new cross-column swap) would
have `stack` writing to columns, which §6.8 of the plan exists to prevent.

**Overlap is irreducible in exactly one place, and it is the cover rather than the layout.** Nothing in
`Stack`'s arithmetic reads a z-order and nothing asserts one: slots are the stored order, and there is
no promotion, no MRU and no reordering on focus. An earlier draft of the plan proposed exactly that, and
it solved a cosmetic problem by making `focus left` twice oscillate between two windows. What *does*
have to be right is the reconstruction, which must be pixel-identical to a desktop whose windows now
overlap — so `Engine.scopeUnion` orders a stack workspace's run by `StackOrder` instead of by placement
order. That is a **read** of the order emira already reconstructs from its own focus reports: no fence,
no confirmation round trip, no focus debt, which is the whole difference between this and pinning.
`State.stackingOrder` is the shared reader, and the guide's minimap uses it too.

Two latent sharp edges were fixed first, while there was still nothing tiled that overlapped and both
were provably no-ops. `World.window(at:)` broke a tie on the lowest id, which was arbitrary and
invisible only because tiled windows did not overlap; it now asks `StackOrder`, building it lazily so
the common one-candidate hit test still allocates nothing. And `Layout.allWindowIds` stopped claiming to
be a z-order — the claim moved to `scopeUnion`, which is now the one place a cover's layer order is
decided.

**The boot seed is the piece that carries risk, and it changes the strip too.** The core's only record
of stacking is the order focus reports arrive in, and a scan focuses nothing — so at launch every rank
tied at zero while macOS's own order was perfectly real. A scan now announces its snapshots **back to
front**, along the window list each `ScanAnswer` was already read against, so `World.focusedAt`
describes the desktop emira found. The list read is one the scan already pays for; asking
`source.windowList()` afresh would have been simpler but breaks the enumerator's standing claim to make
no list call of its own. Two visible consequences, both improvements on an arbitrary answer: the boot
focus lands on the frontmost window rather than on whichever bound last, and a strip's initial
left-to-right order is the desktop's depth rather than the enumerator's. Thirteen shell tests asserted
the old announcement order and were updated; one (`NativeTabTests`) was picking "Ghostty's window" by a
bundle id its own fixture stamps on both apps, and now picks by title.

**`layout.default` is held on `Workspaces`, not threaded through materialisation.** Six sites can be the
first to touch an address — `materialize`, `reconcile`, both initialisers and all three subscript
setters — and "an unmaterialized address answers as an empty workspace" has to stay true in one place,
because that is the whole reason nothing in the container branches on whether a workspace exists. It is
a seed and not a leash: a reload changes what the *next* address becomes and leaves every existing one
alone, which is the shape a window rule already has.

**The `strip` → `tiled` rename came first, and was the cheapest it will ever be.** `strip` meant two
things: the scrolling ribbon, and *on the tiled surface at all* as against floating or pinned. The
second stops being true the moment `strip` also names one layout of two — `stripWindowIds` would read as
"windows on strip-arranged workspaces" when it means every tiled window everywhere, and it feeds
`Workspaces.reconcile`, the single most load-bearing call in the system. Mechanical, wide, and proved by
an unchanged suite.

## Observations

**Two things only a running daemon can settle, and neither is done.** The daemon here has no
Accessibility grant, so it boots, binds nothing, and answers the socket — enough to prove the verb, the
config key and `dumpState` end to end, and nothing about pixels. What is outstanding is the cover's
fidelity at both ends of a transition over *overlapping* tiles, which is the claim `PRINCIPLES` §5 rests
on and which the suite can only assert the order of; and §5's soft failure, which wants looking at
rather than arguing about: slots 1–5, click window 2 then window 4, and window 3 is reduced to two
corner squares because both its neighbours are now drawn above it. Two keystrokes, not a corner case —
but placement is never wrong, nothing is unreachable, and focusing window 3 repairs it, which is why the
alternative (focusing every window in slot order after every edit — *n* activations, each a flash, each
stealing focus) is worse than the thing it prevents.

**Sizing by `n` means every arrival and departure resizes every window on the workspace**, and the
capture head is therefore `n` × ~25 ms and always cold. `SurfaceCache` cannot help: it matches a still on
size alone, captures run *before* the teleport, so the stills for an arrival at `n = 3` are filmed and
stored at S₃ and the teleport then resizes everything to S₄. The cached still is always exactly one
resize behind, because the edit that would use it is the edit that invalidates it. Not bad luck — a
structural property of sizing by `n`, and the answer is that the head is small rather than that
something hides it. Measure it against `frames-per-transition` before treating it as a problem; the
daemon already prints `145 ms capture head` on a strip.

**A workspace switched to `stack` and back keeps its columns and their widths but not its scroll**, since
the offset is normalised to `0` on the way in and the focused column is revealed on the way out. The
remembered offset lives in `WorkspaceState` and is only written by a *workspace* switch; carrying it
across a layout switch as well would be one line and was left out deliberately, because it is memory
nobody has asked for yet.

**`Stack`'s hard floor is unreachable in practice and untested as such.** The stagger compresses once a
tile would go under 480×320 and stops at the 8 pt identity floor; on a laptop those meet at roughly
`n = 80`, well past the point a cascade is navigable. Below that the tile size clamps and the deepest
tiles would hang past the region — total, and not worth designing for.

**The guide's minimap on a cascade is a 1:1 miniature with the viewport marker permanently full**, since
a cascade's extent *is* one working area. It still answers *what is where*, which is the half a cascade
needs most, and its tiles are drawn in the same `StackOrder` the cover uses. Whether it earns its space
there is a judgement for someone looking at it.
