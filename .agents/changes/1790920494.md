# A window opened over a veiled one is cut out of the veil as it lands

> Change `1790920494` — 2026-10-01

## Goal

With `unfocused-opacity` on, a window opened over a veiled column came up under that column's veil — frosted
wallpaper over the window about to take focus. Measured from a third process sampling the window list at
~1 kHz against the daemon's trace on `CLOCK_UPTIME_RAW`: a tiled arrival stood under the veil ~400 ms, until
its arrival's cover rose, and a floating one 3.3 s, until the reconciliation sweep, since adopting a float
changes no binding and so sends no set.

## Implementation

**Detection was never the problem.** The first `windowAppeared` (`AXMainWindowChanged` naming an unknown
window, `AXWindowCreated` a few ms behind it) reached the daemon 13–34 ms before the window server first listed
the window, across eight runs. Adoption is what is slow — ~190 ms, since the identity join matches frames within 2 pt and the open
animation grows the window ~20 pt, so both immediate scans miss and the 150 ms retry binds it — and nothing
re-cut the mask in between.

**`WorldWatcher.onWindowAnnounced` → `Scrims.expectArrival`.** Any `windowAppeared` arms a watch that reads the
stack once and then every `arrivalInterval` (120 Hz), cutting every veiling display against that one reading
(`paint` now takes a reading it is handed). It waits for the reading to change, then ends `arrivalQuiet`
(18 ≈ 150 ms) unchanged readings later, or after `arrivalLimit` (120) readings. The quiet is long because the
system's open animation overshoots its frame by 2 pt and holds there up to 115 ms before settling back, and a
shorter quiet (the settle watch's four at 60 Hz) ended before the settle and left a hole 2 pt off until the
sweep. Counts rather than wall time, so `ManualScheduler` can drive it. A separate watch from the per-display
settle watch rather than a mode of it: it is armed by no set, spans every display off one read, and waits for
a first change where the settle watch counts quiet from the start.

**The frontmost window is never a sheet** (`Scrims.veils`). A timely re-cut alone did nothing for an arrival
landing wholly on the veiled window: unnamed and wholly on a see-through window is exactly the sheet rule's
shape, so it was veiled on purpose (traced: a re-cut 14 ms after it reached the glass painted nothing). A
veiled window is one focus has left, and focus raises (`makeKey` and activation), so its sheet stands behind
whatever took focus; checked live, a sheet on a column focus then left stood second, behind the focused
column. Rejected: requiring the sheet's owner pid to match its window's — it would catch a cross-app arrival,
but a sandboxed app's save sheet is drawn by the open-and-save panel service, another process.

**A scan that names a window fans out `onStackChanged`.** A newcomer that is not frontmost — a window opened
behind the active app's — is still taken for a sheet until a scan names it, and for a float no set follows.

**The watch yields to the frame loop** (`Scrims.isPainting`, the reader reconciliation already takes). A
tiled arrival's watch runs on into its own transition, where `IMPLEMENTATION.md` records a list read at
450 ms; mid-frame readings are skipped and still spend the limit. On the harness that cut the reads during the
tiled arrival's transition from 27 to 7 (the set's own settle watch, untouched). It also stops the watch
following the open animation's settle-back on a tiled arrival, which lands in the capture head; the set at the
teleport cuts that.

Results on the same harness, four cases (tiled or floating, wholly on the veiled column or straddling it),
final build: the arrival is cut 2–12 ms after the window server first lists it — the tick phase plus one read —
against ~400 ms and 3.3 s before. The arrival transitions ran at 123 fps against 122–123 before, with the
capture head unchanged.

## Observations

- Zero frames is out of reach: the app's first frame and our mask are separate commits from separate
  processes, and no public API reports a window reaching the glass. What is left is the tick phase plus one
  commit, a frame or two at 120 Hz.
- Each reading is a `CGWindowListCopyWindowInfo` on the main thread, 0.6–1.6 ms median and up to 11 ms while
  the window server is busy with the newcomer; ~40–65 per arrival, and only while something is veiled.
- Measured with a test AppKit app only. Ghostty's announcement-to-glass gap is unmeasured: `osascript … new
  window` timed out behind an Automation prompt.
- Adoption takes ~190 ms for any window with the system open animation, for the join reason above. That delays
  every arrival's tiling, not just its veil.
- A floating arrival leaves the core's focus `nil`: its one focus report came before the scan named it, and
  `windowCreated` only notes an activation for a window off the strip. The column it came out of stays
  unveiled while the float has the keyboard.
- The arming read sometimes found the mask already out of date from the previous step of the harness (closing
  the last arrival and focusing back), and corrected it. Not chased.
