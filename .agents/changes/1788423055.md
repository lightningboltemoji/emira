# The cover casts macOS's shadow, and casts it past the menu bar

> Change `1788423055` — 2026-09-03

## Goal

Moving windows under a cover put a dark band above every top-row window and cut it off along a hard
horizontal line at the menu bar's lower edge. Three faults, measured on a white desktop with
`screencapture -x` (**not** `-o`, which strips system window shadows from a full-screen capture and is
how an earlier look concluded there was nothing to compare against):

**One shadow where macOS draws two.** Fitting a Gaussian to the system's own windows — `shadowRadius`
is σ in points and the fit residual is under a level of 255 on every edge, so a `CALayer` shadow *is* a
macOS shadow rather than an approximation of one:

| | opacity | σ | drop |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| macOS, key window | 0.71 | 20 pt | 17.4 pt |
| macOS, every other window | 0.46 | 8.0 pt | 6.5 pt |
| `Reconstruction`, either | 0.35 | 18 pt | 8 pt |

The synthesized one is the key window's blur at half its opacity and half its drop: too flat to read as
a drop shadow, and applied to every stand-in. Measured 8 pt above a tiled window's top edge — which with
`outer-gap-top = 8` is exactly the menu bar's lower edge — the desktop reads 0.068 under the focused
window and 0.014 under the rest, and the cover read 0.068 under both. Five times too dark on every
window the user is not looking at. That is the band.

**The cover stopped at the struts.** `Overlay` was inset by them and `masksToBounds`, so a stand-in's
shadow was clipped along the working area's top edge — and the band above it showed the *live* desktop,
where the real windows have already teleported to their destinations. So the shadow above the line
belonged to the layout the transition was heading for and the shadow below it to the stand-in mid-slide.
Measured across that line, one pixel row: 0.012 above against 0.068 below, a **step of 14–18 levels of
255 spanning 1400 px of width**. It is a fact about moving windows only, which is why it goes away when
you stop and could not be seen in a still.

**The pad clipped the shadow again, but only on smear frames.** `SmearLayer.shadowReach` was a constant
32 pt against a shadow reaching 8 + 3σ = 62 pt, and `CIMotionBlur` output is clipped to the layer
carrying it — a second hard edge, appearing and vanishing with the blur.

## Implementation

**`WindowShadow` (EmiraGuide) is the one reader for both shadows.** It lands there because both hosts
that stand in for a window need it: `Reconstruction` and the settings mock's `PaneLayer`, which had been
carrying its own copy of the old numbers under a comment saying they were the compositor's. `reach`
(3σ + drop) is stated once and `SmearLayer.margin` is derived from `maxReach` instead of the 32.

**`LayerBinding` gained `isFocused`, and the session holds the focus it was minted under.** Which of the
two shadows a stand-in casts is truth the core owns and the shell must not keep a second copy of. The
alternative — the shell reading focus for itself at raise time — is the shadow copy of the core's intent
that `1785545538` argues against. `TransitionSession.focusedWindow` is set at `raiseCover` rather than
read per binding, so a window a retarget sweeps into a raised cover joins one that has already made up
its mind: one cover, one answer. It is the focus the stills were filmed under, which is the state the
cover has to match; where it ends up is what the closing cross-fade is for.

**The cover is the display, not the working area.** The strut inset existed because the base capture
carries its own menu bar and it would double with the live one at `.mainMenu`. That is answered at the
source instead: `SurfaceCapturer` drops every window whose `SCWindow.windowLayer` is above
`Overlay.level` from the base, so there is nothing in it to double with, and the cover can be opaque
over the whole display while the real menu bar and Dock composite on top — over a stand-in's shadow
exactly as they composite over a real window's. Verified rather than assumed: SCK reports the menu bar
at layer 24 and excluding it does remove it from the image.

Two alternatives were rejected. Leaving the cover inset and softening the clip with a gradient mask
fixes the seam and not the band, and matches nothing — a real window's shadow reaches the top of the
screen through the transparent menu bar, still at 0.038 six points above the line. Covering the display
but leaving the chrome band transparent so the base needs no change is worse than the status quo: the
band would then carry the live shadow *and* the synthesized one, doubling wherever they agree and
pulsing as the cover fades.

The change deletes a latent bug on the way past: anything above the cover's level that was inside the
working area — a notification banner, a Control Center popover — used to be baked into the base *and*
composited live on top of it.

## Observations

Measured after, on the same desktop: the step across the old cover edge is 0.5–2.5 levels of 255, which
is the gradient of a continuous shadow across one point rather than a discontinuity; the band above an
unfocused stand-in reads 0.016 against the desktop's 0.014; menu-bar text weight under a raised cover is
within two levels of the uncovered desktop, so nothing is doubling. Transitions run at 63–65 fps, as
before.

The pad is wider — margin 128 → 173.5 pt — so a smear frame's offscreen pass grows by about 16% in area
for a full-height window. Not measured against a frame budget; nothing in the transition timings moved.

Both shadows were fitted on one display, in light appearance, against a white desktop. macOS is not
known to vary them by appearance or display, and nothing here re-measures at runtime — a system that
changed them would need the constants changed with it. The fit is a single Gaussian, which is what
`CALayer` casts; the largest residual in both is at the first sample outside the silhouette, where the
window's own edge antialiasing is in the measurement.

`WindowShadow.unfocused` stands in for every window that is not focused, which is what macOS draws —
but macOS also has a third state the fit does not cover, the window of an *inactive application* versus
an unfocused window of the active one. Both were measured as the same shadow here; if they differ, they
differ below the level the measurement resolves.
