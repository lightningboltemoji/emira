# Parking is the desktop's corner, not each display's

> Change `1787279726` — 2026-08-20

## Goal

A parked window is a nub at the bottom-right corner of its lot and a whole window hanging off that
corner, to the right and down. The lot was the working area of the display holding the strip, which is
correct for exactly one arrangement: the one where nothing is beyond that corner. Put a second display
to the right of the first and every parked window's body lands on it — a full window sitting in view,
overlapping whatever is tiled there, for as long as its column is scrolled off. The same happens with a
display directly below, and even with one whose right edge merely lines up with the lot's, which catches
a sliver-wide line of window down its whole height.

`PRINCIPLES` §5 licenses parking on the claim that the body leaves the screen. On a desktop of several
displays that claim is about the *arrangement*, and only one corner of it can make good on it.

## Implementation

**One `ParkingLot` for the whole desktop**, chosen from the display arrangement by
`ParkingLot.init(among:)` and carried in `LayoutMetrics.parkingLot`, so every display's metrics hand out
the same corner. `Layout` no longer builds a lot from `workingArea`; it reads the one it is given, which
is what stops a call site forming a second opinion about where a parked window goes.

**The clearance test is the body's own reach, not a heuristic.** `clears(_:)` asks whether a display ends
left of the nub's left edge (`frame.maxX - visibleSliver`, not `maxX` — a display below with the same
right edge is otherwise missed, and it is the one that catches the sliver-wide line) or lies entirely
above the lot's top edge (`frame.minY`, because the tallest row in a lane starts there and a body only
ever hangs *down*, so a screen above cannot be caught however far right it sits). Both halves are exact
rather than padded. A display the candidate *covers* is not an obstacle: mirrored displays report the
same frame, so their pixels are the candidate's own — which also disposes of the candidate itself.

**The rank is clearance, then main, then furthest bottom-right, then enumeration order.** Only the first
rung is a requirement. Main is there because a nub is a grab handle: §4 already says which screen is the
user's is macOS's to name, and among corners that all work, theirs is the one to put the handles on. The
last two rungs make the answer total and stable — including for an arrangement where no display is clear,
which is answered rather than trapped. That case looks unreachable (intrusion is nearly a strict order on
disjoint rectangles, so a maximal element exists), but nothing here depends on proving it.

**A learned park floor is measured from the lot's bottom edge** (`Engine.handleParkCorrected`), not the
window's own display. A window on the left screen parked at the right screen's corner answered 56 pt of
chrome as −44 against its own working area, and the slot the app just refused would be issued again.

Two alternatives were dropped:

- **A lot per display, mirroring to the bottom-left when the bottom-right is blocked.** It keeps nubs on
  the screen their strip is on, which is the one thing the desktop-wide lot gives up. But a display
  sandwiched between two others has no clear corner of its own in either direction, so it needs the
  desktop's answer anyway — two mechanisms where one does the whole job, and a second geometry (a body
  hanging off to the *left*) to keep true everywhere the first one is.
- **The corner of the union of every display frame.** Usually on no display at all, so the position is
  not honoured and macOS clamps the window back into view — the failure the sliver exists to avoid.

The cost is stated plainly: on a multi-display desktop every nub is at one screen's corner, so rescuing a
parked window by hand may mean reaching for a different screen than the strip is on. The alternative is a
window standing in view on that screen, which is worse in kind, not just in degree.

With one display attached the lot is that display's working area and nothing changes — the existing suite
passes untouched, including every park, workspace and monitor case.

## Observations

**Not yet confirmed against real hardware.** One display is attached here, so the two-display cases are
unit tests over synthetic arrangements (`DesktopParkingTests`), including the join through
`State.metrics(of:)`. Worth docking a real second display and checking that a scrolled-off column leaves
a nub at the far corner and nothing at all on the screen next door.

**A nub can now appear on a display with no cover up.** A park writes under the cover of the display
holding the strip; its nub blinks in or out at the parking display's corner, which may be another screen
mid-nothing. D8's gate is unchanged and was not widened to the parking display: what shows there is at
most a few points wide at the very corner. If it turns out to read as a flicker, the gate is the place to
fix it.

**The lane step is not in the clearance test.** Lane *n* pokes `n × laneStep` further in, so a body's
left edge can sit a few points left of `frame.maxX - visibleSliver`. A display could only be missed by
that difference if its right edge were within a few points of the lot's, which for disjoint displays
means it is above or below — and the vertical half of the test already answers both.

**A Dock on the parking display's left or right edge still gets crossed.** The lot is the working area so
the nub stays grabbable, which leaves the body running out through the Dock's band, where the Dock draws
over it but does not cover the band's full height. That predates this change and is unrelated to the
arrangement; the fix, if it is wanted, is a choice about which of the two the nub owes more to.
