# A picture is of a window, and a whole film has a shelf life

> Change `1793164258` — 2026-10-27

## Goal

"A wrong screenshot can live much longer than it should… we used a screenshot of the main zoom window, even
after I'd moved focus around several times." Not reproduced as such on this machine (Zoom paints off-display
regions here, as `1790052991` also found), but two ways for exactly that to happen were found.

**An id handed to another window kept that window's pictures.** `WindowRegistry.rebind` re-points a
`WindowId` at a successor standing on the departed window's rectangle — native tabs — and nothing that holds a
picture by id was told: `SurfaceCache`'s photograph (the cover's stand-in under `immediate`), the hoist's,
the peek's. Zoom makes the rectangle match: its second meeting window of a session opened at exactly the frame
emira had tiled the first one to, and it pre-creates and swaps windows around a meeting. Any destroy-and-create
at one frame inside one scan is a succession, and the home window's photograph then stands in for the
meeting window — every transition, since the store keeps a film at its size until a better one arrives.

**A whole film held over newer holed ones, for good.** `1790052991` keeps a whole film over a newer one with a
hole (an app that only paints what is on the display — reported for Zoom and Chrome on the primary machine)
and named the cost in its observations: a window never seen whole again keeps its last whole film
indefinitely. Under `immediate` a head film often lands after its cover's teleport, so "seen whole again" is
rarer than the bounce argument assumes.

## Implementation

`WindowRegistry.onRebound` fires when `rebind` points an id at a different number; the daemon drops the id's
photograph (`CaptureService.forgetStill` → `SurfaceCache.drop`) and has the hoist and peek planes film again
(`forgetPicture`). The corner reading stays: it is the chrome's, and is read again anyway. It is a push from
the one place a binding is re-pointed rather than a number check at every read, because there is one writer
and three readers.

A whole film outranks a newer holed one only while it is younger than `SurfaceCache.wholeShelfLife` (5 s),
with the clock injected for the tests. A first cut bounded it by count — "outranks one holed film, not two" —
and a probe that paints only its on-display part (`Painter`, a counter changing every 100 ms) broke it at
once: one transition can produce two holed films, its late head film and the next. A first cut also re-filmed
an outranked film at rest from `keep`; with the shelf life it buys too little to be worth a capture.

## Observations

With the shelf life, returning to the probe's column after 8 s cross-fades mid-transition to the film its head
took (`captureRefreshed` at +336 ms). The trade is the one `1790052991` weighed the other way: past 5 s, a
column that only ever arrives hanging off the edge slides in with a band of nothing rather than with pixels
from before — the lesser lie for a call, a greater one for a static page. The succession path is pinned in the
suite but was not provoked live: Zoom here never destroyed and created on one rectangle inside one scan.
