# A size drawn by hand is a share of the screen it was drawn on

> Change `1787624031` — 2026-08-24

## Goal

`adoptDraggedSize` recorded what the hand drew in points — `widthOverride = .fixed(620)`,
`heightOverride = .fixed(500)` — and points do not follow a display. A window shortened by hand and then
drawn back to the full height its screen offers was pinned at that screen's height, so moving the
workspace to a taller one left it short by the difference; the same on the other axis, and the same
through an unplug or a resolution change. On a 1000×800 display sent to 1600×1200: a full-height drag
stayed 800 tall, a full-width drag stayed 1000 wide, and a stack whose divider had been drawn at 600/800
arrived as 600/600 with the shares destroyed.

The user-facing reading is a snap: draw a window back to full height and it should *be* full height from
then on. The implementation reading is narrower — one rung of the size stack was in the wrong unit.

## Implementation

**The intent is stored as a proportion of the extent it was drawn against.** `Extent.proportion(of:)`
already existed for precisely this, and `grow 50%` already used it; the drag simply inherited `.fixed`
from the change that introduced it, which recorded no argument for the unit. A hand names a size by
drawing a rectangle on the screen in front of it, and the same rectangle on another display is the same
share of it.

**Snapping needs no threshold, because there is nothing to recognize.** Both adoptions were already
clamped to their extent — `adoptWidth` at `max(available, from)`, `adoptHeight` at the room its
stackmates leave — so a drag at *or past* an edge already landed exactly on the extent. Run through the
inverse that is exactly `.proportion(1.0)`, gaps and all. A tolerance test for "near enough to full" was
therefore rejected: it would be a second, fuzzier opinion about a number the clamp already lands on the
nose.

**A stack needs nothing beyond this.** The auto rung divides the column's leftover and so has always
tracked the display; the pinned override was the one rung that did not. Pinning the auto siblings to
their computed shares as well was rejected — it freezes them against the `SizeCorrection` re-division
that is the whole point of `auto`, and buys nothing a proportional override does not already give.

**`grow 100px` still leaves points.** The typed-unit rule is untouched: the user asked for a point count
there and gets one. Only the drag, which carries no unit of its own, is read as a share.

`LayoutMetrics.heightExtent` is the vertical twin of `widthExtent`, so the caller recording a height
intent and `Column` resolving it cannot disagree about the span. `Extent.proportion(of:)` now answers
`nil` at a zero extent rather than dividing by zero, which also retires `handleResizeColumn`'s
`case .percent where available > 0` — the guard was the inverse's own all along.

## Observations

The trade is real and accepted: a window sized by hand to an exact point count — a terminal drawn to fit
80 columns — no longer keeps that count across displays. `cycle-width` already discarded it, and an app's
real floor still comes back through `SizeCorrection`, so what is lost is the coincidence rather than the
constraint.

Not reachable by the suite, and wanting a hand on a mouse with two displays attached: whether a drag that
*feels* like it reached the edge actually lands on the clamp. The adoption reads the frame the app came
to rest at, so an app that quantizes its height — a terminal on a row grid — stops a few points short and
records 0.99 rather than 1.0. The clamp cannot see the difference between that and a deliberate 0.99, and
inventing a snap band to cover it is the tolerance rejected above; if it bites, the place to fix it is the
`SizeCorrection` round trip, which already knows the window refused.

The four cross-display cases were each checked to fail with the two adoption lines reverted to `.fixed`,
on their geometry assertions and not only on the stored spelling.
