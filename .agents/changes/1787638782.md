# One photograph store

> Change `1787638782` — 2026-08-24

## Goal

Two containers held one relation. `CaptureService.surfaces` held a window's still for as long as its
cover; `SurfaceCache.entries` held a reduced copy afterwards. A still crossed from the first to the
second at teardown, through `clearStore` → `keep` — a `Task.detached(priority: .utility)` plus a hop back
to the main actor — and the head of a batch does that teardown four lines before it reads the cache for
its stand-ins. Under `CoverMode.immediate` a keypress landing inside the outgoing 0.22 s cross-fade
therefore made the head batch's *own* release the thing that freed the outgoing stills, after it had
already read the cache: the cover raised over the film before the last one, deterministically, for as
long as presses kept landing inside the fade. On an alternating `focus left`/`focus right` the only
difference between consecutive films is active-window chrome, so it reads as the wrong window looking
focused.

Three further defects shared the root — a photograph's identity tied to a cover's lifecycle rather than
to the moment it was taken. A piece arriving after `finish` removed its `Pending` was dropped; so was one
whose cover generation had been superseded; and `install(_:for:on:fresh:)` never removed from
`freshlyCaptured` on `fresh: false`, so a window held at capture resolution by one display's cover and
stood in by another's was released as though the stand-in were a fresh capture and re-reduced —
`400x300 → 100x75 → 25x19 → 6x5` over four transitions, with the live cover's own full-res still
overwritten by the reduced one on the way past.

## Implementation

**One relation, keyed by window: the most recent photograph of it, and what is known about that
photograph.** `SurfaceCache` becomes `[WindowId: Photo]`, where a `Photo` is the surface, the batch that
took it, the set of live covers entitled to paint it, and whether it has already been reduced. The plan
named the pins as a second dictionary; they are a field, because two further relations — *when was this
filmed* and *is this already at cache resolution* — have to live somewhere too, and four dictionaries
over one key is the shape this change exists to remove. `CaptureService` loses `surfaces`, `owners`,
`freshlyCaptured`, `install` and `keep`. `baseImages` stays: a base is a photograph of a screen, not of a
window, and has no second lifetime to reconcile.

**The record is unconditional and precedes both guards.** A piece that arrives after its batch stopped
owing acks, or after its cover was superseded, is a photograph that was paid for; both guards are about
the entitlement to *paint* pixels, which is not a reason to forget them. A record no live cover pins is
stored reduced rather than at capture resolution — the reduction is the disclosure as much as the budget,
and a superseded batch's arrival is exactly the pixels a later stand-in would raise.

**Preceding the guards makes arrival order the write order, and arrival order is not film order.** A
batch that runs long reaches `receive` after the batch that superseded it, so an unguarded write lets an
older film replace the newer one under a live cover: `Reconstruction.addLayers` reads the store *at* the
raise, and a head batch still collecting pieces builds that window's layer from a film of it at a size
the transition has already left behind. Nothing downstream repairs that — `refreshed` and
`Event.captureRefreshed` are both gated on `isCurrent`, which the slow batch fails, and its successor has
already spent that window's ack. So a photograph carries its batch, and `record` settles entitlement and
pixels separately: the entitlement unconditional, holding over whichever film is the best one on hand;
the pixels taken only where they are newer. An overtaken piece still records its pin, and gets a better
photograph to paint than the one it arrived with — which is what a guard over the pins could not say.

Keying the order off the entitlement instead — *an unentitled arrival may not replace a pinned
photograph* — is a proxy for it (entitled implies current implies newer) and fails both ways: it lets
two superseded batches land out of order with no cover watching, and it refuses the cross-display film
in Observations, which is genuinely fresher and wanted. The batch is the mint and the moment the piece
landed is not; stamping in `receive` would record the inversion itself. No shutter time was available to
prefer — `SCScreenshotManager.captureImage` answers with a bare `CGImage`, and `SCStreamFrameInfo`
`.displayTime` belongs to the stream path this does not use — and the batch orders soundly anyway,
because the window server serializes screenshots. Finer resolution would buy nothing: every piece within
one batch is a different window. The counter is `CaptureService`'s and pre-incremented, so anything
seeded into the cache from outside it mints below 1, or a stand-in ties with its own refresh and is
never overtaken by it (`aStoodInWindowsOwnStillArrivesAsARefresh`).

**`isReduced` is a property of a photograph, not of a cover.** That is what `freshlyCaptured` was
reaching for and could not express: a stand-in pins a photograph that is *already* reduced, and demoting
it a second time is the dissolve. Tracking it beside the pixels makes the double reduction
unrepresentable rather than merely avoided; the suite pins it (`aPhotographStoodInAndReleasedAgainIsNot
ReducedTwice`), and that test fails at 3 px with the flag ignored.

**The reduction moves onto the hot path, and that is what makes the fix work at the site the bug lives
at.** At this display's geometry a full-screen 2× still reduces in 0.13 ms and a 6-window scope in
0.62 ms serial; against the measured capture model (`1785301362`) that is 0.73% of a 6-window batch and
1.9% at the head of an `immediate` transition. The detachment bought 0.6 ms and paid for it with the
ordering of the read. Running the daemon over an alternating focus at 120 ms — inside the fade — the gate
lands on the base and nothing else: 43–57 ms with 3/3 standing, against 145 ms cold.

**The byte budget is over the unpinned set.** Counting a live cover's own would put a 6-window scope at
90 MB against a 48 MB budget and evict every stand-in on the desk to make room for pixels that are
already on screen and coming down with their cover regardless. Pinned photographs are skipped by
eviction and cost the budget nothing, which is also why `removeAll` keeps them — the name is about what
the store is free to drop, and `Event.screensChanged` takes the live transitions down itself.

**`keepsStills` moves from the service to the cache.** It decides whether a photograph outlives the cover
that took it, which is a retention policy over the store; with one store there is no second place for it,
and the daemon already reaches `surfaceCache` on the adjacent line. `CaptureStore.surface(for:)` stays
entitlement-gated behind `pinnedSurface(for:)`: `Reconstruction.addLayers` builds a layer for every
window the store answers for, so an ungated read would paint an unmatched photograph where the base
should show through. `CaptureReport.standing` derives from a `refreshed` set on `Pending` now that
`freshlyCaptured` is gone — a `standing` of zero against a high `stoodIn` is the signature of the mode
buying nothing, and it is the only instrument that can see it.

## Observations

The cache holds the film taken at the *head* of the previous transition, and the focus change lands after
that film. This takes the alternating-focus case from two films stale to one: the window still shows the
chrome it had before the last press. Closing that needs a re-film once a transition settles, which
`1785306827` names as the honest next step.

Nothing here can assert which film a real cover raises over — the suite drives the seam with synthetic
pixels, and the daemon's instrument says the mechanism runs, not that the pixels are the right ones. The
two orderings converging is what the round-trip test pins; the visual half is still read by eye.

A photograph recorded while another display's cover pins it stays at capture resolution, so a later
stand-in can raise over full-res pixels. Intended rather than tolerated: they are the freshest film of
that window, and the disclosure the reduction buys is about staleness.

`pinnedSurface(for:)` is ungated on size, and what makes that safe is the entitlement rather than the
match — a cover is pinned to the window it asked for. It is not safe where the two come apart: a
size-matched stand-in from an older batch, overwritten by a superseded batch's *later* film of the same
window at its pre-transition size, reaches the raise and is fitted to the geometry being placed. Probed
against the service, not inferred — `800×600` before that arrival and `123×45` after. Closing it means
the pin carrying the size it was granted at, rule 2 applied to the entitlement rather than to the match,
and it is a product decision as much as a mechanical one: a cover with no matching photograph paints
nothing and lets the base through, where today it paints something wrong.
