# A fast frame is smeared, not strobed

> Change `1788398408` — 2026-09-02

## Goal

At 120 Hz the springs read as motion. At 60 Hz the fast part of a transition — the first few frames,
where a critically damped `k = 800` spring from rest is at its peak — reads as a strobe: a run of crisp
stills, each a long way from the last, the "24 fps film" feel. It is not pacing; the frame clock was
measured at the refresh rate. It is arithmetic. The spring's peak speed is `D·ω/e ≈ 10.4·D` per second,
reached `1/ω ≈ 35 ms` in, so the peak step per frame is:

| travel                   | at 60 Hz | at 120 Hz |
| ------------------------ | -------- | --------- |
| 300 pt                   | 52 pt    | 26 pt     |
| 600 pt column            | 104 pt   | 52 pt     |
| 1000 pt workspace slide  | 173 pt   | 87 pt     |

A frame is one still held for a refresh, and the display cannot be asked for more of them. What a
single frame *contains* is the only lever left, and film's answer to the same problem is the shutter:
each frame integrates the motion over a fraction of the interval, so a fast object is a smear rather
than a jump. Games call it per-object motion blur; hand-drawn animation calls it a smear frame.

It began as a spike behind constants, judged by eye at 60 Hz, and stayed. What landed is two settings:

```toml
[animation]
motion-blur = 0.5           # the share of each frame's motion smeared; a 180° film shutter, 0 is off
motion-blur-attack = 0.05   # seconds the smear takes to build; it clears at once
```

Under `[animation]` rather than a section of their own: everything there is what a transition looks
like, and "display" in this project means a monitor. Two keys of the table rather than an
`[animation.motion-blur]` sub-table, though that was the first draft and it matches the springs: the
settings window splits a section whose *main surface* spans sub-tables into sub-tabs (`ControlSlab`),
and a sub-tab holding one slider is a place to go for nothing. The springs escape that rule by being
advanced, and the blur's strength is the one number here a user is meant to reach for. The value is a
shutter fraction rather than a "strength" for `stiffness`'s reason — it names a defined quantity, so
`0.5` and `1` mean something before the comment is read — and the key is the plain noun because
`motion-blur = 0` is how one turns it off.

## Implementation

**The smear is the shell's, derived from consecutive frames.** The core emits the same
`setLayerFrame` stream it always has; `SmearLayer` differences this frame's top-left against the last
one it placed, and that step is what the shutter integrates. No velocity crosses the plane boundary and
no reducer path changes — the geometry stays the core's, the look the shell's. The top-left rather than
the origin or the centre, because a resize anchored there is not a step: only the columns it pushes
smear, which is right.

**Three pieces, in the modules their dependencies allow.** The two numbers and the envelope that turns
a step into a σ are `MotionBlur` and `Smear` in `EmiraMotion`, pure and tested there, beside
`SpringParams` for the same reason `Config` can carry them. The layer plumbing — the pad, the filter,
the key-path writes — is `SmearLayer` in `EmiraGuide`, the module whose reason to exist is a layer tree
the daemon and the settings window host *as the same object*: `Reconstruction` wraps each stand-in's
`root` in one, and `PaneLayer` wraps each mock pane's shadow layer in one at the mock's scale, so the
settings window previews the two keys through the mechanism itself. `ConfigSchemaTests` and
`CatalogTests` force that shape: every field of `Config` needs a key, and every key needs a picture
or a written reason it has none, of which the catalog allows one. The layer's plumbing is tested
off-screen in `EmiraGuideTests`: a `CALayer` answers whether the filter is on, what it was asked for
through the key path, where the pad sits and which placements are origins — everything but the look.

**`CIMotionBlur` on a padding layer, updated through the key path.** The filter sits on a pad
`SmearLayer.margin` wider than the content on every side, because a layer filter's output is clipped to
the bounds of the layer carrying it — a smear on the window's own layer would be cut at its edge, which
is exactly where it needs to draw. Its inputs are written per frame with
`setValue(_:forKeyPath: "filters.smear.inputRadius")` inside the frame's transaction; mutating an
installed `CIFilter` directly is undefined.

**Installed while moving, removed at rest.** Below either threshold the filter comes off entirely rather
than staying at radius zero: a layer carrying any filter renders through an offscreen pass at a cost
that does not shrink with the radius, and nothing guarantees a Core Image pass is bit-identical at
zero, while the still at rest must be — the cross-fade that ends a transition is pixel-against-pixel.

**Two thresholds, one per question.** `Smear.minStep` asks whether there is a strobe to fix: under
eight points a frame the stills are close enough to read as motion. `Smear.minSigma` asks whether
the smear would draw anything: a σ under half a point softens an edge, and pays the whole offscreen
pass for it, which is what a low shutter over a fast step would otherwise buy. At the default shutter
the step threshold is the one that binds; under about a fifth, the σ one is.

**The mock does not smear a snap.** `renderNow(settle:)` teleports the panes, and Reduce Motion makes
every render one; a teleport read as a step would be one frame of maximal smear saying the window
moved at a thousand points a frame. Those renders pass `MotionBlur.off`, which records the new
position as the origin of the next step and draws nothing.

**The mock renders a frame once.** A hover or an edit retargets the panes, and under a running clock
`renderNow` leaves the drawing to the tick: a second render of the same frame is a step of nothing,
which the layer reads as σ to zero and the filter off, with the attack from the top on the next tick —
one crisp frame in the middle of a smeared run, which is the strobe frame. It draws itself only for a
snap, or when the clock is stopped and no tick is coming. The daemon needs no such rule: the executor
places each layer once per run, and a run is a frame.

**Two slider rules moved for the attack.** A slider snapped to tenths cannot reach `0.05`, so under
one it snaps to hundredths; and a seconds slider was never narrower than two seconds, which for a
default of `0.05` is a handle with the setting in its first pixel, so the floor of that span is half a
second. Both are presentation, and both hold for every other seconds key at least as well.

**What the filter actually is, measured rather than assumed.** A headless `CARenderer` — the engine
the window server runs — over the reconstruction's own layer tree, read back after the GPU had
finished:

| asked                                          | answer                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| shape                                          | Gaussian along the angle; a step edge's 10–90 % ramp is `2.56·r` |
| unit of `inputRadius`                          | **layer points** — the ramp doubles at 2× — so no backing-scale factor |
| does the sign of the angle matter              | no; `0` and `π` render identically                            |
| is the output shifted                          | no; the window's 50 % edges do not move at any angle          |
| is it clipped to the filtered layer's bounds   | yes                                                          |
| cost, 1670×1045 pt at 2×, per frame            | 3–5 ms of GPU, **flat in the radius** (a fixed tap count), linear in area |
| does it accumulate across frames               | no — an early reading that said so was a read-back race in the harness |
| a filtered stand-in wholly outside the host    | culled, filter or none: 0.45 ms against 0.43 ms unfiltered            |
| the pad's area                                 | nothing: `128` and `3σ + 32` render in the same time at every σ from 2 to 32 |
| the radius, again                              | nearly flat: σ 2 → 32 is 2.7 → 3.1 ms on a 1200×900 window over 1.1 ms unfiltered |
| a full strip, three 520×980 columns            | +3.8 ms for the three filters, over 1.9 ms unfiltered                  |

The last four rows are a second harness, an M3 at 2×, timing `CARenderer` frames to GPU completion.

So `inputRadius` is σ in points, and the calibration is variance: a shutter integrates evenly over a
box of length `L = shutter · step`, whose σ is `L/√12`, hence `Smear.sigmaPerPoint`. A ground-truth
render of the same step as sixteen "multiples" at `1, ½, ⅓ … ` alpha agreed on the ramp width at
`r ≈ 0.28·L`.

**The capture rect is not a step.** A layer is built at the rect its still was filmed at, so the raise
is pixel-identical — and for a parked window that rect is its nub in a corner, from which the core's
first placement teleports it to the strip in the raise's own transaction. Read as a step, that teleport
smeared the window vertically at full strength on the raise frame, at the screen edge where it was
entering — visible as a vertical smear during a horizontal scroll, exactly when windows were being
unparked. So the capture placement is followed by `forget()`; the first placement the core makes is
where a layer's steps begin.

**An attack on the way up, none on the way down.** A critically damped spring from rest is at half
speed after one 60 Hz frame and at peak after two — the smear tracking the step exactly arrived as a
switch, and read as one. So the σ on screen follows the step's σ through a first-order lag,
`attack` to nine tenths, and follows a *fall* at once, so the settle stays as crisp as the still.
With the default 50 ms and a 600 pt scroll the smear is at roughly a quarter, two thirds, and
five sixths of full over the first three frames rather than a half, then all of it.

**A pause is not a long frame.** The layer measures the interval since it last placed the content,
and the engine emits no frames for a settled display — so a stand-in that settled under a cover held
for an AX landing is placed again only when the next scroll extends that cover, after a gap of up to
`hold-timeout`. Read as one long frame, that gap put the first frame of the second scroll at its full
σ: the switch again, on the one path that had skipped the attack. So the lag runs over at most
`Smear.longestFrame`, a 60 Hz frame; a gap longer than that is a pause, and the frame that ends it is
the first frame from rest. At 60 and 120 Hz the clamp never binds on a real frame; a 30 Hz display
builds its smear over twice the frames.

**Rejected: the multiples themselves as the mechanism.** N copies of one still at sub-frame offsets
is pure compositor fill and would cost nothing the filter's offscreen pass costs. It ghosts: at the
sample counts a scroll's worth of layers could afford, high-contrast text reads as a comb rather than a
smear. Kept as the ground truth it is, not the look.

**Rejected: a cull by visibility, and a pad sized to the σ.** Both were the levers the cost at
120 Hz first pointed at, and both measure as nothing (above): Core Animation does not render a layer
outside its clipping ancestor, filter or no filter, and the filter's cost is per layer and the
content's area rather than the pad's. So the margin stays one constant, derived from the σ cap it
exists for, and the thresholds are the lever.

**Rejected: rendering the smeared still ourselves.** A per-frame Core Image pass in our own process
puts GPU work on the frame's critical path — the one way to manufacture the dropped frames this is
meant to hide — and `PRINCIPLES.md` §5 says where smoothness work goes. The filter runs in the window
server's frame instead, which is the cost worth knowing (below).

## Observations

**The cost is the window server's, not ours.** 3–5 ms per screenful of smeared layer area per frame is
comfortable at 60 Hz and tight at 120 — where the step halves and the smear with it, but not the
filter's flat cost. Whether a fast scroll of a full strip at 120 Hz holds its frame rate is the first
thing to look at; if it does not, the thresholds are the lever — `Smear.minStep` and `Smear.minSigma`,
since below either the filter is off and the offscreen pass with it — because the two that looked
cheaper were measured and buy nothing.

**Judged by eye at 60 Hz, after a headless start.** The screen was locked while the mechanism was
built, so it was validated through `CARenderer` first and looked at afterwards. The verdict was that it
is the enhancement it was meant to be, with two faults — the vertical smear on unparked windows and an
onset that read as a switch, both above — and the defaults are the two numbers that were looked at.
The settings window's preview of them was not looked at on a display: the mock's pane goes through the
same `SmearLayer`, so what is unverified is the look at mock scale, not the mechanism. Two more paths
carry large steps and were not looked at either: a trackpad glide under the hand, whose steps are as
uneven as the finger and whose falls are followed at once, so the σ may shimmer; and the mock's
camera, whose travel moves every pane's top-left and so smears them — physically right, unverified.

**Resizes are not smeared.** A resize is scale motion, and this filter is translation; the analogous
filter is `CIZoomBlur`, and both `.crop`'s clip and `.stretch`'s stretched still complicate it. A
column pushed by a resize smears as any translation does.

**The shadow smears with the window**, since it is inside the pad. That is physically right and was
not separately checked for look.

**The units question was worth measuring.** The first draft multiplied by the backing scale, on the
reasonable guess that the render server filters pixels. It filters points. Without the harness the
smear would have been twice too long on every Retina display and correct on none of the user's.
