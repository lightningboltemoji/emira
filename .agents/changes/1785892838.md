# Reconciliation holds three invariants

> Change `1785892838` — 2026-08-04

## Goal

Two failures that looked unrelated and were one. `reconcile` only ever *discovered* windows, so a window
that closed without its `AXUIElementDestroyed` reaching us — a wedged app, an app we never managed to
observe — kept its column for the life of the daemon. And `ensureWatching` gave up after three attempts,
so an app too busy to accept an observer at launch was deaf to us from then on: no window creation, no
destroy, no move.

Both were confirmed against the stubs before anything was written. The second is the sharper one, because
of how it ends: a fully bound desktop leaves reconciliation with nothing to complain about, so twenty
ticks and twenty window-list reads all report a healthy world while emira hears nothing that happens on
it.

## Implementation

The diagnosis is that the retry budget was attached to the wrong noun. `maxScanAttempts` is right for what
it was built for — "asked too early" is a race that resolves in frames, and after 450 ms it is not a race
any more. `ensureWatching` borrowed it on the stated grounds that a failed registration is "the same
asked-too-early race", and that is the error: a failed *bind* is a race, because the window list catches up
by itself, while a failed *registration* is a state that nothing catches up on. Budgets are for races.
States need a level check, and there was nowhere to put one — `reconcile` checked one direction of one of
three invariants, so the other two had grown ad-hoc bounded retries in the edge plane, which must
terminate and therefore must eventually give up.

So `reconcile` now makes all three true: every app observed, every managed window still real, every
on-screen window managed. `ensureWatching` splits, with `register` doing the registration alone;
`reconcile` calls that and nothing else, deliberately not the retry chain, which would answer a
permanently unobservable app with three full scans of it every interval — the cost `maxReconcileRounds`
exists to keep off the lanes. The budget is left in front of it unchanged, now bounding the hurry rather
than the repair. `maxReconcileRounds` gets its stated meaning back as a side effect: it was also, silently,
the cap on observer repair, since reconciliation's stray path was the only surviving caller of
`ensureWatching`.

A repair that repeats forever cannot afford the failure path the budget could. `ObservationSource`'s
`unwatch(app:)` was one verb for two facts, and a refused registration reached it: the observer went, and
the app's AX lane went with it. At three attempts clustered at a launch, before the app has a managed
window, that is invisible; every interval for the life of the process, against an app the write path is
placing windows on, it is the one thing `AXClient` exists to prevent. A dropped lane does not pause —
`perform` captures the lane it dispatched onto, so queued work runs on while the next call mints a second
serial queue beside it, and two sets race into one window out of order. `dropObserver` is the observer half
alone and is what a refusal takes; the lane is now dropped for exactly one reason, which is the process
being gone.

Rejected: a seam under `AXObservers` to hold that down in the suite. `AXClient` is a concrete dependency and
the observer path is the one place in the AX layer with no fake behind it — worth fixing on its own terms
rather than to hold a two-line split.

Removal rests on the window server, which owns the window rather than the app does and so cannot be
starved by one. That makes absence from `CGWindowListCopyWindowInfo` stronger evidence than anything the
AX paths hold, where silence has a second reading and every removal is corroborated accordingly. Two edges
make it safe, and the first is probably why this looked unfixable: the predicate is absence from the list
*entirely*, never `isOnScreen`. A sample desktop answered 2 on-screen layer-0 entries against 37 off-screen
ones — background tabs, other Spaces, Finder oddments — every one of them a live window, and the discovery
direction filters on the opposite sense for exactly that reason. Keying removal on the flag that is already
in hand would clear the strip. The second edge is that an empty list is a failed read (`current()` answers
`[]` when the CG call does) and is refused before it can retire anything.

Removal goes through `vanish` rather than `retire`, so a window closed unheard still gets one scan to find
a successor: closing a tab group's selected tab is precisely a case where the number leaves the list and
the column must not. `vanish` is already idempotent and already terminates on its grace deadline when the
app never answers, so the wedged case — the reason we are here — cannot hang on it. Nothing new was needed
for the direction that felt hardest. It runs before the discovery half and nothing rests on that: `vanish`
defers and its scan is still out when the strays are gathered, so a removal lands a lane round trip later and
races the arrivals whichever order the two are written in. Losing that race costs one extra tiling pass and a
dead column for up to `successionGrace`, which is the trade that constant already prices — and the app that
lost a window unheard is by construction the slow one, so it is lost routinely.

Rejected: hysteresis on removal, symmetric with the `unaccounted` rounds counter. It would cost 3 s of a
dead window on the strip to defend against a *partial* list read, which there is no evidence occurs, where
the empty-list guard covers the failure that demonstrably can.

Making `vanish` a reconciliation path is what puts weight on `alreadyOpen`, which is provenance — whether a
notification told us a window was being born — and which two more seams therefore have to carry. `vanish`
asks with it: a destroy names a window *leaving* and says nothing about arrivals, and the successor it is
actually after comes back as `succeeded`, which never consults the flag. And the scan coalescer's two maps
both become `[pid_t: Bool]`, so a request it dropped is replayed as the request it was rather than as the
default, with `absorb` splitting its replay in two instead of picking one. Where several coalesce the flag
merges with `||` — a choice of failure direction rather than a guess, since the report cannot separate the
merged requests' windows and, of the two mistakes available, only "born now" resizes a window somebody was
using and follows it off their desktop under a workspace rule. The coalescer could always drop it, given a
destroy and a stray in one app a tick apart; what the removal direction changes is that an app with both is
now the ordinary case, because an app nothing observed missed its creations and its destroys alike.

`scanning` carries the flag for the same reason `rescan` does, which is clearest if the coalescer is read as
several requests answered once rather than as one request with a queue behind it. The replay stands for all
of them, and what it is left to announce is whatever the scan in flight missed — so the question that scan
was asking merges in like any other, and reconciliation's stray cannot be turned into a birth by a
`windowAppeared` that lands behind it. The two orders fail separately and are tested separately: the
notification asking first is answered by putting the flag on the coalesced request, reconciliation asking
first only by merging the one in flight.

Rejected: replaying everything as already-open, right for reconciliation and wrong for the `windowAppeared`
burst the coalescer exists for, where four ⌘N presses would stop taking their preset width. And deriving the
flag per window inside `absorb`, which has nothing to derive it from — `windowAppeared` names a pid and not
a window, which is the reason the re-scan exists at all.

Every guard here is mutation-checked, because a reconciliation test can pass against a broken `reconcile`
by beating the heartbeat without ever firing the grace deadline that does the damage. Dropping the
empty-list guard, swapping list membership for `isOnScreen`, replacing `vanish` with `retire`, removing the
registration sweep, and dropping `alreadyOpen` from the `vanish` scan, from the coalesced replay, or from
the scan it coalesced into: each fails exactly one test, and nothing else. The lane split is the one thing
here the suite does not hold, since `AXObservers` reaches a concrete `AXClient` and has no fake behind it.

## Observations

A number the window server recycles onto a new window before the next tick leaves the dead id on the
strip, since reconciliation sees the number present. A miss rather than a false removal, and the destroy
notification covers it whenever the app is healthy.

A permanently unobservable app now costs one `AXObserverCreate` and one lane job every interval, forever,
uncapped. Cheaper than a single round of the stray path (seven round trips per window) and unlike a window
number, an app being deaf is always worth trying to fix — but it is the one unbounded cost added here. It is
also silent: `register`'s default failure handler is empty, so an app deaf for an hour reads in the logs
exactly like a healthy one, where the scan side has `onIncompleteScan`.

Several windows of one app going missing on the same tick each call `vanish`, which the scan coalescer
collapses into one scan plus one repeat. Correct and bounded; the usual multi-window case is an app
quitting, which has its own path.

A tick that both removes a window and finds a stray in one app scans that app twice. The removal's scan is
still on the lane when the strays are gathered, so the stray is unbound and asks for a scan of its own,
which coalesces and replays into a report where everything rebinds. That is the deaf app's ordinary tick,
and the wasted round trips are the cost `maxReconcileRounds` exists to keep off the lanes. Skipping the pids
vanished this tick when building the stray targets would drop it — the removal's scan covers the whole app
anyway — and was left out only because it is a cost rather than a fault.

Native full screen was the one transition that could have produced a false removal, and it does not:
measured across a full-screen and back on Ghostty, the managed window keeps its number throughout, going
off screen and changing frame but never leaving the list. What *does* leave are transient windows the
transition mints and destroys — a full-screen-sized one with no menu-bar inset, and a menu-bar-height
strip. Two of them surface on screen and layer-0 under a pid we manage, so they reach the discovery half
as strays and can buy one scan of that app; the transition is shorter than the interval, so most are never
sampled, and `maxReconcileRounds` bounds the rest. A scan that lands mid-transition reports them as
`unclaimed` and can therefore log one spurious incomplete scan.

The set the predicate reads is layer-0 only, so a window changing `kCGWindowLayer` would read as a death as
surely as a close would. Sampled at 5 Hz across Mission Control, App Exposé and a Space switch, no managed
window changes layer or leaves the list. That matters more than the full-screen case: Mission Control stays
up for as long as the user holds it, so unlike a transition measured in frames it would span ticks.

The fake answers a scan synchronously unless `holdsAnswers` is set, so a reconciliation test sees `vanish`'s
scan complete inside the `vanish` call — an ordering the real lanes never give. Anything turning on whether
a removal lands before or after an arrival has to set that flag to be testing anything at all, and so does
anything counting scans: under the synchronous answer the removal has already adopted the strays and the
second scan never happens.

`appLaunched` still scans as a birth. A relaunched app restoring windows is the arguable case, since nobody
watched those open either, but it is a separate question and nothing here moved it.
