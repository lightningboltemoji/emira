# A float's stand-in is ready before it is needed

> Change `1788327943` — 2026-09-01

## Goal

The other half of the flash. `1788326836` fenced the way *out*; the way *in* still showed the float go
behind and come back, because a stand-in built at the moment of burial is late by exactly one screenshot.
That change recorded the gap as "not fixable this way" and named two routes out of it. This is one of
them.

It started as a better question: **why not order our picture below the real float and above everything
else, so it is already in place when the float loses focus?** Probed, and the answer is no, twice over:

| asked                                                     | answer                                    |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| `order(.below, relativeTo:)` against a foreign window      | ignored — our window did not move at all  |
| a level-0 window of ours interleaved with foreign windows   | never; every listing sorts by layer first |

AppKit's ordering calls reach only windows in our own app, and window levels are a strict partition: to
survive the raise a picture has to be above the tiled window, the tiled window goes to the *front of
level 0* when macOS raises it, and anything above level 0 is above the real float too. There is no rung
between "below the float" and "above what buries it".

## Implementation

What the z-order idea actually wanted was not a position but a **readiness**, and that is reachable:
`Overlay` has kept its window ordered in at `alpha 0` for the life of the process since the first
compositor, precisely so a raise is a pure alpha flip. The same idiom, applied to a surface there is more
than one of.

**`HoistState` splits the set the core already computes.** `.covered` is the old rule — something emira
placed is in front — and `.standby` is every other on-screen float: its panel built, its still filmed,
showing nothing. Burial is then `reveal()`, and the screenshot is already paid.

**Standby is *on screen*, not "something could cover it".** The narrower predicate is the tempting one
and it is wrong: a float overlapping a column leaves and re-enters that set every time the strip scrolls
out from under it, so it would build, film, destroy and rebuild a picture nobody ever saw, twice per
scroll. On screen is stable under everything the strip does, and the price of being generous is one
still and one invisible window per float — which is a bounded number, since `isFloatedByChoice` already
narrows this to the floats the user asked for.

**Two photographs, and the second is the honest one.** The standby still is filmed while the float is in
the open, so it carries the window's *focused* styling and whatever it looked like before the user's last
interaction. The burial's own film is what the desktop actually just showed. So the reveal paints the
first and cross-fades to the second as it lands — `CoverMode.immediate`'s trade in another place, a
stand-in buying the instant and its own capture overtaking it, at `Reconstruction.refreshDuration`.

**The panel is now a two-stage surface.** `setImage` loads pixels and pays a reveal that was owed;
`reveal` shows and takes clicks, instantly where the pixels are loaded and owed where they are not;
`conceal` fades out and *stands by* rather than tearing down, since the next burial wants the same panel.
`dismiss`/`reclaim` are gone — reclaiming is what `reveal` does, because the panel never left. A panel is
only retired when the core stops naming the float at all: it closed, stopped floating, or left the
screen.

`conceal` drops `isRevealed` at the *start* of the fade rather than its end. Ending it there looks more
careful and is worse: a second pass finding the panel still revealed starts a second fade over the first.
Coming back mid-fade still works, because `reveal` bumps the generation and writes its alpha through the
animator at zero duration, which replaces a fade in flight (`Overlay.raise`).

**One behaviour changed outside hoisting.** Floating a window now always emits an effect — the hoist
plane being told the float exists — where before a `float` that placed nothing emitted nothing at all.
`RulesTests.floatTrueKeepsAStandardWindowOffTheStrip` asserts the new shape; the claim it was making
(emira has no opinion about where a float sits) is untouched.

## Observations

**A standby panel holds a full-resolution still for as long as the float is on screen.** A 800×600 float
at 2× is about 7.7 MB, so a desk with three floats holds a little over 20 MB permanently — beside
`SurfaceCache.defaultBudget`'s 48 MB, and unlike that budget nothing evicts it. It is not in the cache: a
panel's `CALayer.contents` is its own reference, which is what makes the reveal free. If this ever
matters the answer is to pin the hoist stills in `SurfaceCache` and read from there, at the cost of a
second entitlement kind.

**The first burial after a float appears may still flash**, in the window between the core naming it
`.standby` and its film landing. It is much narrower than before — a float is normally in standby long
before anything buries it — but a window floated and clicked away from inside ~40 ms has no pixels yet.
The reveal is owed and paid by the film, so it self-corrects rather than being lost.

**A standby still can be arbitrarily stale.** It is filmed once, when the float becomes on-screen, and
not again until the size changes. A float left alone for an hour reveals an hour-old picture for the
length of one cross-fade. That is the same trade `CoverMode.immediate` already makes and the reason the
burial film exists.

**Validated by the suite and by the z-order probe, not against the daemon**, on the same terms as the two
changes before it. `HoistPlaneTests` covers standby, the owed reveal, the fence, and coming back
mid-release; what it cannot answer is how the reveal reads by eye — in particular whether the cross-fade
from a focused-looking standby still to the burial film is invisible at 0.12 s or reads as a flicker of
its own. That is the confirming pass worth making on the next install.
