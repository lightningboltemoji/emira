# A proportion is a share of its gap too

> Change `1786429487` — 2026-08-10

## Goal

Two columns at `width-presets = [0.5]` did not fit on screen. With `column-gap = 20` and
`outer-gap = 20` on a 1512 pt display the content area is 1472, each column resolved to 736, and the
run came to 736 + 20 + 736 = 1492 — twenty points too long. The viewport scrolled by exactly one
`column-gap`, and at rest the second column's right edge sat on the display edge with its outer margin
eaten. In general a run of proportions summing to 1 overflowed by `(n − 1) · column-gap`.

The outer gaps were not the cause and were already right: `resolvedWidth` has always resolved against
`contentArea`, so "½" was half the *logical* viewport rather than half the display. What no one
subtracted was the inter-column gap, which was charged on top of a full share.

The same arithmetic was wrong one container over, where it was visible as an inconsistency rather than
as overflow: `Column` resolved a pinned height against the full column box while **auto** shared what
was left *after* the gaps, so a window pinned to ½ came out one whole `window-gap` taller than an auto
neighbour — 436 against 416 on the display above. Two pinned halves overflowed the column's bottom by a
gap, which on the strip is the bottom outer margin.

## Implementation

`Extent { span, gap }`, and a proportion resolves as `(span + gap)·p − gap`: `n` tiles have `n − 1`
gaps between them, so each takes its share of the span widened by one gap and pays one gap back. The
`n` gaps paid and the `n − 1` between them cancel to the one folded in, so proportions summing to 1
fill the span exactly — independently of how the 1 is partitioned and into how many pieces. The outer
and inter-tile gaps stay separate quantities, so the fold is over `column-gap` alone and the outer
margin stays outside it — which is what keeps "100%" meaning the content area rather than the display.

A type rather than a `gap:` parameter with a default, because the bug *was* a call site resolving a
proportion without one: a defaulted argument reproduces it silently, while `Extent` makes the span and
the gap impossible to supply separately. `LayoutMetrics.widthExtent` is the strip's, `Column.extent`
the stack's. `PresetSize` is now data only — `resolved(available:)` is gone, and with it
`PresetCycle.resolved(at:available:)`, which had no caller outside tests and was a second way to
resolve a width without a gap.

`Extent.proportion(of:)` is the inverse, for the two callers that record a resolved width back as an
intent — `grow`/`shrink` at a `.percent`, and the settings preview's mirror of it. Stated beside the
formula it inverts rather than open-coded as `width / available`, which is how those two would drift.

Three properties pin it, and each is a test: `p = 1` is the whole span at any gap, so `fullscreen`
cannot drift a gap away from `width-presets = [1.0]`; at `gap = 0` the fold vanishes, which is what
lets every zero-gap test in the suite stand as the guard on the rest; and a partition short of 1 leaves
exactly its shortfall's share, so a strip under-fills or scrolls by precisely what was over- or
under-asked.

Eight tests asserted the old arithmetic. Six were re-derived. The other two had a premise the change
overturns — *raising `column-gap` moves columns without resizing them* — which is now false, because
the gap is part of what a proportion is a share of. Both were repinned on `.fixed` widths, which is the
honest way to isolate spacing from resolution and leaves them testing what they were about (a reload
re-placing only what moved; the movement spring rather than the resize one). `EngineResizeTests`'
centred-detent trio moved to fixed widths for the same reason: it is a test about where a detent
catches, and under a proportion it had become one about how a proportion resolves.

## Observations

`column-gap` is now load-bearing on width and not only on spacing, so a config reload that changes it
re-places every proportional column rather than sliding them. That is inherent — a proportion cannot
tile without knowing the gap — but it is a real behavioural change to the reload path, and
`raisingTheColumnGapReResolvesProportionalWidths` is there to say so out loud.

A lone ½ column is now half a gap narrower than half the content area (726 rather than 736 on the
display above). "½" means *two of me tile the viewport*, which is the reading that makes the ladder
exact; the alternative makes a single column look right and every pair wrong.

Nothing here changes what a *fixed* preset does, and `SizeDelta.resolved(available:)` was deliberately
left alone: a `grow 10%` is a delta, not a share of a run, so it has no gap to fold.

The strip is infinite, so widths cannot be normalized across "the row" — there is no bounded row to
solve over. A per-tile formula is the only shape available, and this one happens to be exact.
