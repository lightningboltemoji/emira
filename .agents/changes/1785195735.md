# A full-width column stranded its neighbour on screen

> Change `1785195735` — 2026-07-27

## Goal

Focusing away from a ⅓-width column onto a `fullscreen` (or grown-to-ceiling) one left the departing
window standing at the left edge of the display — roughly screen height, about 40 pt wide, and
permanent. The motion itself was clean; the strand arrived with the cross-fade.

## Implementation

`Strip.visibleColumnIndices` is the reducer's setFrame-vs-park switch, and it asked for strictly
positive overlap. The two sides of that comparison are computed differently: a column's right edge is
a running prefix of the widths (`leftEdge`), while the offset that frames a viewport-wide column is
their sum (`maxOffset` ← `contentWidth`). Those agree in exact arithmetic at precisely one
arrangement — a column exactly as wide as the content area, revealed against a neighbour whose width
no binary fraction holds, so ⅓ of 1760 but not ½ — and disagree by ~1e-13 in a `Double`. The
neighbour therefore overlapped the viewport by a tenth of a picometre, counted as on screen, and was
`setFrame`d 586 pt off the left edge. macOS refuses to place a window there and clamps it back to a
~40 pt sliver (`PRINCIPLES.md` §6), which is the artifact. The presentation plane derives from the
same numbers and drew the layer where it was actually asked to go — nowhere — so nothing was wrong
until the cover came down.

The predicate now requires more than `Strip.visibilityTolerance` of overlap, half a point, the same
sub-pixel tolerance `Engine` diffs placements at. Applied symmetrically at both viewport edges, since
the arithmetic that ties is symmetric. It reaches the sweep and the park set through the same call,
so the capture scope and the tile/park decision stay the one question they were.

Rejected: making the two offsets agree exactly (chasing float identity, and only for this one
arrangement); relaxing the end-of-strip clamp so a short last column could sit flush left with
wallpaper to its right (a scroll-policy change to fix a rounding bug, and the clamp is deliberate).

## Observations

- The tolerance is about representation, not about placeability. A column left genuinely — not
  spuriously — a few points on screen is still tiled and would still be clamped by macOS; the only
  path to one is a `SizeCorrection` narrowing a near-viewport-wide column, which no app has produced
  here yet. If it shows up, the fix is a real placeability floor, not a bigger epsilon.
- The clamp itself is inferred, not measured: `PRINCIPLES.md` §6 records ~40 pt from an extreme
  coordinate and a 1 pt sliver honored at the right edge, and the reported strand matches the former.
  Nothing here depends on which it is.
- Verified in the core only (`Layout`/`Strip` replay against the reporting display's exact geometry,
  882 tests green). The desktop-level confirmation — focus back and forth, end on the wide window — is
  still worth doing.
