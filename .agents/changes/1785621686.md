# The detent watches both viewport edges

> Change `1785621686` — 2026-08-01

## Goal

`Strip.resizeDetent` computed the uncentred notch from the right viewport edge alone, on the argument
that a reveal is the minimal scroll: the viewport does not move until the focused column is flush
against its right edge, so anything evicted off the left is strictly further away than the right-edge
notch. The argument is sound and incomplete. It holds only while there *is* a right-edge candidate,
and there is one only while the focused column is short of the viewport's right edge — the case it was
written for. The moment the column rests against that edge, growing it scrolls the strip from the first
point of the delta, every edge ahead of it is frozen against the viewport's right, and the left edge is
the only bound crossing anything at all. The function returned `nil` there and the press spent in full.

That resting state is not an edge case: it is where the strip's last column always sits, and where a
reveal leaves any column it had to pull in. Three columns at 30%, 30% and 60% rest at offset 200 with
the last focused; `grow 20%` walked the middle column 100 pt off the left edge, when 10% was the flush
arrangement 100 pt away. Shrinking at the far end had the mirror hole — the viewport is pulled off
`maxOffset` rather than uncovering anything to the right, so the column it had cut comes back whole with
no notch to stop on.

## Implementation

**The sweep is two-phase, and the phases are sequential rather than simultaneous.** Uncentred, `drag`
is how much width it takes before the viewport starts to move — `viewMax − R(i)` growing, `maxOffset −
offset` shrinking. Below it only the right edge closes on the columns from `i` on; above it the strip
travels with the column and only the left edge sweeps the columns up to `i`, so a left-edge crossing is
`drag` plus its own reach. Centred, both edges move at half speed from the first point of the delta and
the two are simultaneous, which is what the existing `2 ×` branch already said.

The two are still tried against each other with `min` even though the right one wins whenever it exists
— its reach is at most `drag` by construction. Writing the comparison out costs a line and means the
code does not rest on that lemma being re-derived by the next reader.

**Flush is per bound now, not a bail for the whole function.** `isFlush` returned `nil` if *either*
list held an edge on its bound; the tolerance is gone from the guard and into the candidate search,
where `> at + tol` / `< at − tol` skips the edge a bound is sitting on and takes the next one out. This
is what keeps the pass-through — a bound already on an edge crosses nothing of its own — while letting
the other bound catch, and it drops two claims that were true only of the global form: a packed strip
now catches where the neighbour goes entirely off screen rather than nowhere, and growing an
already-cut column catches where it exactly fills the viewport. Both are arrangements the feature
exists to land on, and neither is reachable in under a press.

**Crossings at or before zero are discarded.** With the widths read at rest and the offset at
`.target`, the two can disagree — an app's `SizeCorrection` can leave the focused column cut on its
right, where `drag` is negative and the reveal's answer is a jump rather than a sweep. The edges inside
that jump are crossed for any delta, so they are not notches; dropping them is also what makes the
already-cut case land exactly on `V − w`, where the drag stops because the column now fills the
viewport and the reveal switches to aligning its left edge.

## Observations

- The check for "already flush" is still `Strip.visibilityTolerance`, and the caveat it carries is
  unchanged: an app that quantizes its width answers some points off the notch, so a second press there
  will sometimes catch again instead of passing through.
- The left-edge notch has the same three-press shape on screen as the right-edge one — pack, push past,
  pack again — but like the rest of the detent it has not been watched under `smooth`, only asserted.
- A shrink whose viewport is dragged off `maxOffset` now catches, which the previous note called inert
  and only was while right edges were the sole candidates. The strip going exactly flush inside the
  viewport is the notch it lands on.
