# Reduce released stills off the main thread

> Change `1791700631` — 2026-10-10

## Goal

`ASSESSMENT.md` P5. `SurfaceCache.unpin` scaled every released still down to quarter size with
`CGContextDrawImage`, on the main thread, at the end of every cross-fade. In the assessment's sample that was 48
of ~150 busy main-thread samples over two transitions, and the stall probe caught 37, 52 and 65 ms. It landed
just as the next keypress's head batch wanted the main thread, and it stuttered any scrim or guide fade in
flight. The unpinned path of `record` (a late film, a hoist's film) paid the same cost.

## Implementation

**The release stays synchronous; the reduction does not.** Change `1787638782` argued for a synchronous
`unpin` because of *which* photograph is current. The next head batch releases the outgoing cover a line
before it reads the store, and a demotion a turn late would hand it the film before last. That argument
concerns the entitlement, not when the pixels shrink. So `unpin` still drops the pins in the same turn,
and a photo that needs reducing is marked `isReducing` and handed to a `Reducer`.

**A reduction lands only on the film it was made from, and only while that film is still released (`land`).**
Three things can intervene, and each makes it a no-op:

- A newer film, which has a different mint.
- A cover pinning the photo again. `pin` clears `isReducing`, because the photo is on screen at full size.
- A forget or a `removeAll`. The photo is gone.

A reduction that fails leaves the photo as it is, marked reduced so it is never retried. The never-twice rule
holds, because only a landing sets `isReduced`.

**A photo whose reduction is in flight is outside the budget.** Counting it at full size would evict
stand-ins to make room for pixels about to shrink sixteenfold. Every byte-accounting site now goes through
`budgeted(_:)`, so pinned and in-flight photos are excluded in one place.

**`record`'s unpinned path checks reducibility synchronously** (`reducedSize(of:)`, dimensions only), then
stores the full still with its reduction in flight. "Too small to reduce is not stored" is therefore decided
in the same turn as before.

**`Reducer` is the seam.** `.detached` runs `reduced` in a `Task.detached(priority: .utility)` and hops back
to the main actor. It is the default, and `main.swift` takes it. `.inline` is for tests about outcomes rather
than timing: the 22 `SurfaceCache` constructions in `CaptureTests` now name it. Three new tests use a manual
reducer to hold a reduction in flight:

- the release is immediate and the reduction lands later;
- a reduction is taken only by its own film, with two reductions in flight;
- a re-pin cancels a reduction, and the next release reduces once.

The P5 alternative of a faster scaler (`vImage`) was not taken. It would shorten the stall, not remove it.

**Validated live** with `EMIRA_STALL_PROBE=16` over 13 transitions on the 5K display with 4–6 windows. There
were no stalls in `SurfaceCache`, `reduced` or `CGContextDrawImage`. The stalls that remain are
`Scrims.paint`/`watch` window-list reads (33–35 ms, P6) and one `SLSGetDisplayLink` (35 ms, P11).

## Observations

- A read between release and landing gets the full-resolution photograph. A stand-in raised in that window
  is therefore sharp rather than soft. The softness is the disclosure that a photo is old, and a photo
  released a few milliseconds ago is not old.
- `.utility` priority was chosen so the reduction yields to the next head batch's capture work. Under
  heavy load a reduction can land late; nothing waits on it.
- Not measured: peak memory. In-flight photos hold their full-size pixels a little longer than before, at
  most until the detached task runs.
