# A window's corner is read inside whatever border it is drawn in, and a reading at rest outranks the rest

> Change `1792265628` — 2026-10-17

## Goal

On the primary machine, after a browser alone on its workspace and then fn-space opening a Ghostty window, the
veiled Ghostty window showed a dark crescent at both top corners: its own unveiled background, between its real
corner and a veil cut rounder than it.

Measured off the screenshot, the veil's arc is a circle of 27 pt (54 px, fitted at five points between 53 and 55)
around a window whose real corner is the system's 17.15. The crescent is Ghostty's own background (50,34,28)
unveiled. The body is veiled, and the browser beside it is not. Every other window on screen was cut right, and
so was Ghostty's corner on this machine at rest, so the radius came from one still.

27.2 pt is exactly what the area measure reads off a 17.15 pt corner with an extra 383 px² in each block: a
clear pixel along the top and down the side, two pixels along the top alone, or a two-pixel border of which the
one-pixel trim takes one. `1792030100` recorded the first of these (27.20, "clear on all four") as the opening
still it fixed. Read with the code before this change, on synthetic windows:

| still of a 17 pt corner | before | after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| at rest | 17.04 | 17.04 |
| 1 px clear on all four | 17.04 | 17.04 (not at rest) |
| 2 px clear on all four | **27.14** | 17.04 (not at rest) |
| 2 px clear along the top only | **27.16** | 17.04 (not at rest) |
| 1 px clear along the top only, or top and sides | 22.67 | 17.04 (not at rest) |
| rim ¼ px deep (part clear) on all four | 20.03 | 17.05 (not at rest) |
| rim ½ or 1½ px deep | 22.65 | 17.05 (not at rest) |
| 3 px clear on all four | 34.36 | nil |
| whole window at alpha 0.98 / 0.995 | 33.57 / 21.39 | nil / nil |

**And one bad still was enough, because the radius a veil is cut by was the newest one read off any still.**
`SurfaceCache` took a radius from every film and replaced the last with it, opening stills included, and nothing
promises a window is filmed again. An arrival is refilmed only if its kept still no longer fits its column, and
a veiled window that does not move is not filmed at all. So one reading taken while the window server animated
the window rounded its veil for as long as the window stood still.

Not reproduced on this machine. Twelve fn-space and tab flows through the instrumented daemon (Chrome alone
and beside Ghostty, a fresh launch, a new window, a new tab in a focused group and in a parked one) filmed
Ghostty's open in three phases: clear top and bottom (frame 1856×1161 pt, still 3744×2346 px), clear on all four
(1882×1179, 3764×2358), then rest. All three were one pixel wide and fully clear, and the trim read them right.
The primary machine's covers are filmed faster and catch the animation at another moment. The exact still there
is unknown.

## Implementation

**The border is read out of the probe blocks themselves** (`measuredCorner`), which replaces the one-pixel crop
(`withoutBorder`). Each block also reads two strips past any corner's reach: its last quarter of rows, and its
quarter of columns furthest from the window's side. An opaque window is opaque there right out to the edge.
What both blocks show in the outer two pixels of those strips is the border, and it comes off both corners
analytically (`D − P·s − P·t + s·t`). So a rim only part clear comes off as the part it is, and a border along
the top needs no partner at the bottom. A block that shows more than the shared border is unpainted window and
is not read, which keeps the lesser-corner rule. One clear further in than two pixels (`borderLimit`) is no
corner at all, which is what turns a fade, a translucent window and a column hanging off both edges into `nil`
rather than a plausible radius. A block reaches no further than half the window's shorter side, so its far
strips stay clear of the corner below it and the one beside it. A radius is read only short of where those
strips begin: past that, their being opaque says the clear is not a corner's. `anUnpaintedEdgeDoesNotInflateTheRadius` and
`unpaintedEdgesNeverUnderReport` pass unchanged. The measure is also cheaper: 0.055–0.065 ms a still against
0.12–0.21 ms before, and against 1.56 ms on a bordered still, where the old crop made a subimage that was slow
to draw.

**Every reading says whether it was taken at rest** (`CornerReading`): its still the frame to the pixel
(`isExactly`) and inside no border. Every opening still filmed here fails one or both. The reading replaces the
bare radius on `CapturedSurface`, `SurfaceCache`, `CornerSurvey` and the planes that round by it.

**At rest outranks animating everywhere, and animating outranks the fallback.** `SurfaceCache` keeps a reading
at rest over one taken animating whatever their batches, and of two alike the newer batch. So an opening still
can never undo a settled reading, and a window only ever filmed opening is rounded by that reading, not by
`fallbackCornerRadius`. `CornerSurvey` keeps what it reads too, and films a window again after `retryInterval`
until it reads it at rest, up to `attemptLimit` films. Any change to its reading cuts again, one coming to rest
at the radius it already had included: that is when the daemon turns to it from a cover's reading taken
animating, which is the one that may be wrong, and an opening still unbordered but larger than its frame reads
the same radius to the bit as the window at rest. The scrim names every window outlined without a reading
at rest, a managed one filmed only opening included, so the survey starts for it. The daemon picks between the
cover's reading by id and the survey's by number (`CornerReading.preferred`, in `corner(of:number:)`): at rest
from either, else the cover's. The scrim, the tarp and a cover's pins all ask that one function, so a pin now
sees the survey's reading where it used to see only the cache's.

Validated through the daemon with tracing on `SurfaceCache.record`, the survey's answers and every cut's
guessed windows. Ghostty, launched beside a Finder window: its arrival still was 2580×1508 px against a
1292×756 pt frame, read 17.146 and not at rest. The next cut drew it at 17.146 rather than 12 and named it, and
the survey read 17.146 at rest. System Settings: the survey's first film caught it animating, the cuts after it
drew that reading rather than the fallback, and the second film read it at rest. Notes, Calendar and Safari
arrived at rest. A probe of eleven windows across eight apps at rest (light appearance, macOS 27.0.1) read
exactly 0 in every border strip, so none is mistaken for animating. Titled windows filmed alone as
`surfaceShot` films them, with and without a unified toolbar, read 17.146 at rest from 66 pt to 500 pt on
either axis.

**Rejected: refusing every reading not at rest**, which this change first did. The survey would then supply the
radius, but until it landed a window filmed only opening took the 12 pt fallback, about 0.7 s through the daemon.
A pin never asks the survey, so it kept the fallback until a cover happened to film it at rest. Meanwhile the
new measure reads every synthetic opening still above to within 0.01 pt of the window at rest, and every real
one filmed here to within a quarter point (16.93, 17.146 and 17.16 against 17.15). Refusing them threw away a
reading that is almost always near to guard against one nobody has caught. **Rejected: keeping the newest
reading of any kind.** That is the rule the bug lived in, since nothing films a resting window again on
schedule. **Rejected: the median of all readings at rest as the fallback.** Every window filmed here reads a
bit-identical 17.1459 (Chrome, Safari, Finder, System Settings, Ghostty), which makes it tempting, but it is
still a guess that depends on which windows a session happened to film.
**Rejected: re-measuring each corner against its own edge margin** (`1792030100`'s rejected alternative). A band
on one side reads as margin, and that corner reads short. Taking only the margin *both* corners share, and
refusing a corner that shows more, is what tells a border from a band. **Rejected: a 96 pt block whatever the
window's size**, the old measure's. Below about 110 pt its far strips reach the corner beneath, whose clear
reads as border or as no corner at all: real titled windows 90 and 100 pt tall read `nil`, and 106 pt read
14.88 and not at rest, which would spend every survey film on it and cut it 2 pt short. **Rejected: bounding a
radius at half its block**, the old measure's. With the block itself half the window, that refused every window
under 70 pt: real titled windows 56 to 66 pt tall, which read within 0.2 pt at rest without it, took the 12 pt
fallback. The far strips already refuse a block clear where no corner is. What the bound still catches is clear
inside a block short of them (`aBlockClearPastItsCornerIsNotACorner`), refused at 72 pt in a full block rather
than 48. No window filmed here reads near either.

## Observations

- **The failing still is inferred, not caught.** Every border mechanism that yields 27.2 shows in the far strips
  of both blocks and now reads 17.04. If the primary machine films one this cannot see, its reading rounds the
  window until the survey reads it at rest: about one `retryInterval` after the cover comes down for a veiled
  window, but indefinitely for a pin or a tarp neighbour, since neither starts a survey. The one case this
  cannot see at all is a window genuinely drawn with a rounder corner for a moment (a style change while an app
  sets up its titlebar), exact in size and unbordered. That still is read as at rest. This machine is on macOS
  27.0.1, where a window with a unified toolbar reads 17.15 as one without does. If the primary runs a release
  whose toolbar windows have larger corners, Ghostty's tab titlebar is the place to look. A window drawn whole at alpha 0.99 read 28.2 before this change, as near 27.2 as any
  border, and now reads `nil` and takes the fallback. Ghostty is opaque on this machine. A crescent on a Ghostty
  window long at rest on the primary, not only one just opened, points there instead, which this does not fix.
- **Dark appearance reads as light does**, set per window (`NSWindow.appearance` on a titled window, with and
  without a unified toolbar): 17.146 at rest, exactly 0 in every border strip. The system setting itself is
  untested. A part-clear edge would have to be under about 0.9 alpha to stop windows reading at rest, and they
  would still read within a quarter point.
- **A window under about 60 pt on either axis reads short.** Its block, half the window, is too short for the
  far strips to clear the tail of its own corner, which runs further along each edge than a circle's (a 17 pt
  circle reads exactly down to 46 pt) and comes off as border. Real titled windows: 66 pt and up read 17.146 at
  rest, with a unified toolbar too; 60 pt reads 17.098 and 56 pt 16.964, at rest; 50 pt 16.62 and 46 pt 16.26,
  not at rest; and 40 pt `nil`. Short is the side the fallback errs on, and from 50 pt down, outlined, it costs
  `attemptLimit` survey films.
- **The survey now films most arrivals.** Every opening still filmed here is not at rest, so a window arriving
  under a veil is filmed one to three more times once its cover comes down, where a managed window with any
  reading never was. A film is 25–30 ms here (about 90 ms cold), started only while the capture plane is idle,
  but a cover that starts during one waits behind it.
- **A translucent window has no radius at all**, now as before. It used to read past the bound or read inflated.
  It is now refused, and keeps the fallback.
- **A column hanging off both edges by up to a point, from an app that does not paint off-screen**, is read as a
  two-pixel border at 2× and reads short. It is not at rest, so a reading at rest from any later film replaces it.
- The fallback (12) is stale on this OS, where every window measured is 17.15. It now shows only where nothing
  has read a window at all.
- `CornerSurvey` still keeps an answer for as long as its number is on screen, and a second reading alike cuts
  nothing again.
