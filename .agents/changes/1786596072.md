# The pointer arrives before the window stops

> Change `1786596072` — 2026-08-12

## Goal

`[mouse] follows-focus` paid its visit when the transition closed, which is the settle gate plus the AX
landings: the cursor jumped into the middle of a window that had already visibly stopped, after ~350 ms of
motion the eye had just finished following. Two motions in sequence where the user reads one event, and the
second is a snap. The destination has been knowable the whole time — the reals teleport at the cover's raise —
so nothing about the old timing was buying information. It was buying the guarantee that the cursor never lands
where its window is not, and only the close gate was ever asked for it.

## Implementation

`Engine.revealHasReached` replaces `!s.motion.isTransitioning` as the payment gate: the visit is due when the
owed window's **stand-in already covers the point the cursor will land on**. Both numbers exist during
`.covered` and nowhere else — the destination on the truth plane (teleported, so it is the resting frame,
clamped into the working area exactly as the warp clamps it) and the stand-in on the presentation plane (the
same `naturalFrame` + displacement expression `emitLayerFrames` blits). Containment of the one point in the
other rect is the whole predicate.

That gate is the *earliest instant that keeps the original guarantee*, which is why it needs no tuning
constant: if the destination point is inside the arriving window's own layer, the cursor cannot land on bare
desktop, on a neighbour, or on a photograph of a layout that has moved. It self-scales with travel, since
"half a window still to go" is a distance the spring covers in a fraction of its run. Measured on the suite's
full-width scroll at 120 Hz: the visit is paid on tick 8 where the session closes on tick 43 — a ~290 ms lead,
and the cursor spends it sitting on the window sliding under it.

Rejected: a fixed lead in milliseconds (needs a spring settle-time prediction, and a constant that is wrong for
every travel but one); a fraction of the total travel (needs the origin recorded, which a retargeted transition
invalidates — newest-wins would have to re-seed it); warping on the focus change itself (the flash the cover
exists to prevent, and the reason the debt exists at all); and animating the cursor along its own spring, which
is a warp per frame — the window server suppresses real mouse deltas briefly after each one, so it would fight
the hand for the whole transition to buy motion the eye is not looking at.

The gate also stopped being the desktop's and became the destination display's. A cover on the other screen
says nothing about where the pointer is going or what is drawn under it, and holding the visit for one left the
cursor behind for a reveal it was not part of. `Motion.isTransitioning` (the global) is now the frame clock's
question alone.

## Observations

The lead is invisible under `[mouse] hide`, which hides the cursor for the whole keyboard-driven command —
the two settings only interact for a user running one without the other.

Nothing is paid during `.capturing`/`.raising`: the reals have not moved, so the truth plane still names the
old frame and a containment test there would trivially pass at the *wrong* point. That costs the capture head's
few ms of possible lead and is why the phase switch is exhaustive rather than a `!isCovered` guard.

Not yet watched on real hardware — the arithmetic and the effect stream are pinned by `EngineWarpTests`
(`theCursorLandsOnTheArrivingWindowsStandIn` asserts the emitted `setLayerFrame` contains the emitted warp's
centre, in the same batch), but whether ~290 ms of lead reads as anticipation or as a cursor that jumped too
early is a question for the eye. If it is too eager, the predicate takes a tightening factor on the remaining
travel without changing shape: `0.15 × width` moves the visit to roughly tick 15 of 43.
