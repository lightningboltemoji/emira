# A cover is held off a pin's silhouette, not off its band

> Change `1789936391` — 2026-09-20

## Goal

Scroll a column under a pin and it shows up in the pin's rounded corners at once, while the cover is
still carrying that column's stand-in across the screen.

The cleared region was `pinInsets` — a full-height rectangle as wide as the outer gap plus the pin —
and `Overlay.setClearing` shrank the base and the strip to what was left of the display. So everything
the band held that was *not* the pin was uncovered too: the margin the pin stands in, and the four
notches its corner rounding cuts out of its own box. A real window whose destination overlaps the band
teleports there the moment the cover is up — that overlap is exactly the set `guardPins` fences — and
the notches then show it for the rest of the flight.

Measured A/B on one scripted scenario: 1710×1107, a left pin at half the display, three 513 pt columns
beside it, and a two-column scroll (~1.05 s under a stiffness-8 spring) that slides one column under
the pin and lands the next over its corner. The sample is the pin's bottom-right notch, 2 pt in from
each edge, with a second sample out in the strip to say whether the cover is still flying:

| | notch reaches the settled colour | strip still animating until |
| --- | --- | --- |
| before | 3083 ms | 3917 ms |
| after | 4050 ms | 3900 ms |

Before, the corner was showing the settled desktop 834 ms early — 80% of the flight. After, it tracks
the reconstruction instead: the column sliding under the pin reaches it at 3133 ms, the seam between
that column and the next crosses at 3983 ms, and the settled colour arrives at 4050 ms, just behind the
strip sample, which is the right order for a corner that sits to its left.

## Implementation

**The clearing carries the pins** (`Effect.setCoverClearing`, `PinnedFrame`). `settleCoverClearing`
emits `metrics.pinFrames` minus whatever the session is already drawing — the same predicate as before
over a different shape — and `LayoutMetrics.pinInsets` loses its only reader and goes with it. The
rounding is the shell's: `Reconstruction.cornerRadius` asks `SurfaceCache` for the measurement, the
source and the fallback the scrim already uses, since the plane that films windows is the only one that
can measure it. A pin has one in practice, because pinning a window moves it, which scopes it into a
cover, which films it — and a radius outlives the photograph it was measured from.

**The cut is a mask, and the mask is what this costs.** `Overlay` stops moving the base and the strip
and puts an even-odd path on the host: the display, with each pin's rounded rectangle taken back out.
A mask is an offscreen pass — the masked subtree is rendered into a scratch texture the size of the
layer's *bounds*, the mask's alpha multiplies it, and the result is composited — so it is priced by the
area of the cover, not by the area actually cut out, and a 12 pt notch costs exactly what half a screen
would. That is what the rectangle was chosen to avoid, so it was measured rather than argued: a
cover-shaped harness (full-screen base image, three window-sized stills with shadows, blitted every
refresh) on an M3 Air at 1710×1107 @2x, means of three rounds:

| | GPU device utilization | late frames |
| --- | --- | --- |
| opaque, flush with the display — a cover with no pin | 27.8% | 0 |
| non-opaque, base and strip clipped off the band | 21.4% | 0 |
| non-opaque, host masked to the silhouette | 36.1% | 0 |

The middle row is the one to price against, and it is *below* the first: clipping two layers off the
band is 35% fewer pixels to rasterize, and going non-opaque costs nothing measurable next to that. So
the mask is **+14.7 points, about +70%,** over what a pinned display used to pay — half of it the
offscreen pass and half of it rasterizing the band's pixels before throwing the pin's box away.
WindowServer CPU is unchanged throughout (~40%), nothing drops, and a live daemon with a pin up holds
63–64 fps on the 60 Hz panel. Masking the stand-in container alone measures the same as masking the
host, so there is no cheaper place to put it.

What it buys is that the band stops being a special region at all. Everything outside the silhouette is
drawn like any other part of the screen, so the raise is pixel-identical there too, and a window
sliding under the pin goes on being the cover's to draw right up to the rounding.

Rejected: a `destinationOut` compositing filter, which would cut the shape with no offscreen pass at
all. It does not punch through — the erase lands on the window's own backing store and comes out
opaque white, against a live desktop, both with the fill on the host layer and with it on a sublayer.
An even-odd mask on the same window shows the desktop through the same shape.

Rejected: filling the band's non-pin region from the base photograph, which needs no mask and no
per-frame cost. The base is the display with the moving windows taken out of it, so the notches would
show wallpaper where a stand-in belongs: the raise stops being pixel-identical there, and a window
leaving the pin pops out of the corner at the raise instead of at the teleport. It moves the artefact
rather than removing it.

**A radius guessed short is the safe error**, which is why the fallback is a floor rather than a
guess at the current macOS. Cutting inside the true rounding leaves the cover drawing a sliver of the
pin's own corner from the base, where the pin is standing still and therefore identical; cutting
outside it leaves the desktop showing, which is the whole defect.

## Observations

**Checked live**, as the table above and as two frames of the same scenario 3.5 s in: before, a green
sliver — the window that has already teleported — sits in the pin's bottom-right notch while the cover
is still drawing the magenta column across it; after, the magenta continues into the notch and under
the rounding, with no sliver and no crescent at the curve, which is also what says the measured radius
is the window's own.

**The GPU number is one machine and one display** — an M3 Air at 60 Hz, a scene of three full-height
stand-ins moving over a full-screen base. A denser cover or a faster display would pay more of it, and
nothing here adapts.

**The mask is on the whole cover because the thing it cuts spans the whole cover**, which is the
structural mismatch in it: the region removed is four notches and a margin, and the price is the
display. The proportionate version is the one the teleport fence already uses two sections down — mask
only when a scoped window is over a pin *or about to be*, which `guardPins` computes today, and take
the rectangle otherwise. That is two paths through `setClearing` and a mask path rewritten per frame in
a moving stand-in's own coordinates, for a cost that currently drops no frames, so it is not built.

**The notch is what was sampled on the glass.** The margin strips above, below and outside the pin are
the same mechanism and the same path, and were not separately measured; with a zero outer gap they are
empty anyway. `Overlay.cut` is geometry and is checked directly in `CoverCutTests`, including that a
point inside the rounding is left to the desktop and a point in the notch is not; installing the mask
needs a window server and is not.

**Two pins are two subpaths of one even-odd cut**, which is correct only because they cannot overlap —
a left band and a right band. Nothing enforces that here; `LayoutMetrics.pinWidth`'s clamp does.
