# Round the veil's cut-outs too

> Change `1789619551` — 2026-09-16

## Goal

A float over an unfocused window stays opaque, as it should, but the patch it cut out of the veil was its
square frame. The float's corners are rounded, so the window under each one showed unveiled in a sharp
triangle. A `stack` workspace had the same artefact on the other path: where a veiled tile stands on the one
behind it, the declined overlap was a rectangle, and the tile behind showed unveiled outside the front tile's
corner.

## Implementation

**Occluders are rounded.** `ScrimWindow.mask` painted a region rounded only when its veil was above zero,
on the argument that outside an occluder's corner the screen holds its shadow over whatever is beneath, so
rounding it would leave "four lit crumbs". That is true only for a radius larger than the window's own.
Outside a correct corner is the window beneath, which keeps the mask value it was painted with, and that is
exactly what the screen shows there. The argument predated measured radii (`1789618529`). With a guessed 12,
over-rounding was the risk. With a measured one, square is the error. Checked live on a focused float, then
the same float with focus on a third window: the veil follows the float's corner in both.

**A decline is the window behind, inside the window in front.** The stamp was the intersection rectangle,
square at every corner. It is now `ScrimRegion(frame: behind, cornerRadius: behind's radius, within: front's
silhouette)`, painted as the behind window's rounded rect clipped to the front window's rounded rect. That is
exact at all three kinds of corner. Where the front window rounds, the region outside keeps the behind window's
veil. Where the behind window rounds, the front window's own veil stays. Where two straight edges cross, the
corner is square because the windows really do cross there. Checked live on a three-tile cascade: the square
patch at the middle tile's top-left corner, visible before the change, is gone.

Rejected: keeping the intersection rectangle and giving it four radii, one per corner, chosen by which window
owns both of its edges. It is the same shape in the common case, and wrong where an arc is larger than the
overlap. It also needs a hand-built path, where a clip is one call.

`CGPath(roundedRect:)` does not trap on a radius larger than half the rect (checked), so a tiny foreign window
now rounded as an occluder needs no clamp.

## Observations

A float whose tile has focus is hoisted, and `HoistPanel` draws its still above the scrim. The still is
transparent outside its corners, so that case was already correct and does not reach this code.

A window never filmed is rounded by `Scrims.fallbackCornerRadius` (12), now as an occluder too. On macOS 27
that is under the measured 17.15, so it errs toward a small unveiled corner rather than a veiled crumb on the
opaque window. A radius that is measured *too large*, as on the arrival film `1789618529` describes, now
also over-rounds occluders and declines: a thin veiled crescent on an opaque window's corner until it is
filmed again.

A sheet is still veiled with its window rather than cut out (`1789617270`). With rounded occluders the
square-corner reason for that is gone, but the case for it (the cover draws it that way) stands.
