# A float that floats

> Change `1788325925` — 2026-09-01

## Goal

`float` took a window off the strip and stopped there, which meant it did not float — it was *ignored*.
macOS stacks by what was focused last, so the first click on a tiled window put the float behind it with
no route back but Cmd-Tab or the Dock. Nothing emira may call fixes that: `AXRaise` orders a window
within its own app and says nothing about the app's place among the others, and re-levelling a foreign
window needs SkyLight and therefore SIP off.

A second defect sat next to it and had not been noticed. A cover's base is the display *minus the scoped
windows* (`SCKCapturer`), scope is `sweptWindowIds` over strip columns only, so a float is baked into the
base — and every layer is added above the base. Scroll the strip past a float and the tiled columns pass
*over* it, then it pops back at the cross-fade.

Both are the same missing thing: emira had no opinion about z-order at all. `World.window(at:)` said so
outright — "it knows frames, not stacking".

## Implementation

**Hoisting is the presentation plane outside a transition**: the float's own pixels in a window of ours,
standing exactly where it stands, one level above the cover. That placement is what fixes the second
defect for free — a hoisted float is over the reconstruction, so the sliding columns pass *under* it, and
`Reconstruction` never learns what a float is.

**The decision is the core's** (`State.hoistBindings` → `Effect.setHoists`), because "what is on the
screen" is a question `World.isOnScreen` and `World.window(at:)` already answer and a second opinion
about it is the mistake `PRINCIPLES` §1 names. Three rules, each removing a way for the picture to be a
lie, and the first is the one that keeps the rest honest.

**Only while something covers it.** A hoist is a photograph: it cannot be dragged, resized, scrolled or
hovered, and floats are precisely the windows people position by hand. Hoisting every float would trade a
z-order bug for an interaction bug on windows that never had one. Confined to a covered float, the trade
is free — none of that worked on a buried window either.

**Only what the user chose.** `World.floating` is a tri-state and `isFloating` collapses it; an explicit
`true` is only ever written by `Command.float` or a rule's `float = true`, so the distinction was already
in the data and only needed a reader that does not collapse it (`isFloatedByChoice`). The taxonomy floats
every dialog, sheet, popover and tool palette, and the occlusion rule alone does not save you from them:
a background app's utility panels are exactly the windows a tiled window covers, and pinning them over
the window you are typing in is not floating them. One wart fell out — `handleFloat` early-returns when
the toggle agrees with the current value, so `float on` over a dialog recorded nothing and there was no
way to say "adopt this one". It now records the choice. **One direction only**: `float off` over a tiled
window still records nothing, because nothing reads "explicitly tiled" differently from "tiled by role" —
a role is read once, at adoption, so it cannot move under a live window and there is no answer to
outrank. The symmetric version also breaks `aFloatThatChangesNothingIsSilent`, which is right to hold.

**Only a window emira placed can bury one** (`World.placedOnScreen`). A float behind something emira
never placed was not buried by emira. This also happens to keep hoists away from native full-screen
windows, which emira does not place.

**Stacking is reconstructed, never asked.** `World.focusedAt` is a monotonic counter written on every
focus change, and `StackOrder` reads it lexicographically on `(app, window)` — macOS's own two-level
model, where activating an app lifts all of its windows and within an app the newest focus is on top.
Both terms are needed: window rank alone misses a sibling rising with its app, app rank alone cannot
separate two windows of one app. The window server's own answer was rejected outright, not on principle
but on the measurement in `1788322209` — a `CGWindowListCopyWindowInfo` blocking for 190–223 ms while
another app animated is not a question that can be asked at the rate stacking changes. The
reconstruction fails in one direction only: an app that raises a window without focusing it reads as "not
covered", which is a float left alone, and the other direction would be a picture over a window genuinely
in front.

Writing it **only when focus moves** is load-bearing. `theFocusEchoOfASwitchIsAbsorbed` asserts that
folding our own `.focus` echo leaves `World` byte-identical, and a counter bumped on a re-assertion
breaks it — correctly, since re-focusing the window that already has focus raises nothing.

**The closure over overlapping floats.** Hoisting float A draws it over *every* real window, including a
float B genuinely in front of it. So a float above a hoisted one, overlapping it, is hoisted too, and the
two keep their real order among the pictures. Iterated to a fixpoint, bounded by the candidate count.

**One `NSWindow` per hoist, not one per display, and the mouse decided it.** Both passthrough mechanisms
were measured on this machine and both fail:

| mechanism                                            | click over the transparent region |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| non-opaque window, fully transparent pixels           | swallowed by the top window       |
| `NSView.hitTest` returning `nil`                      | swallowed — reached neither window |

A working-area panel with `ignoresMouseEvents = false` would therefore eat every click on the desktop.
The only region the window server routes on is a window's frame, so the frames have to be the hoists. The
alternative considered and dropped was a per-display panel toggling `ignoresMouseEvents` from a global
mouse-moved monitor: it works, but it adds a standing idle cost the daemon otherwise only pays for two
opt-in settings, to buy back a shape that is worse in two other ways.

Because the shape is one window per float, `hasShadow = true` gives macOS's **own** window shadow around
the float's silhouette. Photographed against a uniform field beside `Reconstruction`'s synthesized one
(0.35 / 18 / −8), the synthesized shadow is visibly broader and softer; the system one is the article. A
per-display panel would have had to synthesize, since its shadow would be cast from the panel's rect.

And it settles Spaces declaratively. A panel carrying neither `.canJoinAllSpaces` nor
`.fullScreenAuxiliary` belongs to the Space it was ordered in on, so it cannot appear over another app's
full-screen one. Nothing observes `activeSpaceDidChangeNotification`, nothing reads `AXFullScreen` on a
focus change, and there is no signal to get wrong.

**Filming is the shell's, and nothing gates on it.** `Effect.setHoists` carries ids and rects; where the
pixels come from is `HoistPanels`, which diffs the set and asks `SurfaceFilmer` — a new protocol beside
`CaptureStore` rather than a method on it, because nothing about a hoist is a cover's: no base, no
session to open or grow, no ack the reducer counts down, and no entitlement in `SurfaceCache`, since the
panel's own layer holds the `CGImage`. The panel goes up empty and becomes visible when the photograph
lands, so a machine with no Screen Recording grant simply never sees one — the cover ladder's degradation
ending in the same geometry.

**A photograph is taken at burial and not again.** That instant is the freshest one available: the pixels
are what the user was just looking at. Both alternatives cost more than they buy — a timer puts a
screenshot through the window server every few seconds for a window nobody is looking at, and an
`SCStream` mirror (which would genuinely work; SCK streams an occluded window) holds the screen-recording
indicator lit for as long as the window floats. A float that comes forward and goes back is filmed again,
because it left the hoisted set and re-entered it.

**The click hands off by itself.** `Event.hoistClicked` reduces to `focus` + `raise`; the hoist stays up
until the focus report comes back and `settleHoists` finds the window no longer covered. A timer would
have had to guess at that.

## Observations

**A hoisted float's content is frozen**, and that is the honest cost of 4.4 above: a floating terminal,
video or chat window shows the frame it was buried at until it comes forward. Live mirroring is the only
true answer and its price is a permanent recording indicator.

**A hoisted float cannot be dragged, resized, scrolled or hovered.** The occlusion rule confines this to
windows where none of it worked anyway, but the first click on a buried float is now always a raise
rather than whatever the app would have done with it.

**Floats still have no workspace.** `writeTruthPlane` walks `strip.allWindowIds`, so a float is never
parked and is on screen on all 36 addresses; `moveToWorkspace` guards on `columnIndex(ofWindow:)` and
declines a float outright. Hoisting makes this louder rather than causing it — a float is now pinned on
top of every workspace you visit. Sticky is the deliberate first pass; parking floats per workspace is a
change of its own, and it drags the quit cascade with it, which excludes floats today on the grounds that
emira never placed them.

**Hoists are not in the guides.** A hoisted float is unmissable on screen and absent from the minimap,
which is a hole in "the strip's own projection at another scale" — but it is not on the strip and cannot
be focused from one, so putting it there would be the guide answering a different question.

**A partly-visible float doubles its shadow at the edge**, where the real window's own shadow shows
beside ours. Small, and only in the band the occluder does not cover.

**Validated by the suite and by two probes, not against the daemon.** The two AppKit facts the design
turns on — click passthrough and `hasShadow` — were measured with standalone binaries posting synthetic
clicks and capturing the result, because both are load-bearing and neither is documented in a form worth
trusting. The end-to-end path was not run against the installed daemon: that means `make install` over a
running window manager, and an ad-hoc signature's cdhash changes with every build, so macOS re-asks for
Accessibility and the WM is down until it is re-granted. `Engine.reduce` is total over (`State`, `Event`)
and `HoistTests` drives the same events the daemon folds, so the gap is `HoistPanel` and `HoistPanels`
only. Worth a confirming pass on the next install — in particular that a click on a hoist lands on the
right window when two overlap, and that the hand-off has no visible flash.

**`SurfaceFilmer` writes into `SurfaceCache` unpinned.** A hoist's photograph is therefore available to a
later cover as an `.immediate` stand-in that nothing paid for, and the store may reduce or collect it
freely — the panel's layer is what keeps it on the screen.
