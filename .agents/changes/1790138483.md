# The pin fence asks about an order

> Change `1790138483` — 2026-09-22

## Goal

With a pin up and a quick shuffle along the strip — five windows, a right pin, `focus` left and right a
handful of times — a column that rests partly behind the pin sometimes ends up drawn over it, and stays.
The sequence the gate exists to produce (pin's app activated, then the target's, leaving overhanging <
pin < target) was being issued in the right order and not landing in it.

## Implementation

### What was measured

Reproduced live, on five windows across four apps with Finder pinned right and 50% columns, so the
column beside col 0 rests across the band. Six presses 60–80 ms apart, ending on col 0, then a
`CGWindowList` read of what sits above the pin in its band: 2 of 33 rounds left Safari over Finder.
Traced with the reducer log plus writer and fence timestamps and a window-server stack sample after each
activation; the failing press, in order:

- The previous press's owed focus activates Safari. Safari's window does not actually rise for ~50 ms.
- The new press asks about the pin. `PinFence` reads *nothing foreign over the band* and reports — Safari
  is at x=20 on its way to 865, not in the band yet. It is already above Finder.
- Finder is activated 15 ms after the fence has reported; the owed focus (Ghostty) goes out 17 ms after
  that. Finder never rises: its raise had not reached the glass when another app was activated, and it is
  simply lost. Final order Ghostty > Safari > Finder, Safari across the band.

Across that whole run the fence was asked 204 times and answered *clear* on its first read every time. It
was not a fence: the window it exists to protect is, in the common case, outside the band at the moment
it is asked — `guardPins` names it precisely because it is *about* to cross — and the read was taken
beside the pin's activation rather than after it, so it described the desktop before anything had been
asked of it.

`FocusIntent` supersession, the first suspect, is not the mechanism: every activation in the failing
sequence was made, in the intended order. What reorders them is that `activate()` returning is not the
raise; the raise is the app's own, tens of milliseconds later, and a second activation inside that window
takes it away.

### The fix

**The question is an order.** `Effect.confirmFocus` carries the at-risk windows, and `StackProbe` answers
*covered* if any of them is above the pin wherever it stands, as well as anything foreign in the band
(the old question, kept: an unmanaged window over the band is still something the cover would not hide).
`Transition.atRisk` records per pin what `guardPins` found, accumulated for the session and never
drained — a window that has landed in the band is exactly one the pin must stay above. Per pin rather
than the existing `clearance` union, because a pin need not be above what crosses the *other* pin's band,
and `clearance` drains on landing.

**It is asked from the activation on.** `PinFence.confirm` registers the request and arms the grace;
`activated(_:)`, called from the writer's completion, starts the reads. A read before it can only say how
the desktop stood before the pin was asked for. The grace still counts from the request, so an activation
that is superseded or refused — neither of which completes — is waited out rather than awaited forever.
The grace deadline moved from a `Date` comparison onto the injected scheduler, which is what made the
fence testable at all; it had no tests.

**The grace is half of `hold-timeout`**, not a fixed 0.4 s. The pin's activation waits behind `makeKey`,
three AX messages on the pin app's lane at up to 0.25 s each, so a fixed 0.4 s could expire before a
slow app had been activated at all — and did, once, at ~440 ms. Both deadlines arm as a session opens or
retargets, so one is a fair budget cut from the other; the half left over is for the teleport and its
landings, because a session reaching `hold-timeout` closes over reals still in flight, which is worse
than a pin briefly under a window. 75% was considered: it helps only stalls between 0.5 and 0.75 s, and
leaves 250 ms to land, which one timed-out AX set on a slow app spends on its own.

**A re-ask replaces rather than short-circuits.** `PinFence` holds one request per pin, the newest, with a
generation as `HoistPanels` does. A second request used to be answered on the spot (the first
Observation in `1788593843`), which opens the gate unverified; with reads now waiting on an activation,
requests live long enough for that to be reachable. The older request's report is dropped, not
delivered: `focusConfirmed` names only the window, so it would answer the newer question.

**A window swept in mid-ask has the pin asked again.** `requirePin` used to ignore a pin already on the
wire. Its answer covers only the windows it named, so if a retarget adds one, the pin is re-queued and
asked once the first answer lands.

Rejected: probing for "the pin is the frontmost ordinary window". It is the strongest evidence the
activation landed, but a window of another app above it on another display, or any unrelated activation
during the gate, holds it to the grace every time. The at-risk set is the actual requirement.

Rejected: lengthening or ordering around `FocusIntent`'s supersession. The pin's activation was never
dropped in the failing case; delaying the owed focus by a fixed interval would be a guess at an app's
latency where the window server can simply be asked.

### After

Same desktop and sequence: 0 of 70 rounds. The fence now reads *covered* and waits on 204 of its reads
across 190 asks. Request to report: p50 24 ms, p90 74 ms, max 92 ms (was 2–7 ms), overlapping the capture
head; only the teleport of the reals behind the cover waits on it.

## Observations

**The grace still lets a slow enough pin app through.** One round in 40 of random left/right sequences,
under the fixed 0.4 s grace, failed the same way: Finder sat on the `makeKey` for ~440 ms with nothing of
ours on its lane, the grace expired with the activation unmade, and the owed focus superseded it. Half of
the default `hold-timeout` covers that stall; one past 0.5 s still goes through, as a delay and not a
veto. Where inside `makeKey` the time goes was not measured — if it is the best-effort `AXFocused` set,
the fence's write could skip it.

**A session can close with a pin ask on the wire.** `isReadyToClose` does not read `isPinCleared`, so a
re-ask provoked late in a session (seen once, after a `.system` focus report naming the pin retargeted
it) is outlived by the session, whose closing pays the debt and supersedes the ask. Harmless where
observed, since the pin was already on top; not investigated further. Why that echo arrived as `.system`
rather than `.ours` was not looked into either.

**Two windows of one app.** An at-risk window belonging to the pin's own app, or to the target's, is
ordered by the app rather than by activation; the fence waits out its grace on the first and cannot see
the second. Unchanged by this.

The probe's named-window read is not unit-tested — it is a `CGWindowList` call — and was validated only
on the live desktop above.
