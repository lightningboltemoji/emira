# A window the strip goes around

> Change `1788583572` — 2026-09-04

## Goal

A reference window — a chat, a video, a document you are typing out of — has nowhere to live on a
scrollable tiler. Floating it works until you click the work, at which point macOS buries it and hoisting
turns it into a picture you cannot use. Putting it on the strip works until you scroll. What is wanted is
neither: a window emira **places** at an edge of a display, that no workspace scrolls past and no cover
hides, and that the strip is laid out around rather than under.

`pin left|toggle-left|right|toggle-right|off` and `focus-pinned`.

## Implementation

### One area becomes three, and only two of them a pin narrows

The load-bearing statement is that **pinning moves the strip and re-proportions nothing**: `[0.5]` is
half the screen with a pin and without one, so pinning resizes no column and every app is re-asked for a
size it already has. That is one rename plus one inset. Today's `contentArea` becomes `nominalArea`; the
name `contentArea` keeps its job — *where the strip lives* — and takes a new value, the nominal area
minus each pin's band and the column gap beside it. Every existing geometry call site was already on the
side of the line it needed; only `widthExtent` moved (onto nominal) and only `fullscreen` and the
ceilings gained a second extent (`contentExtent`, onto clear). The physical pair is the same cut:
`screenArea` is the working area up to each pin's own edge, and `physicalViewport` shifts by the distance
between the two left edges rather than by `outerGaps.left`, which is the same number with nothing pinned.

**A pin band is forbidden ground, like a strut, and not empty ground crossed in motion, like an outer
gap** — the arithmetic being identical is the trap the outer-gap paragraph already names. A layer sliding
into the band would be drawn over the pin's own photograph in the captured base, which reads as a column
sliding *over* it, so the cover's layer container clips at the band and the pin band is the one place
where the layout's clip and the cover's clip are the same edge.

Rejected: resolving presets against the clear area instead. It makes `[0.5, 0.5]` keep filling the
visible strip, at the cost of resizing every column on the display the moment you pin one — a desktop of
AX writes for a command that should be a move, and a rule ("half of what is left") the user has to hold
in their head against the one the file states.

A preset can therefore resolve wider than the clear area. That is not a new state: `width-presets = [1.5]`
already produces a column wider than the viewport and `Strip.offsetToReveal` already frames one that
cannot fit. Only `fullscreen` and the explicit ceilings mean *usable*, which is exactly what a bound
should mean.

### The pin record is `World`'s, because strip membership is

`World.participatesInStrip` must answer `false` for a pin, and four things inside `World` read it —
`setFocus` (which is what writes `lastStripFocus`, and so what `focus-pinned`'s return leg depends on),
`pruneStripFocus`, `stripWindowIds`, `isOnScreen`. A pin recorded in `Monitors`, where per-display
structure otherwise lives, would make membership a join and hand all four a second container. So `pins`
sits beside `floating`, keyed by `WindowId` for its reason: it is the same *kind* of fact, the user's
answer about whether a window is on the strip at all.

The objection is `World`'s own header — *every field is refreshed by observation* — which has not been
true since `floating`, `corrections` and `placedOnScreen`. The live distinction is `Pointer`'s: a record
nothing can refresh is a wish. A pin is not a wish; it is re-asserted on every placement pass, and an app
that moves the window has it moved back.

What that costs is the `detached` memory `Monitors` would have given: a display leaving strands its pins,
and they go to the display the user is on (evicting that side if it is taken) rather than waiting for the
screen to come back. With no display attached they simply wait, because nothing is placed at all until
one arrives and `setMonitors` runs again — which is what makes a clamshell transient harmless.

**Pinning and floating are exclusive, and `World` is where the exclusion is stated** rather than argued
at each verb: both mean *off the strip*, and two records of that would be two authorities on membership.
`setPin` clears the float and `setFloating(_, true)` clears the pin. Hoisting then needs no change at
all — a hoist is only ever a float *by choice*, and a pin is never one.

### The width verbs are the pin's width control

A pin seeds its band from the column it left, both rungs of it, and carries `ColumnLayout`'s two lower
width rungs — an override shadowing a preset index — minus the fullscreen one. So `grow`, `shrink` and
`cycle-width` with a pin focused are the same arithmetic one container over, and there is no `[pin]`
table, no schema entry and no second authority on a width. The clamp is `LayoutMetrics.pinWidth`'s, once,
beside the resolution it bounds; two pins split what is left evenly rather than each taking a share of
the other's remainder, which is what stops the pair being a fixed point.

`EmiraConfig` and `EmiraSettings` change by zero lines, and the two verbs become bindable in the keys
editor for free because it reads `Vocabulary.verbs`. Both go on `Catalog.notDemonstrableVerbs` for
`float`'s reason plus one of its own: the mock has no clear area, so the strip a pin narrows is not a
thing the preview has.

**The quit cascade rescues a pin like a parked window.** `cascadeOrder` is a walk over strips and a pin is
on none, so it is appended there for `scopeUnion`'s reason — and left out, a quit would abandon it standing
in a band, which is exactly as unsurvivable as a nub in a corner and for the same reason: nothing else on
the machine knows why the window is there.

### The cover stops at the band

A pinned window sits *below* the cover, which is at `.floating`, so leaving its band uncovered is the
whole of what keeps it live. **A band off the edge and not a hole in the middle** — a pin is full height
and hard against its own edge, so what has to be uncovered is one rectangle rather than four, and that
turns the mechanism from a mask into a frame change on two layers. A mask on the layer host would cost
the whole cover an offscreen composite on every frame of every scroll; the frame change costs nothing.
The base is cropped with `contentsRect` rather than scaled (it is `.resize`), the window stops being
opaque while anything is cleared, and the strip container's `bounds.origin` tracks its `frame` so
sublayers keep addressing themselves in the window's own coordinates.

The band swallows the outer-gap margin beside the pin along with the pin itself. Nothing of ours is drawn
there and the live wallpaper under it is the wallpaper the base was photographed from, so the two are the
same pixels — and it is what makes the uncovered region one rectangle. The known cost is a parked nub: a
right pin puts the lot's corner inside the band, so a nub teleporting there is live rather than frozen
for the length of a transition. It is a ~1 pt sliver behind an opaque window and is left alone
deliberately.

**The band is held clear until `crossfadeDone`, not until the session closes**, and the difference is
the whole of a visible bug. A cover keeps its stand-ins for the length of the fade, and the column that
has just scrolled off the strip's near end sits at a natural frame reaching right across the band —
measured, on a 500 pt band: 41 of the 82 layer frames one `focus right` emits intersect it. The only
thing holding that stand-in back is the clip, so growing the cover back at `endTransition` draws the
window that just went behind the pin across it for 220 ms. `Compositor` keeps the decision too, since a
surface rebuilt under it starts flush with its display while the core still believes a band is clear.

`setCoverClearing` is a post-pass for `settleHoists`' reason — what a cover must leave alone is the
product of the pins, the scope and the phase, and no one verb owns it. It runs ahead of the two pointer
passes so it stays inside the fold's own presentation run and reaches the window server in the same
`CATransaction` as the raise beside it; a cover that reached the glass a frame before it was cut back
would be a frame drawn over the pin.

### The teleport gate

The cover leaves a band uncovered, so it hides nothing there. A real window whose *current* frame
overlaps the band is drawn over the pin from the instant the reals teleport until its app answers the AX
set — and emira may not re-level a foreign window, so focus is the whole of the lever: activating an app
lifts all of its windows above every other app's.

**The gate hangs on the teleport, not on the command.** The phase machine already says *nothing may move
until the display reports the cover is up*, so pinning adds a second fact of the same shape — *nothing
may move until the window server reports nothing foreign over the band* — beside the first, as
`Motion.mayPlace`. Both requests leave in the same head batch as the captures, so the activation round
trip overlaps the capture head instead of being serialized behind it, and the pass fires from whichever
answer lands last.

Gating the *command* instead was the first design and is strictly more machinery: it needs a deferred
`Event` in a new container, and "which deferred event wins" becomes a second retargeting policy beside
the one `Motion` already has — for a case `driveTransition` handles for free, since an interrupting
command retargets the open session and the gate is simply re-read.

**The trigger is the at-risk set, not "a pin exists".** `guardPins` asks which scoped windows sit over a
live band **or are about to** — the second half is the commoner one, and reading only the first leaves the
whole ordinary case unguarded: a column scrolling off the strip's near end is still in the clear area
when the command lands, and only reaches the band once the reals move. It is read at the *aimed* offset
rather than through `placements()`, which answers at the live one until a cover is up — which is exactly
the moment this has to decide. The answer is empty on the ordinary desktop and on every scroll past a pin
nothing reaches, which is what keeps a menu-bar flash off the path of the commonest thing a keyboard user
does. A pin the session is *drawing* is not live — its band is covered, so there is no hole to protect,
which is the same predicate `settleCoverClearing` reads.

**And the ordering the fence buys is what survives the transition, not just what hides it.** A column at
the strip's near end genuinely overhangs the band — 2 × 50% of the nominal area does not fit in the 80% a
20% pin leaves, and no scroll position removes the overlap — so which of the two is drawn on top is
decided by whichever app was activated last. The fence activates the pin's app *after* whatever last
activated the overhanging window, and the owed focus then activates the target on top of that: the order
comes out overhanging < pin < target, and the pin stands above the window that scrolled behind it. That
is why the debt is paid at the *end* rather than dropped, and it is the whole reason the sequence is three
focuses rather than one.

`Effect.confirmFocus` is the same write as `.focus` under a different promise, which is the seam
`.restoreFocus` already sits on, and the shell answers it with the probe hoisting already owns:
`StackProbe` asks precisely this question, off the main thread for precisely this reason. `PinFence`
re-asks it at about a refresh until it comes back clear, bounded by a grace that **reports anyway** on
expiry — a fence is a delay and not a veto, the contract `Overlay.publishGrace` and
`HoistPanels.releaseGrace` already keep, with `hold-timeout` as the real backstop.

One pin is asked about at a time. Two focus requests in flight supersede each other on `FocusIntent`'s
record and the second drops the first's activation, so a batch asking about both would leave one of them
un-raised; the queue drains on each `focusConfirmed`, and the teleport is edge-triggered on the gate
opening so a late duplicate answer cannot re-teleport and clear a landing wait that is still in flight.

**Focusing the target is what puts its app back above the pin**, so it goes last. `settleGatedFocus` is
the post-pass that holds it and `State.owedFocus` is where it waits — on `State` rather than on the
session, because every exit owes it and a session that timed out, was abandoned or lost its display has
no way to say so. A debt whose session has gone falls due at once, which is how "every exit pays" holds
without being repeated at four teardown sites.

### Focus around the pin

A pin is a virtual column outside the near end. `focus left` at column 0 with a left pin lands on it;
`focus right` from a left pin re-enters where the user was working, else the near end. The other three
directions from a pin go nowhere — the strip is one way and the display's own edge the other.
`focus-pinned` cycles left, right, strip, skipping the sides that are empty, which with one pin is the
plain toggle it reads as.

**Returning to the strip must not move it**, and holding that is why `stripReentry` is one expression
rather than two that agree today. A pin holds focus without holding a column, so `restingOffset` frames
nothing for it and the edit leaves the offset where it was — which, with a strip wider than what the pin
leaves, rests it *across* the band. The place the pin vacated is where `focus-pinned` hands focus back,
so the strip is framed there at the moment of pinning instead: the return is then a focus change and
nothing else. Measured on two 70% columns behind a 70% pin, the difference is the strip resting at offset
400 with 400 pt of column over the band, against offset 0 with none.

## Observations

Run against a live desktop, which is what found the cross-fade above: the fix for it is the only thing
here that a headless test could have caught and did not, and the test it now has asserts the *mechanism*
(a stand-in reaching the band) as well as the behaviour, so it cannot quietly stop testing what it was
written for. The two things still unmeasured are how long `PinFence` actually waits for an activation in
the wild, and whether the non-opaque overlay with its cropped base reads as pixel-identical to the
desktop it replaces along the band's own edge.

**A window whose app refuses to be as narrow as the clear area spills into the band and stays there.**
Standing rather than transient, so the gate does not help: it is the same class as a window refusing to
be narrower than its column, and it resolves the same way — except that here the pin pays rather than a
neighbour. A pin that refuses its *own* band width is a second case of it, and worse, since a pin is on
no strip: `handlePlacementCorrected` finds no column for it and learns nothing, so the same set is
re-issued on every placement pass. Bounded by real events rather than a busy loop, but it is a set per
event for the life of the window.

**Under `transition = off` the stacking is undefended.** The reals move in the open, so a window crossing
the band is visible over the pin for as long as its app takes. The geometry is identical, which is the
trade `off` already makes everywhere else.

**`float` on a pinned window snaps rather than animating.** The exclusion in `World` fires inside
`departFromStrip`'s `leave` closure, and the window is then on no strip, so the column guard declines the
transition and `reassertTruthPlane` re-places the display. The clear area grows with nothing travelling —
which is the same answer a config reload gives to the same kind of change, and is why it was left there.

A hoisted float overlapping a pin's band draws its picture over the live window, because the hoist plane
knows nothing about pins. Worth a look once this has been used.

The word *pin* now means two things in the layout: a window held at an edge, and a window's height fixed
to a preset (`Column`'s water-fill, `heightSelections`). Nothing was renamed — the second usage is
established and local to the vertical axis — but the new comments say "fixed to a preset" where they
would have said pinned.
