# A still is its own window, and every corner the scrim cuts is measured

> Change `1791960179` — 2026-10-13

## Goal

Two long-standing Zoom symptoms with one root. After a call, the main window's veil is cut too sharply — "12 where
it should be 17". And a buried Zoom dialog is hoisted as a tiny, stretched copy of Zoom's main window.

**ScreenCaptureKit films a window together with its child windows, and a child together with its parent.**
`includeChildWindows` defaults to `true`, and under it a desktop-independent capture of a child window is the
whole family. A capture of a parent whose child reaches past its edge is the union of both. Either way the
established screenshot API scales the result into the size asked for, with nothing in the image to say so.
Zoom attaches its modals ("Join meeting", "Update required") as children of its main window, so:

| window | `includeChildWindows` true (before) | false |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zoom "Join meeting", 400×441 on screen | radius 5.25 / 4.26 — the main window, squeezed | 17.15 |
| synthetic parent, child hanging 150 pt off its edge | 14.94 / unmeasurable | 17.15 |
| synthetic child | 6.90 / 5.86 | 17.86 (its own card) |

The squeezed child is the tiny hoist. The squeezed parent is a main window measured too sharp whenever a child
stuck out at the moment it was filmed. That mechanism is reproduced synthetically, not caught on Zoom itself.

**A window no cover films had no radius at all.** The scrim cuts every on-screen window in front of a veil, but
`SurfaceCache` holds radii only for windows a cover filmed. An unmanaged dialog, like the one Zoom puts up after a
call over its veiled main window, was cut at `Scrims.fallbackCornerRadius`, 12 pt, a constant from before
macOS 26. The dialog's real corner is the system's 17.15. Between the two arcs, a crescent of the window behind
showed through the veil at each corner. Reproduced on a live zoom.us/test call.

## Implementation

**Window stills move to the macOS 26 screenshot API at its own size** (`surfaceShot`). Left unsized,
`SCScreenshotManager.captureScreenshot` answers the surface's true size: 2628×1420 px for a 400×441 Zoom dialog,
exactly frame × 2 for every ordinary window on screen. `isSurface` turns that into a detector: both axes must
match the frame at the filter's `pointPixelScale` within `surfaceSlack` (2%). That is loose enough for a window
filmed while it opens (final 800×600 px against a still-animating 398×298 frame) and tight against a family
(×2.3 by ×2.0, or ×6.57 by ×3.22). `pointPixelScale` was checked against the surface at 1× and 2× (a display
switched to a 1× mode for the probe): it is the scale the window is drawn at. It replaces inferring the scale
by rounding the width ratio, which reads a family exactly twice its child each way as the child at 4×.

A still that fails is taken again with `includeChildWindows = false`, and **the retake is kept only if it fits**
(`keptStill`). A frame read before a resize fits neither take (reproduced: a childless window resized between the
content read and its film came back 1360×864 against 600×432 both times), and its children were never the
trouble, so it keeps the first still, sheets and all. A sheet inside its window's frame doesn't change the size,
so it still rides in the stand-in, which is what the scrim's sheet rule relies on.

Colour space, pixel format and pixels are identical between the two APIs (checked on three windows). The radius
is now measured at the still's own scale rather than the capturer's, and a still that is not its window's frame
records no radius rather than a wrong one.

**The retake is paid once per window, not per cover** (`SCKCapturer.alone`). The first cover that films a family
window pays a second capture; from then on it is filmed alone in one. Only a retake that fit records that
(`SurfaceStill.needsAlone`): a window caught mid-resize would otherwise lose its sheets for the session. Measured
on this machine, where `origin/main` already takes 165–265 ms for a four-window batch against the 250 ms
deadline: the first batch holding a family window went from 246 ms (raised) to 262 ms (deadline, cover not
raised). The next batch was 159 ms against 165 ms before. Batches without a family window measured the same as
before (80–124 ms vs 81–138 ms).

**`CornerSurvey` measures every window the veil cut outlined at a guess**, once each, keyed by window number.
`Scrims.veils` reports them: a veiled window, or one taken out of a veiled window, drawn at
`fallbackCornerRadius`. A window no veil touches and a sheet veiled with its window outline nothing, so they are
never filmed for it; on a scrolling desktop that is most of the display's windows, parked slivers included, and
each would cost a full-size capture. An unmanaged window has no `WindowId`, and a managed one ordered out and
back in keeps its number while AX mints a new id. It films the window alone with one capture. It only starts one
while none of emira's captures is out (`CaptureService.isIdle`: no batch owed, no cover open, no `film`; and no
`DesktopCapturer` film): busy, it looks again after `retryInterval`, or when a cover comes down. Anything that
starts while a survey film is out waits behind it, a content read and one still: 64–67 ms measured through the
daemon for a 300×200 dialog, cold read included. A film that could not say is retried after a pause, up to
`attemptLimit`, because the first trace caught exactly that: the survey filmed the dialog mid-open and filed it
unreadable for good. Answers are dropped once their number leaves the screen.

`Scrims` asks for a radius by `(WindowId?, CGWindowID)` and names what it outlined at a guess on every cut
(`surveyCorners`), with every number the server reads as the on-screen set, departed and tarp-concealed
included: those are still on the glass, and their numbers still name them, so their answers stand.
`cornersMeasured` cuts again when one lands.

Verified live, A/B against `origin/main` with the same scripted scenario: a Zoom-shaped unmanaged dialog ordered
in front of a veiled window. The pixel 4 pt in on each corner's diagonal went from the window behind showing
through (223–246) to the veil's own value (120–157). Through the daemon, the synthetic parent and its adopted
child each came back from one retake at their own size and radius (17.15, 17.86), and every other window in
one capture. With the survey narrowed, four unmanaged rounded dialogs on a two-window desktop cost one film:
the frontmost, over the veiled window (17.00). One over the focused window and two standing wholly on the
veiled one as sheets were left alone. A parent with a child past its edge was retaken alone once, then filmed
in one capture at its own size.

**Rejected: `SCContentFilter.contentRect` as a detector before the first capture.** It reports the window's own
frame whether or not a child hangs past it, so only the still's size can tell. **Rejected: one content read
shared across a survey's queue.** A warm read is 11–17 ms against 23–26 ms for a dialog's still, but the narrowed
queue is almost always one window, and a shared read goes stale for the next. **Rejected: the median of all
measured radii as a fallback** (staged earlier, never committed). It is a guess that depends on which windows a
session happens to have filmed. **Rejected: a black/white difference matte of the composited window.** A single
display-scoped capture keeps transparency anyway, and what looked like Zoom painting its own corners was its
parent in the capture.

## Observations

- **The main-window case is not caught on Zoom.** In a test call, neither Zoom's main nor its meeting window had a
  child past its edge. The mechanism produces exactly "too sharp, sometimes", but if the cause on the primary
  machine is something else (a film mid-animation, the hide/show id change), this does not touch it.
- **A child inside the window's frame that sits over a corner** still contaminates that corner's reading: the size
  check cannot see it. Nor can it see a child reaching less than `surfaceSlack` past the edge.
- **A window caught mid-resize pays a retake that cannot help**, each time it is caught, and keeps its sheets.
- **The survey waiting out a desktop film** is covered by unit tests, not caught live: the runs only showed the
  reverse, a desktop film started at a cover's dismissal behind a survey film begun during the fade.
- **The first cover to film a family window pays a capture.** On a machine already at the deadline that can drop
  that one cover. Making covers never retake (keep the squeezed still once, record no radius, film alone next time)
  is the alternative if this proves visible.
- **A window moving between a 2× and a 1× display** is now filmed at the scale it is drawn at, not the destination
  overlay's, so its stand-in can differ from the real window at the cross-fade. Not observed; one display here.
  The same change fixes the opposite case: a 1× window filmed by a 2× capturer came back padded, measuring
  its radius at half.
- **Hoists** go through the same path, so a buried child window is now hoisted as itself; verified at the capture
  level, not through a live hoist.
- `SCKCapturer.alone` is never pruned. A reused number costs its new window its sheets in a stand-in, never a
  wrong still.
- **Landed onto the tarp** (merged from a second machine). `TarpCut.cut` takes the same `(WindowId?, CGWindowID)`
  lookup, so a tarp neighbour's hole is rounded by a surveyed radius too. Only the veil cut asks for a survey
  (`surveyCorners`), so on a display with a tarp and no veil an unmeasured neighbour keeps the fallback.
