# The cover was asking the window server for permission

> Change `1788322209` — 2026-09-01

## Goal

Closing TextEdit's Open panel with ⌘W left the column standing open for a third of a second after the panel
had finished animating away. `1788317771` established that nothing on our side is slow while that lasts and
made the fence wait it out, so the animation is at least whole; this is the wait itself.

Sampled from a third process at ~2 kHz, against the built daemon on both sides of the change:

```
                    cover published on the glass, from the ⌘W keystroke
before   306.6  306.6  312.1  312.2  302.5 ms
after     98.8   98.1   99.3  105.2   96.2 ms
```

The core is never the laggard. `AXUIElementDestroyed` lands 30–40 ms after the keystroke — TextEdit's own
teardown, and within a millisecond of it `vanish` takes its fast path and `windowDestroyed` reduces. Every
remaining millisecond was the cover: 56 ms of capture, then a single `CGWindowListCopyWindowInfo` on the
fence's queue blocking for **190–223 ms**, returning `0.999` at the exact instant the closing window left the
window server's list. The reducer holds every real move and every frame of motion until `coverOnScreen`, so
that block *is* the dead time — the strip does not move until it lifts.

## Implementation

**`raise` no longer orders the window front.** That one call is the whole deferral. Four variants of the raise,
five closes each, fence duration in milliseconds:

| raise                                     | close                       |
| ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| animator alpha + `orderFrontRegardless`   | 190 208 206 211             |
| direct alpha + `orderFrontRegardless`     | 223 220 214 206             |
| animator alpha, no ordering               | 3 5 2 2 2 211               |
| direct alpha, no ordering                 | 2 3 2 6 2 5                 |

The ordering is now re-asserted in `orderToFront`, called at build time and from `fadeOut`'s completion — the
cover is at alpha 0 there, so the call is invisible and nothing is waiting on it. Ten closes measured 2–5 ms
on the fence afterwards. The class comment already said a raise had to be a pure alpha flip, and the
`orderFrontRegardless` beside it had been there since the first compositor.

**What was ruled out on the way.** The zero-duration alpha animation is not it — a bare `alphaValue` assignment
defers identically, so the animator stays, and with it the reason it is there (it replaces a cross-fade still
in flight, which a direct assignment would lose to the fade's next frame). Nor is it the window's own
properties: `isOpaque = false` with a clear background measured 205–216 ms, and moving the overlay a level up
to sit where `GuidePanel` sits measured 204–215 ms. Only the ordering call moves the number.

**The deferral is ours, not the desktop's.** A third process asked Finder — over AX, so Finder commits, not
us — to move a window 30 ms into the same close animation, and the window server published the move 5 ms
later. So the server is not frozen and the display is not stalled; what is held is this connection's
ordering change, and every commit queued behind it.

**Rejected: keeping the raise's ordering and starting the transition earlier.** The capture is what the raise
waits on and it cannot start before the destroy, so there is nothing to bring forward. Rejected too: dropping
the fence now that the ordinary deferral is gone. It is what makes `coverOnScreen` a fact rather than a hope,
it costs 2–5 ms when there is nothing to wait for, and it is the only thing standing between a server that
does defer and the defect `1788317771` fixed.

## Observations

**⌘Q gains little, and did not have much to lose:** 157–182 ms before, 154–155 ms after. Quitting destroys the
window with no animation, so there was no deferral to remove — what is left is the app's own teardown and the
capture, and the tighter spread is the ordering call no longer being there to sometimes catch one.

**The capture is now the largest piece emira owns**: 56 ms of the ~99 ms, splitting about evenly between
`SCShareableContent.excludingDesktopWindows` (13–35 ms, an enumeration, re-asked every batch) and the base
screenshot itself (28–66 ms). Caching the shareable content across batches is the obvious next move and is not
free to get right — a stale `SCWindow` mis-fills the base's exclusion list, and the window it describes may
already be gone.

**The base's ghost of the closing window is unchanged and starts sooner.** The base is photographed at ~56 ms
either way, with the closing window baked in at whatever alpha its animation had reached; what moved is when
that photograph goes up. It used to appear at 308 ms, by which time the real window was at alpha 0 and the
frozen ghost replaced nothing; it now appears at 99 ms, against a real window still at roughly a third. The
artifact is the same size and the transition it sits on is 200 ms shorter, but the step at the raise is new.

**Ordering was verified rather than assumed.** 7400 samples of the front-to-back window list across five
closes: the cover was up in 857 of them and no ordinary window was ever in front of it. The window level does
most of that work — a `.floating` overlay outranks every level-0 window whatever the ordering says — so what
the re-assertion actually buys is the top of level 3 itself, which is why it is kept rather than dropped.
