# A raised cover stops a thousandth short of opaque

> Change `1785549810` — 2026-07-31

## Goal

A transition over a window playing video ended wrong. Nothing looked amiss during the motion, but the
moment the strip settled — a stable image, held for a beat, then the dissolve — the video *popped*, the
cover having read duller than the window it stood for. No other window did it. The suspicion was a
colour-space mismatch between the still and the live surface, and it was not: the still is exact.
`SCStreamConfiguration()` already returns the display's own wide-gamut profile rather than sRGB, and
cover-versus-real agrees to four decimals in mean linear RGB, luminance and saturation. Static page
chrome cross-fades at exactly 0.00% deviation at every alpha and every sample of a real animated fade.
The disagreement is confined to a separately-composited video plane, on the first frame of the dissolve.

## Implementation

`Overlay.raise()` drove the window to `alphaValue = 1`. A window at full alpha over an opaque frame
marks everything beneath it occluded, and an occluded app may stop feeding its video plane; when the
dissolve starts the plane has to come back, and the first composited frame is dark. `raisedAlpha =
0.999` disqualifies the cover from occluding anything. With the video *paused* the content is fully
static, so a correct cross-fade must be exactly flat and every deviation is the artifact: over six
dissolves each, full alpha deviated on five of six — worst per run −12.5%, −6.0%, −4.8%, −10.8%, 0.0%,
−7.8%, always on the first sample and gone within ~50 ms, the sixth being the sampler landing after it
— against +0.00% on every sample of every `0.999` run, with a static control region flat in all twelve.

Two alternatives lose. Capturing the still in HDR — `captureHDRScreenshotLocalDisplay`, the obvious
reading of "the video is HDR and the screenshot isn't" — makes it *worse*, −13% to −19% against today's
−2%, and `preferredDynamicRange = .high` recovers only about half of that. Fading the layer tree
instead of the window fades to black, the window staying opaque behind it, which is not a cross-fade at
all.

The cost is stated where the guarantee is. The overlay promises that nothing behind it shows while the
real windows teleport, and a thousandth of transparency is no longer literally zero — but it is under
one 8-bit level, and it is the smallest hole that closes this. The windows underneath also keep drawing
for the length of a transition instead of being throttled.

## Observations

The perceived pop has a second contributor this does not touch: the cover freezes a video frame and a
transition runs ~600 ms, so the reveal jumps the video forward by that much however exact the pixels
are. `Reconstruction.refreshLayer` and `CoverMode.immediate` are the existing lever if it matters.

Whether the remaining thousandth is ever visible has been judged by measurement, never by eye.

The occlusion reading is inferred from the alpha threshold behaving exactly as documented and from the
effect being confined to media windows; the occlusion state of the app underneath was never read
directly. Nothing in the fix depends on that being the right name for it.
