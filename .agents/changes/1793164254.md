# A click on a hoist brings the float forward, not the daemon

> Change `1793164254` — 2026-10-27

## Goal

Reported from IntelliJ: a prompt "will often sit at the back, even when you click the hoisted window."
Reproduced with a Swing probe on IntelliJ's own runtime: bury its dialog, click the hoist once, and the
frontmost app afterwards is **emira-daemon**, with no focus report for the dialog at all. A second click on
the same hoist works.

`HoistPanel` was a borderless `NSWindow`, and its comment claimed that declining key "keeps a click on a hoist
from activating the daemon". It does not: a click on a window of an inactive app activates that app whether
or not the window can become key. Measured with a standalone accessory app — borderless `NSWindow`: the
probe is frontmost after the click; `NSPanel` with `.nonactivatingPanel`: the front app is unchanged, and
`mouseDown` is delivered either way. So the click raced the activation it was asking for, and when the
float's own app was already active — the usual case, a float buried under its own app's frame — that app's
`activate()` was a no-op and the daemon's own activation won. The second click "worked" because the daemon
was by then already active.

Then, with real IntelliJ, a second defect: a click on the hoist raised the dialog but produced no focus
report (IntelliJ keeps the keyboard in the dialog throughout, so nothing changed for it to report), and the
core — already believing the dialog focused — wrote nothing, so the picture stood over the live dialog until
something else moved the record.

## Implementation

`clickablePanel` builds both stand-in panels, the hoist's and the peek's, as `NSPanel(.borderless,
.nonactivatingPanel)` with `hidesOnDeactivate = false` (a panel hides with its app's deactivation by default,
and the daemon is never meant to be the active app). The peek had the same activation and a worse side to
it: activating the daemon raises every window it owns, which is the reason the focus sink is a process of
its own.

`hoistClicked` is now a focus verb like every other: it writes the focus ahead of its echo (an app need not
post one), and writes the stacking half (`noteActivation`) even when focus does not move — a raise is a
raise, and an app can lift its frame over a dialog that keeps the keyboard. The hoist therefore comes down in
the core at the click, and the release fence that already existed (`StackProbe`, bounded by
`releaseGrace`) is what holds the picture until the window server shows the real window in front. The
original reason for leaving the core pessimistic — the flash between the report and the glass — predates
that fence.

`return (s, handleHoistClicked(&s, id))` captured `s` before the handler mutated it, the file's own
"local first" trap; it was harmless only while the handler wrote nothing.

## Observations

Bury-and-click loop with the probe: 5/5 at idle, 6/6 with every core pegged — the probe frontmost, the
dialog focused in the core, the panel concealed each time. With IntelliJ (three full fetch → prompt → bury →
click cycles) the real dialog came in front on the click every time, the picture fading under it as it
arrived.

What a click on a peek does once the daemon no longer activates was not exercised: one display here.
